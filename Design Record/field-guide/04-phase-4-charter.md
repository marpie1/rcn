# Phase 4 — Charter

**Purpose.** A small self-organizing team, a Solution ME, forms around each chosen scenario, writes the scenario properly, sketches the ecosystem it will need, draws what moves in that ecosystem today and what should, agrees who gets what share when residents recognize value, and charters itself with explicit promises. The Platform releases the first tranche of seed capital. Everything after this phase rests on how well this one was done.

**Conversation type.** Action. This is the first phase in which people make promises. Every promise is to someone, about something specific, by some time, and can be declined.

**Reasoning.** Design Record Sections 5 (the two scenario levels, the template, the bid), 6 (the Platform as EMC organizer; the four currencies), 7 (the speech acts of chartering; who is Customer and who is Performer), 8 (the formation sequence; institutions committing separately), 9 (sizing, gates, the VAM).

**Time.** Three to four weeks. The chartering conversation itself is one evening.

## Who forms the ME

After Phase 3, ask the room who wants to work on the chosen scenario. Expect three to six people to step forward; the scenario's own list of who would do it from Phase 3 is the starting point. This is the ME's core. One of them will hold the Customer role on the residents' behalf, the primary-ME in the Design Record's vocabulary; let the team choose that person.

People join as themselves. The school nurse joins as a person who knows what happens in the first weeks, with her own portfolio; she does not join as the school, and nothing she promises binds the school. If the school needs to do something, that is a separate commitment, made later in this phase by someone at the school authorized to make it. Say this to the team at the start, because confusing the two is the most reliable way between-institution work fails.

You, the convener, are the Platform's actor here: the EMC organizer in Haier's sense. You help the ME form, you mentor, you bring contacts and contract help, you bring the seed. You do not run the ME and you are not on it.

## Step one: write the scenario

Phase 2 produced a compound-need scenario: title, situation, needs, who counts, where, existing attempts, attribution, and the invitation. Phase 4 turns it into a vision-blueprint: the ME's proposal for how the need will be addressed. The template in the tools file has both halves; the second half is:

- **Value proposition.** What value will be created, for whom, in what form, over what time? Named specifically enough that a resident could say afterward whether it happened.
- **Value creation process.** How the work will go, from forming the ecosystem to delivering to residents, in sequence.
- **The primary-ME's proposal.** Who is leading, what they bring, what they are committing to.
- **The EMC network invited to form.** Which people, small groups, and institutions the ME needs around it.
- **Bid terms.** What participation looks like: time, capacity, accountability, and what comes back.
- **Story.** Is it compelling? Would someone reading it want in?

Writing a scenario is a creative act, not a form. The tools file points to the Story Structure model and to Marc's FedWiki pages on the customer scenario for how to write one that moves people. Separate the writing from the editing: draft it in one sitting, in the residents' language, and clean it up the next day. Have one of the residents whose life it is about read it before anyone else does.

## Step two: sketch the ecosystem

With the ME core, on the wall of the founded commons, sketch the ecosystem the scenario needs: which capacities are required, which institutions and small groups have them, which specific people bring what. This is the EIP Stage Sketch in the RCN Graph Tool, Environment, Institutions, Politics, and it can equally be sticky notes: fifteen to twenty roles at most, readable from across the room, the missing connections drawn in as questions rather than findings. Keep this sketch; Phase 5 will want the series.

Then draw what moves. On the same roles, draw a value network in Allee's notation: every arrow labeled with the deliverable that moves between two roles, solid for tangible (rides, forms, a changed intake process, money), dashed for intangible (trust, translation, what is actually going on at home), and dotted red for any return that ought to exist and does not. Read it: who is giving several things and getting nothing back (that is a burnout shape, and it is usually the CHW); where does something arrive and stop; does every path run through one person; does the money circuit close on the service circuit or somewhere else. The Graph Tool's value-network preset and its validator carry this; the tools file says how.

The red arrows are the ME's work, named. Each one becomes a specified future flow in the next step.

## Step three: agree the shares — the VAM

Before any work starts, the ME, the people and institutions it is inviting, and the Platform agree how compensation will be shared when residents recognize value. This is the Value Added Mechanism. At neighborhood scale it is a page, written by hand, and it says for each party: what they contribute, what share they receive when the scenario is settled, in which currency, fiat, time, gifts, or reputation, and within what lower and upper limits.

Work from the value network: each red arrow you are promising to make real is a future flow with a party and a share attached. The Platform's own share is on the page too, and it is the last to be paid.

Nobody has written a neighborhood VAM yet. Write one anyway, on one page, and keep it; the Design Record calls this the research that has to be done by doing.

## Step four: the institutions commit

Go to each institution the ecosystem sketch shows the ME needs, the school, the agency, the clinic, with the scenario and the sketch, and make a request of someone who can answer it: will the school change its intake to include the CHW, lend the nurse two hours a week, share what the agency sends? The answer is a promise, a counteroffer, or a decline, and any of the three is legitimate. Most institutions commit by lending a person's time without money changing hands, betting that the ME will save them waste and delay; write that down as their contribution and give them a seat in the VAM if they want one. Record every institutional commitment in the charter, separately from the promises of the people who lent.

## Step five: the gates and the seed

Before the chartering evening, you and the ME agree the gates. The Design Record proposes three, to be tested on the first ME:

1. **Launch.** The charter is complete; the first tranche is released.
2. **First recognition.** Within a stated window, residents have recognized something on their own terms: the mothers say the intake went differently; the scorecard, if there is one yet, shows it. The second tranche is released.
3. **Metabolism.** The ME can say how it continues without the Platform's money, or that its work is done. The last tranche is released or, more often, is not needed.

Missing a gate stops the seed, settles what was created so far, and discards nobody; the scenario is enriched with what was learned. Say this at chartering so it is not a surprise.

Size the seed to what the launch actually needs and no more. Under-seeding makes the ME fail for want of a few hundred dollars; over-seeding turns the seed into a budget the ME cannot come down from. A first-cycle neighborhood ME is usually small: a stipend for the navigator's extra hours, a translator, printing, a meal for the room.

Ask the ME what of its own it is putting in. Haier requires the team's own money; here it is time and reputation as often as money. It should be on the VAM page.

## Step six: the chartering evening

One evening at the founded commons. The ME core, the people and institutions committing, the residents whose scenario it is, the Platform actor, and as many of the Phase 2 room as want to witness. This is a conversation for action and it is dense with speech acts, so take them one at a time and name each as it happens.

- The primary-ME **declares** the scenario as the object being addressed, by reading its title and value proposition aloud.
- The ME **declares** its Leading Targets: what it is aiming for, specifically enough to be checked, and the window for gate two.
- Each ME member **promises** specific work, to the primary-ME, by when. Ask each one: is that a promise, or a hope? A hope is fine; it goes in a different column.
- Each ecosystem member **offers** their contribution; each institution's authorized person **promises** theirs.
- The Platform **promises** the seed in its tranches, against the gates, and **declares** its share of the VAM.
- The residents whose scenario it is are named as the ones who will say whether it went well. Say it out loud: the promises are to them. The ME's members are performers to the primary-ME; the primary-ME answers to the residents; the Platform is a customer too, but a secondary one.
- The VAM page is read and agreed.
- Anyone may **decline** anything. Protect that. A charter with a coerced promise in it fails later.

Write it all on one sheet, the charter, with names and dates. Everyone who promised signs it. Photograph it. It goes in FedWiki, and, when CfA-dSC can carry a multi-party charter, into CfA-dSC; until then the paper is the contract and the properties it must preserve are these: every promise has a person and a date, the shares are stated before the work, the seed is gated, and settlement waits on residents' recognition.

Release the first tranche that night.

## Handoff to Phase 5

Phase 4 is done when: the vision-blueprint scenario is on FedWiki; the ecosystem sketch and the value network exist; the VAM page is agreed; the institutions' commitments are recorded separately from the people's; the gates and the seed are stated; the charter is signed; and the first tranche is released.

## Worked example — Phase 4

**Honesty note.** The composition of the ME, the intake redesign, and the Leading Targets follow the worked example in Section 8 of the Design Record, which was itself constructed from Spokane #7's institutions. The VAM, the gates, the amounts, and the evening are constructed. No neighborhood has written a VAM.

Five people step forward: the CHW, the school nurse, one of the Somali mothers, the father, and a teacher who was not in the Phase 2 room and whom the nurse brings. The mother is chosen as primary-ME; she says she will do it if the CHW is beside her.

They write the scenario over two evenings. The value proposition, in the mother's words and the CHW's translation: a new child's first year goes well when the family knows one person by name from the first week, when the school knows what the agency knows before the child arrives, and when something going wrong at school reaches the family in a way they can answer. The Leading Targets: every new family this year has a named navigator by the end of enrollment week; one intake conversation with agency, school, family, and navigator present; check-ins at two weeks, six weeks, and each term; a way to raise a problem that starts with a call to the navigator, in the family's language.

The ecosystem sketch, on the garage wall: the family, the navigator, the school (nurse, teacher, front office), the agency (caseworker), the clinic, the mosque, the childcare ring, the Platform. Sixteen notes. The value network drawn on it has one red arrow going into the navigator from nowhere: everyone gets something from her and nothing comes back. The team says that is the CHW's load, exactly as the room saw in Phase 3.

The VAM page. Contributions: the CHW, eight hours a week beyond her paid hours; the nurse, two hours a week lent by the school; the mother and father, the family's side of every check-in and the writing of what "going well" means; the teacher, the classroom side. Shares on settlement: the CHW, a stipend in fiat from the seed plus first claim on any future navigator funding the district finds, plus reputation; the nurse, reputation and a portfolio entry, since the school pays her; the mother and father, gifts and reputation, and they say the gift is the year going well; the Platform, ten percent of any money that later flows and the portfolio entries of everyone. The red arrow now has a return on it.

The institutions. The school's principal, asked by the nurse and the convener together, promises the intake change and the nurse's two hours, and declines to share the agency's file without the family's written consent, which the team accepts and builds into the intake. The agency's director counteroffers: one caseworker hour per new family, not two. The clinic declines for this cycle. All three are written on the charter.

The seed: six hundred dollars in three tranches, two hundred at launch for the CHW's first month and a translator, two hundred at gate two, two hundred at gate three if needed. The team puts in its time and the mother's standing at the mosque. Gate two's window is the end of the first term.

The chartering evening runs two hours. When the teacher is asked whether her check-in promise is a promise or a hope, she says hope, because of her contract, and it goes in the hope column; the nurse takes the check-ins as her promise instead. The mother reads the value proposition in Somali and the CHW in English. Fourteen people sign. The two hundred dollars is handed over in an envelope, and the convener writes the charter into FedWiki that night.
