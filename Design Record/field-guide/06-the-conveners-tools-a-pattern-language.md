# The convener's tools — a pattern language

**First pass, 2026-09-11.** This is Marc's idea, written out: the tools and methods of the field guide as a pattern language on Alexander's model, in which each pattern completes larger patterns above it and is completed by smaller patterns below it, so that the whole forms a network of supporting and supported tools rather than a list. A convener grabs a pattern when a phase calls for it and follows its links to what it needs.

Each entry has the same parts. **Context** says which larger patterns this one helps complete. **Problem** states, in a sentence, what goes wrong without it. **Therefore** is the move. **Supported by** names the smaller patterns it needs. **Phase** says where in the guide it is used. **State** says whether a tool carries it today: built, partial, or paper. **Material** points at the intro, manual, deck, or schema that already exists in the rcn repository. **Skills** is the Basic / Intermediate / Advanced ladder, a seed for the SODOTO pages that will teach each one in FedWiki; every tool and method needs these three pages, and this is the first statement of what each level means.

The network is drawn in `../diagrams/field-guide-pattern-language.rcn.json`.

The numbering follows the cycle: the patterns of readiness first, then seeing, choosing, chartering, doing and settling, and the patterns that run through everything last.

## 1. Founded Commons Diagnostic

**Context.** Completes The Cycle (Phase 1). **Problem.** Work started on borrowed ground, a lent room, an institution's space, a convener's home, runs off when the lender's patience ends, and the neighborhood never recognized the work as its own. **Therefore.** Before inviting anyone, learn whether the neighborhood has a place its people created, recognize as theirs, use as a commons, and that no outside actor directs. Hold the eleven questions internally (origin; founder relationship now; governance in practice; access and membership; safety; cultural fit; function match; financial durability; constituency; history under stress; neighborhood recognition); ask the five short ones out loud (who decides who comes in, who has keys, who cleans up, who pays, who decides what happens); learn the hard three by being there. Decline to proceed if the answer is no, including in your own neighborhood. **Supported by.** Coffee Conversation, Moods Reading, Patience. **Phase.** 1. **State.** Paper. **Material.** Design Record Section 4 has the full eleven with what each looks for. **Skills.** Basic: can walk the five questions in a neighborhood and say whether the answers distribute. Intermediate: can hold the eleven, read the hard three from observation over weeks, and write a short honest finding. Advanced: can do it in their own founded commons through a trusted other, and can teach a new convener to see the difference between a founded commons and a friendly space.

## 2. Small Groups Filter

**Context.** Completes The Cycle (Phase 1). **Problem.** A neighborhood with no groups already getting things done has nothing for the field guide to amplify, and money poured on it runs off like rain on cement. **Therefore.** Find the small groups doing work outside business and government; follow the names; list what each does and who holds it together; notice the business owners and officials in peripheral roles (health) and any group an institution is treating as its program (capture). Proceed only if the groups exist and are findable. **Supported by.** Coffee Conversation. **Phase.** 1. **State.** Paper. **Material.** Design Record Section 4, "Beyond the diagnostic." **Skills.** Basic: can find and list the groups by following names. Intermediate: can tell a group from a program and a healthy peripheral role from capture. Advanced: can see from the list which groups will form the center of the first map.

## 3. Coffee Conversation

**Context.** Completes Founded Commons Diagnostic, Small Groups Filter, and Enlisting. **Problem.** Conveners recruit before they have found out whether there is any shared interest, and get polite yeses that evaporate. **Therefore.** Meet one or two people at a time, in the neighborhood, over coffee or its equivalent, and find out whether they see the world in enough shared terms to imagine building anything together. Ask what they are working on and what gets in the way. Do not pitch, promise, or explain. Write notes the same day. **Supported by.** Moods Reading. **Phase.** 1. **State.** Paper. **Material.** Design Record Section 7, "Conversations for coffee, possibilities, and action" (Marc and Robin Asby's addition to Dunham). **Skills.** Basic: can have one without asking for anything. Intermediate: can hear the mood and the map in what is said and write it down. Advanced: can tell from a set of coffee conversations whether a neighborhood is in acceptance, and who should be in the room.

## 4. Enlisting

**Context.** Completes The Cycle (Phase 1 to 2). **Problem.** The wrong room, too big, institutional representatives sent by their bosses, nobody whose life is at the center, cannot make a map worth having. **Therefore.** Invite eight to fifteen people: the holders of the small groups, two or three residents caught between institutions, one or two institutional people who already show up in the groups' work and are trusted here, and the commons' steward. In person, one at a time, by someone they trust. Record who declined and why. **Supported by.** Coffee Conversation, Small Groups Filter. **Phase.** 1. **State.** Paper. **Skills.** Basic: can make the list and the invitations. Intermediate: can choose the institutional people by trust rather than title. Advanced: can read a decline as information.

## 5. e-VSM Survey, Before and After

**Context.** Completes Enlisting (baseline), Retrospective (comparison), and Neighborhood Scorecard (evidence); also Scenario Validation. **Problem.** A room's picture of the neighborhood is set by its loudest voice, and nobody can say afterward whether an ME changed the neighborhood's connective tissue or only hit its own targets. **Therefore.** Survey the neighborhood on the eleven spheres and thirty-three paired homeostats before the first meeting, with the neighborhood's own words for the spheres and respondents' self-chosen worlds; run the aggregator's synthesis; survey again on the same pairs after the ME's work and compare. **Supported by.** Scenario Repository (for the scenario-specific items), Neighborhood Scorecard (Rasch, when there are enough respondents). **Phase.** 1, 4 (validation), 5. **State.** Built: evsm-svg-v3.html, evsm-aggregator.html, evsm-report.html, evsm_excel_tool.html. Rasch calibration of results and the environment interface are not built. **Material.** docs/evsm-intro.html, evsm-workflow.html, evsm-architecture.html, evsm-manual-survey.html, evsm-manual-aggregator.html, evsm-manual-report.html, evsm-manual-excel.html; Design Record Section 3's shared reference. **Skills.** Basic: can answer the survey and read their own report. Intermediate: can set up a survey with local labels and worlds, snapshot it, collect responses, and run the aggregator with synthesis. Advanced: can design scenario-specific items, run compare mode across worlds and across time, read the homeostatic-pairs report, and judge when Rasch calibration is warranted.

## 6. WHY-first Opening

**Context.** Completes Linkage Map and Idealized Design (the meeting). **Problem.** A room that starts on the problem never finds out why it is together, and the map it makes is nobody's. **Therefore.** Go around once: why are you here, from where you stand. Write each answer on the wall in their words. Then WHERE, WHEN, FOR WHOM, briefly. Leave them up. These are declarations; hold space for declaring rather than for asserting a right answer. **Supported by.** Moods Reading, Speech Acts. **Phase.** 2. **State.** Paper. **Material.** Design Record Section 7, "Phase 2's WHY sequence"; Marc's 2014 Idealized Design deck (WHY / FOR WHOM & WHERE / HOW / WHAT). **Skills.** Basic: can run the round without shortening it. Intermediate: can hear resignation in a WHY and not argue. Advanced: can bring the WHYs back at the right moment in a later phase.

## 7. Linkage Map

**Context.** Completes The Cycle (Phase 2) and Customer Scenario (the gap). **Problem.** Nobody in a neighborhood can see how its institutions and groups actually work together around a resident's life, so the failures between them are invisible and blamed on people. **Therefore.** With a resident's situation at the center, put up the parts and draw the flows, clients, information, materials, money, each arrow labeled with what moves and the missing ones dotted, from three perspectives: mainstay (the client's), support (the staff's), driver (the stakeholders'). Fifteen to twenty parts. Start from pain points between institutions, or from one client's end-to-end needs. Keep it on the wall; keep it in the Graph Tool. **Supported by.** WHY-first Opening, Moods Reading, RCN Graph Tool. **Phase.** 2. **State.** Built (Graph Tool, basic mode with a legend preset); no linkage-map preset yet. **Material.** tools/graph-tool-intro.html, graph-tool-manual.html, tools/rcn-graph-tool-intro.pptx, tools/schemas/graph-tool-v22.md; Marc's Logic of VSM Linkage Maps deck (Design Record/sources/); the thirty Spokane maps; Design Record Sections 3 and 8. **Skills.** Basic: can put up parts and labeled arrows on a wall with a room. Intermediate: can hold the three levels, keep the map small enough to read, and enter it in the Graph Tool with a legend. Advanced: can read the between-institution form off the map, see where a scenario lives in it, and compare a map across cycles.

## 8. Idealized Design

**Context.** Completes The Cycle (Phase 2) and Customer Scenario. **Problem.** Without a designed picture of what is wanted, a neighborhood only reacts to what is coming, and the gap that scenarios live in never appears. **Therefore.** Tell the room the system on the wall was destroyed last night; design the one it would want right now, subject only to being technologically feasible and able to survive in the world as it is. Use the 2014 building blocks as prompts: design concepts, requirements (life stages, barriers, communication, place, time, transportation, the professional-client relationship), humanizing principles, components (WHY, FOR WHOM and WHERE, WHAT, HOW). Add the SWOT-type scan of what is coming. Stand the wanted design beside the current map. **Supported by.** Linkage Map, WHY-first Opening, Moods Reading (ambition after acceptance). **Phase.** 2. **State.** Paper. **Material.** Marc's Logic of VSM Linkage Maps Idealized Design deck; Ackoff's Bell Labs lecture (Design Record/sources/); Design Record Sections 3 and 8. **Skills.** Basic: can state the two constraints and hold the room to them. Intermediate: can use the building blocks as prompts without lecturing them, and run the scan. Advanced: can keep a room in ambition without letting it become a wish list, and see the scenarios in the gap.

## 9. Customer Scenario

**Context.** Completes The Cycle (Phases 2 and 4), Charter, and Scenario Repository. **Problem.** Programs come and go while the needs they addressed persist unnamed, because nothing durable holds the need apart from whoever is working on it. **Therefore.** Write the need as a scenario in the residents' own language, at two levels: the interlocking-needs scenario (title, situation end to end, interlocking needs, who counts as user and neighbor, where it is real, existing attempts, attribution, the invitation) in Phase 2, and the vision-blueprint (value proposition, value creation process, the primary-ME's proposal, the EMC network invited, bid terms, story) in Phase 4. Write it as a story someone would want to join. Fork one from another neighborhood when one fits. **Supported by.** Story Structure, Scenario Repository, e-VSM (validation). **Phase.** 2, 4. **State.** Built (FedWiki template pages); the playbook for writing one is not. **Material.** Marc's FedWiki pages "Customer Scenario Template" and "Writing a Customer Scenario" (Design Record/sources/pastes/); Design Record Section 5 (the fourteen elements, the bid mechanic). **Skills.** Basic: can fill the interlocking-needs half from a Phase 2 meeting. Intermediate: can write a vision-blueprint that a reader would want to bid on, and fork one. Advanced: can coach an Experience-ME to write from zero-distance work, and validate a scenario with an e-VSM survey.

## 10. Story Structure

**Context.** Completes Customer Scenario. **Problem.** A scenario written as a form moves nobody. **Therefore.** Use Marc's twenty-six-node Story Structure model as a checklist for what a well-formed scenario could hold and a diagnostic for what a thin one is missing; do not memorize it, reach for it. **Supported by.** RCN Graph Tool (the model is a graph). **Phase.** 2, 4. **State.** Built as a graph and a FedWiki page. **Material.** Design Record/sources/pastes/turn-140-0-fedwiki-story-structure.json, Simple-Story-Structure-Plus-Organizing-Path.svg and .json; Design Record Section 5 and substrate-notes.md. **Skills.** Basic: knows the model exists and can find it. Intermediate: can use its categories to see what a scenario is missing. Advanced: can teach scenario writing with it.

## 11. Weighted Selection Matrix

**Context.** Completes The Cycle (Phase 3). **Problem.** Selection collapses to the loudest voice or the elders' pick. **Therefore.** Weight the room's principles first, each person alone; score every scenario against every principle, each person alone, plus catalyzable, right scale, and who would do it; combine; meet only to adjudicate the outliers; then declare the choice together. Split the cycle's seed across small, medium, and large in advance. **Supported by.** Idealized Design (the principles), Moods Reading (outliers are often moods), Speech Acts (the declaration). **Phase.** 3. **State.** Paper; a tool is on the list to build. **Material.** The 2014 CMG Linkage Mapping playbook (Confluence, community4health.atlassian.net); Design Record Sections 7 and 9. **Skills.** Basic: can run it on paper with a room. Intermediate: can adjudicate outliers as moods and information rather than as conflicts. Advanced: can build and maintain the matrix as a tool and run it across cycles.

## 12. Vester Sensitivity Model

**Context.** An alternative that completes Phase 3 and informs Idealized Design. **Problem.** A weighted selection chooses among options but does not say where the leverage in the system is. **Therefore.** For a neighborhood ready for it, build Vester's impact matrix of the map's variables, rate each direct impact, and read active, reactive, critical, and buffering variables off the cross-plot; choose scenarios that sit on active variables. **Supported by.** Linkage Map. **Phase.** 3 (optional). **State.** Built (SensiMod). **Material.** docs/vester-intro.html, vester-manual.html, vester-overview.pptx; Design Record Section 7's contrast of the two matrices. **Skills.** Basic: can rate a small matrix. Intermediate: can read the four quadrants and explain them to a room. Advanced: can run a full sensitivity model and integrate it with a linkage map.

## 13. Ecosystem Sketch — the EIP Stage

**Context.** Completes Charter and Value Network. **Problem.** An ME forms around the people who happened to be in the room rather than the capacities the scenario needs. **Therefore.** With the ME core, sketch on the wall the ecosystem the scenario needs, Environment, Institutions, Politics, fifteen to twenty roles, the missing connections drawn as questions. Keep each sketch; a series of them is the storyboard of an ME. **Supported by.** RCN Graph Tool (EIP mode), Linkage Map. **Phase.** 4, 5. **State.** Partial: EIP mode exists; the stage underlay, wall print path, and storyboard are on docs/eip-stage-todo.md. **Material.** tools/graph-tool-manual.html (EIP mode); docs/eip-stage-todo.md. **Skills.** Basic: can sketch roles on a wall. Intermediate: can use the EIP stage in the Graph Tool and keep a sketch series. Advanced: can read a storyboard of sketches as the story of an ecosystem changing.

## 14. Value Network

**Context.** Completes Charter (what moves, what should), Value Added Mechanism (the red arrows become shares), Retrospective (the second drawing), and Neighborhood Scorecard (the structure of value). **Problem.** Nobody can see who is giving and getting nothing back, where value dead-ends, or whether money and service circuits close on each other. **Therefore.** On the ecosystem's roles, draw every deliverable that moves as a labeled arrow, solid for tangible, dashed for intangible, dotted red for a return that ought to exist and does not; declare on every arrow whether it is observed or asserted; read reciprocity, dead ends, bottlenecks, circuit shape, and hop depth off the drawing. Draw it at chartering and again at retrospective. **Supported by.** RCN Graph Tool (VNA preset and validator), Ecosystem Sketch. **Phase.** 4, 5. **State.** Built. **Material.** docs/value-network-notation.md; tools/schemas/graph-tool-v22.md, "Value networks"; the six drawings in tools/vna-*.rcn.json and companions; Design Record Sections 8, 9, 10. **Skills.** Basic: can draw one on paper with labeled arrows. Intermediate: can draw it in the Graph Tool under the two rules and pass the validator. Advanced: can read the five findings off a drawing, lay two drawings over each other, and write a VAM from the red arrows.

## 15. Value Added Mechanism

**Context.** Completes Charter and Settlement. **Problem.** Everyone is paid on a job description, or nobody is paid at all, and either way the work is not aligned with what residents receive. **Therefore.** Before the work starts, agree on one page, for each party: contribution, share on settlement, currency (fiat, time, gifts, reputation), lower and upper limits. Write it from the value network's red arrows. Include the team's own stake and the Platform's share, paid last. **Supported by.** Value Network, Four Currencies, Speech Acts. **Phase.** 4, 5. **State.** Paper; the research on neighborhood VAMs is not done and CfA-dSC does not carry it. **Material.** Design Record Sections 6 (VAM explained), 8, 9 (VAM proportion); Hamel and Zanini, HBR 2018, for Haier's. **Skills.** Basic: can fill the one-page VAM for a small ME. Intermediate: can negotiate limits and currencies with several parties. Advanced: can write a VAM from a value network and settle it partially.

## 16. Charter

**Context.** Completes The Cycle (Phase 4) and Catalytic Seed with Gates. **Problem.** Vague chartering, promises that are hopes, resources that are maybes, targets that are wishes, produces broken promises later. **Therefore.** In one evening, with the residents present: declare the scenario; declare the Leading Targets; each ME member promises specific work to the primary-ME by a date; ecosystem members offer; each institution's authorized person promises separately; the Platform promises the seed in tranches against the gates and declares its VAM share; the residents are named as those the promises are to; anyone may decline. One sheet, signed, photographed, into FedWiki. **Supported by.** Speech Acts and Conversations for Action, Customer Scenario, Value Added Mechanism, Catalytic Seed with Gates, CfA-dSC. **Phase.** 4. **State.** Partial: CfA-dSC carries dyadic conversations for action; the multi-party charter is the committed enhancement. **Material.** docs/cfa-dsc-intro.html; tools/cfa-dsc-creator.html; Design Record Sections 7 and 8. **Skills.** Basic: can name each speech act as it happens in a chartering. Intermediate: can turn a hope into a promise or a declined promise without pressure, and record institutions' commitments separately. Advanced: can run a chartering with a dozen parties and enter it in CfA-dSC.

## 17. Catalytic Seed with Gates

**Context.** Completes Charter and Settlement. **Problem.** Seed money becomes a budget, the ME cannot come down from it, and the Platform is subsidizing by year three. **Therefore.** Size the seed to what the launch actually needs; release it in tranches against three gates (launch; first resident recognition within a window; metabolism); stop at a missed gate, settle what was created, discard nobody; take the team's own stake; count skills spread and scenario parts completed as return. Split the cycle's pool among project sizes in advance. **Supported by.** Value Added Mechanism, Neighborhood Scorecard (gate two), Speech Acts. **Phase.** 4, 5. **State.** Paper; proposed, untested. **Material.** Design Record Section 9; the two diagrams in Design Record/diagrams/. **Skills.** Basic: can state the three gates to an ME and mean them. Intermediate: can size a seed between under- and over-seeding and close one out. Advanced: can run a Platform's pool across a cycle with Alexander's split.

## 18. Speech Acts and Conversations for Action

**Context.** Completes Charter, Doing the Work, Retrospective, Settlement, and every Platform-ME conversation. **Problem.** Coordination fails on vague speech: an "I'll try" heard as a promise, a decline never made, a satisfaction never declared. **Therefore.** Know the five foundational acts (assertion, declaration, request, offer, promise) and the four-phase conversation for action (request; promise, decline, counteroffer, or commit-to-commit; declare complete, cancel, or revoke; declare satisfied or dissatisfied). Name the act when naming helps. Walk the ME's promises weekly. Protect the right to decline and to revoke out loud. **Supported by.** Moods Reading, CfA-dSC. **Phase.** All, load-bearing in 4 and 5. **State.** Partial: CfA-dSC tracks dyadic conversations. **Material.** docs/cfa-dsc-intro.html; Marc's nine-state diagram (Design Record/sources/pastes/turn-154-1-dunham-conversation-for-action.svg) and his FedWiki "Action Conversation Template"; Design Record Section 7. **Skills.** Basic: can tell a promise from a hope and a request from a complaint. Intermediate: can hold a conversation for action through all four phases and record it. Advanced: can run a room's commitments on speech acts, as the hospital was run, and teach it.

## 19. Moods Reading

**Context.** Completes every conversation in the guide. **Problem.** A convener in ambition walks into a room in resignation and hears nothing true; a room in resentment is argued with and lost. **Therefore.** Hold six moods as a working set, three closing (resentment, past; anxiety, present; resignation, future) and three opening (acceptance, past; curiosity, present; ambition, future). Read the mood of a person or a room as information about the neighborhood's viable-system state. Acknowledge resentment; name resignation and do not argue with it; invite ambition only after acceptance has settled. Name a mood aloud only when naming would help. **Supported by.** Nothing smaller; this is the floor. **Phase.** All. **State.** Paper. **Material.** Design Record Section 7; Marc's five years with Dunham, Turkovitch, and Lucy (Section 11). **Skills.** Basic: can tell resignation from acceptance in themselves. Intermediate: can read the mood of a room and choose a move. Advanced: can shift a room's mood from closing to opening without pretending the closing mood was wrong.

## 20. Doing the Work at Zero Distance

**Context.** Completes The Cycle (Phase 5). **Problem.** An ME drifts toward what its institutions find convenient and away from the residents it is for. **Therefore.** Keep the residents whose scenario it is on the ME or a call away; walk the promises weekly; revoke out loud; watch the gate window; mentor only on request. **Supported by.** Speech Acts, Moods Reading, Catalytic Seed with Gates. **Phase.** 5. **State.** Paper. **Material.** Design Record Section 8 (execution). **Skills.** Basic: can walk a promise list. Intermediate: can keep an ME at zero distance under institutional pressure. Advanced: can mentor an ME through a missed gate without taking it over.

## 21. Retrospective

**Context.** Completes The Cycle (Phase 5), Settlement, and Scenario Repository (enrichment). **Problem.** A retrospective in resignation or in celebration learns nothing, and numbers read before residents speak replace recognition with measurement. **Therefore.** Survey again first; lay the sketches and value networks side by side; then, in order: what happened (assertions); what residents received (they speak first, at length); what the numbers say; what was learned (declarations); what next. Write it into the scenario's existing attempts the same week. **Supported by.** e-VSM Survey, Value Network, Ecosystem Sketch, Neighborhood Scorecard, Timeline, Moods Reading, Speech Acts. **Phase.** 5. **State.** Paper, with the tools it uses built. **Material.** Design Record Sections 7, 8, 10; the 2014 assessment-of-situation template (mood matrix past/present/future) in the Medford material. **Skills.** Basic: can run the five steps in order. Intermediate: can hold the room in acceptance and keep numbers after words. Advanced: can read the before-and-after e-VSM and sketches with a room and turn them into next promises.

## 22. Neighborhood Scorecard with Rasch

**Context.** Completes Retrospective, Settlement, and Catalytic Seed with Gates (gate two). **Problem.** Value gets measured by outsiders' frameworks, or by summed Likert scores that are not measures, and the neighborhood's own recognition is replaced. **Therefore.** Let the neighborhood write the questions that reveal what matters to it; gather thirty to two hundred responses; calibrate with Rasch to discover which questions make which yardsticks, how far each reaches, and with what precision; keep the residents' declaration above the numbers. Use the Civic Activation Measure as the precedent and, when its result would have a use, as an instrument. **Supported by.** e-VSM Survey, Value Network, RCN Rasch tool, Recognition. **Phase.** 5. **State.** Partial: tools/rcn-rasch.html exists; the CAM pool is drafted and unfielded; Rasch on e-VSM is a committed project. **Material.** tools/rcn-rasch.html and tools/schemas/rcn-rasch.md; Design Record/sources/CAM-Item-Pool-v0.1.md; docs/wright/; Design Record Section 10. **Skills.** Basic: can help a neighborhood write revealing questions and field them. Intermediate: can run a calibration and read the item map, the gaps, and whether the stick reaches both ends. Advanced: can build a neighborhood scorecard dimension from scratch, compare across neighborhoods, and integrate with e-VSM.

## 23. Recognition and Settlement in Four Currencies

**Context.** Completes The Cycle (Phase 5) and Value Added Mechanism. **Problem.** Compensation flows on numbers, or on time served, and not on what residents recognize. **Therefore.** The residents declare, in their own words, whether they received value they recognize, and their determination is final; then the VAM pays each party's share in its currency, fiat, time, gifts, reputation, partial if recognition was partial; the seed closes out; returns go back to the pool; the Platform is paid last and only on recognition. **Supported by.** Value Added Mechanism, Neighborhood Scorecard, SODOTO Portfolio, Speech Acts. **Phase.** 5. **State.** Paper. **Material.** Design Record Sections 6 (four currencies), 8, 9, 10 (recognition). **Skills.** Basic: can run a settlement conversation with residents speaking first. Intermediate: can pay a VAM partially in four currencies and record it. Advanced: can settle several MEs across a cycle and close a Platform's pool.

## 24. SODOTO Portfolio

**Context.** Completes Settlement, Enlisting (who to invite), Charter (who bids), and Catalytic Seed with Gates (skills spread as return). **Problem.** Nobody's history of work is visible when an ME forms or a bid goes out, so trust has to be rebuilt from nothing each time. **Therefore.** After every settlement, each promiser's portfolio gains an entry: the ME, the role, what they did, the residents' attestation, whom they taught. See One, Do One, Teach One: whoever learns must teach, and the chain is the record. **Supported by.** Recognition and Settlement. **Phase.** 5, feeding 1 and 4. **State.** Partial: SODOTO carries signed teaching badges; ME participation and attestations are the committed extension. **Material.** docs/sodoto-intro.html, sodoto-manual.html, sodoto-pitch.pptx, sodoto-teaching-graph-intro.html, sodoto-teaching-graph-manual.html, sodoto-teaching-graph.pptx. **Skills.** Basic: has a portfolio and can read a teach chain. Intermediate: can issue badges and attestations after a settlement. Advanced: can design the portfolio entries an ME needs and read a neighborhood's teach chains for dead ends and bottlenecks.

## 25. Scenario Repository in FedWiki

**Context.** Completes Customer Scenario, Retrospective (enrichment), and The Cycle across neighborhoods (fork and modify). **Problem.** Scenarios live in one project's files and die with it; other neighborhoods start from zero. **Therefore.** Every scenario is a FedWiki page on the template; every retrospective is written into its existing attempts; other neighborhoods fork and modify; conveners and Platforms watch the repository. **Supported by.** FedWiki, Customer Scenario. **Phase.** All. **State.** Built (FedWiki, the template pages); field standards and aggregator queries develop through use. **Material.** docs/fedwiki-lineup.md, fedwiki-git-briefing.md; the fedwiki-page skill; Design Record Section 5. **Skills.** Basic: can read and fork a scenario page. Intermediate: can write one on the template and keep existing attempts current. Advanced: can run a neighborhood's repository and federate with others.

## 26. RCN Graph Tool

**Context.** Completes Linkage Map, Ecosystem Sketch, Value Network, Story Structure, and the diagrams of the record. **Problem.** Drawings made on a wall are lost, and drawings made in software cannot be read by a room. **Therefore.** Draw on the wall; keep it in the Graph Tool with a legend as the registry of kinds; export SVG for documents and rcngraph items for FedWiki so the drawing stays editable; validate before sharing. Icons, border colors, and edge styles carry meaning; keep vocabularies small. **Supported by.** Nothing smaller in this language. **Phase.** All. **State.** Built. **Material.** tools/graph-tool-intro.html, graph-tool-manual.html, rcn-graph-tool-intro.pptx, tools/schemas/graph-tool-v22.md, tools/rcn-icon-sheet.html, tools/validate-rcn-graph.js. **Skills.** Basic: can open a drawing, read its legend, and move things. Intermediate: can make a drawing with a legend, icons, and notes, validate it, and export it. Advanced: can use the modes (EIP, VNA preset, CLD), the composer, and the substrate loaders, and write a drawing by hand in JSON.

## 27. Timeline

**Context.** Completes Retrospective. **Problem.** The story of a scenario, the before-survey, the charter, the gates, the after-survey, is scattered across pages and cannot be read in order. **Therefore.** Put the events of one ME on the RCN Timeline tool with their intervals and relations, so the story of the scenario can be read as a story. **Supported by.** Nothing smaller. **Phase.** 5. **State.** Built. **Material.** tools/rcn-timeline.html, tools/rcn-timeline-intro.pptx, tools/schemas/rcn-timeline.md. **Skills.** Basic: can read a timeline. Intermediate: can enter an ME's events with dates and intervals. Advanced: can relate several MEs' timelines and read overlaps.

## 28. Patience, and Highlander 3.0

**Context.** Completes Founded Commons Diagnostic and Small Groups Filter when a filter fails. **Problem.** A convener forces what is missing to emerge on the granting arm's timeline, and the neighborhood learns that this too is a program. **Therefore.** Stay in relationship without demanding readiness: know the groups, do not invite them into the phases, do not offer money, watch for the place or the groups to emerge. Introduce the neighborhood's actionists to Highlander 3.0, cohorts that spend weeks in places with vibrant founded commons and learn from the places and one another. **Supported by.** Coffee Conversation. **Phase.** 1. **State.** Paper; Highlander 3.0 is an initiative in formation. **Material.** Design Record Section 4; Design Record/chat-memory/areas/highlander-3.md. **Skills.** Basic: can say "not yet, and here is what is missing" without blame. Intermediate: can stay in relationship over months without starting. Advanced: can host a Highlander cohort.

## 29. Seed and Support, Do Not Run

**Context.** Completes every Platform-ME relationship in Phases 4 and 5; this is the pattern for the convener's own role. **Problem.** The Platform actor who helped an ME form starts running it, and the neighborhood has a program again. **Therefore.** Be the EMC organizer: help the ME form, mentor on request, bring contacts and contract help, bring the seed against gates, take a VAM share paid last, and hold no authority over the work. Your compensation and reputation ride on what the ME's residents recognize. From the second cycle on, do less; hand over the phase that still depends on you. **Supported by.** Catalytic Seed with Gates, Speech Acts, Moods Reading. **Phase.** 4, 5, and the whole arc. **State.** Paper. **Material.** Design Record Sections 2 (Principle Two), 6, 8 (how the Platform persists). **Skills.** Basic: can say what is in it for them honestly when asked. Intermediate: can watch an ME struggle without taking it over. Advanced: can graduate a neighborhood and move on.

## 30. Value Stream Mapping

**Context.** Completes Doing the Work at Zero Distance and Charter, whenever an ME's promise changes a process inside an institution; completes A3 Problem Solving by naming the bursts. **Problem.** Everyone knows the process and nobody has watched it; ask five people how a referral moves and you get five sincere, incomplete answers. **Therefore.** With the institution's own staff, map the process as it actually runs, lanes for who does what, the observed times, the storm bursts where the flow breaks, in the Graph Tool's VSM mode; write every burst down, even the ones you will not work on now; hand the worst one to an A3. This is lean's value stream, a tool for inside an institution, and it is a different thing from the RenDanHeYi value chain and from Allee's value network, which the Design Record's Section 8 keeps apart. **Supported by.** RCN Graph Tool, Speech Acts (the institution's lent person leads it). **Phase.** 4 (agreeing how the process work will go), 5. **State.** Built: VSM mode in the Graph Tool, with lanes, storm bursts, the computed sawtooth, and CSV / Markdown / A3 export. **Material.** tools/rcn-lean-healthcare-intro.pptx; tools/graph-tool-manual.html (VSM mode); Medford's Phase 4, which trained project staff in it through Lean Healthcare West. **Skills.** Basic: can walk a process and draw its lanes and times. Intermediate: can map a value stream in the tool with an institution's staff and name the bursts. Advanced: can run the loop, map, burst, A3, measure, across several processes and teach it.

## 31. A3 Problem Solving

**Context.** Completes Value Stream Mapping and Doing the Work at Zero Distance; hands its measure to the Neighborhood Scorecard's run charts and to Catalytic Seed with Gates (gate two for within-institution work). **Problem.** Most A3 templates are project-management forms with Japanese labels; the problem never gets stated as a gap, the root cause becomes a person, and the improvement is proved with two numbers. **Therefore.** One page, in pencil, by the person who does the work, about one problem seen once, in Cindy Jimmerson's healthcare form: the problem as the gap between what is supposed to happen (the standard) and what actually happens (the observation); the root cause as a broken process, never a person, checked by the five whys; one measure with a baseline, a target, and a date; carried to where the work happens, argued over, marked up. Chart the measure over time in the SPC tool; look for a signal, not a before-and-after. **Supported by.** Value Stream Mapping, RCN SPC tool. **Phase.** 5. **State.** Built: tools/a3.html enforces the three rules, prints to 11×17, and takes the VSM's bursts and times in one click. **Material.** tools/a3-intro.html, tools/a3-manual.html, tools/rcn-lean-healthcare-intro.pptx; tools/rcn-spc.html with rcn-spc-intro.html and rcn-spc-manual.html for the measure; Jimmerson, *A3 Problem Solving for Healthcare* (2007). **Skills.** Basic: can write a problem as a gap with a standard and an observation. Intermediate: can complete an A3 with a process root cause and a measure, and chart it. Advanced: can coach an institution's staff through an A3 without writing it for them, and read the run chart honestly.
