# Phase 3 — Choose

**Purpose.** The room decides, together and with every voice weighing equally, which of the candidate scenarios this cycle will work on. The method is the weighted selection matrix from CMG's playbook: weight the principles first, score the scenarios against them with everyone's input gathered separately, then meet briefly to look at where people disagreed and settle. The output is one to three scenarios, declared by the group as its priorities.

**Conversation type.** This is where possibilities become the ground for action. The choice is a declaration by the group. Make the declarative moment explicit: "this is what we are declaring, together, as our priority."

**Reasoning.** Design Record Section 7 (the selection as a distributed declaration; the weighted selection matrix against Vester's impact matrix) and Section 9 (choosing what to seed; Alexander's split across project sizes).

**Time.** One week: three days for people to score on their own, one short meeting of ninety minutes.

## Why the matrix, and not a show of hands

A show of hands, or a discussion that ends when the loudest voice is satisfied, concentrates the choice in whoever is most confident in a room. The matrix distributes it. Everyone weights the principles alone; everyone scores every scenario alone; the arithmetic combines them; and the meeting looks only at the places where people saw things differently. The convener does not pick, and neither does the founder of the commons, and neither does the Platform. That is the point, and it is the one piece of the 2014 method the Design Record calls the operational answer to the danger of the elders choosing.

## Step one: weight the principles

Take the five or six principles the room named at the end of Phase 2, in its own words. Each person, alone, distributes a fixed number of points across them, say 20 points, according to how much each principle should count in choosing. Someone who cares most that "it has to still be there next year" gives it 8 and spreads the rest. Collect the sheets. Average the weights. Show the room the result and say where people differed most; usually one principle has a wide spread, and that is the first thing the meeting will talk about.

Do this on paper or in a shared spreadsheet; RCN has no tool for it yet, and this phase is on the list of tools to build.

## Step two: score the scenarios

Each person, alone, scores every candidate scenario against every principle on a simple scale, 1 to 5: how well would working on this scenario serve this principle? A scenario that would put the mothers in charge of what "going well" means scores high on "nothing about us without us." A scenario that depends on a grant the district will lose in June scores low on "it has to still be there next year."

Add three practical columns, scored the same way, because Section 9 of the Design Record says the Platform will ask them anyway:

- **Catalyzable?** Could a little seed money launch this and then step away, or would this need money forever?
- **Right scale?** Can a team from this neighborhood actually do it, or does it need the county or the city?
- **Who would do it?** Can the room name two or three people who would plausibly form around it?

Collect the sheets. For each scenario, multiply each score by the principle's weight, add them up, and rank. Prepare a one-page summary: the ranking, and, for each scenario, where the scores spread widest across people.

## Step three: the adjudication meeting

Ninety minutes, in the founded commons, everyone who scored.

Show the ranking. Do not start with it; start with the outliers. Where one person scored a scenario 5 and another scored it 1 on the same principle, ask both to say why. Most of these are not disagreements about values; they are differences of mood or of information. One person is in ambition about the scenario and another has been burned by something like it before. One person knows the district's program is ending and another does not. Surface it, let the room hear it, and let people change their scores if they want to. Re-add if they do.

Then the ranking, revised. Then the declaration. Ask the room: are these the one, two, or three scenarios we are declaring as this cycle's work? Say it as a declaration and wait for the room to say yes. Write it on the wall under the WHYs.

**How many.** One to three. A first cycle in a neighborhood should probably charter one ME, maybe two. Section 9's rule from Alexander applies across cycles: many small projects, some medium, few large, with the seed money split among the sizes in advance so that the big one does not eat everything. In the first cycle that usually means: choose the small one that can be done in weeks and shows the neighborhood the pattern, and hold the big one for the next round.

**If the room cannot agree.** Do not force it. A scenario the room is split on is a scenario that will not get a team. Choose the one the room is least split on, and carry the contested one forward to the next cycle with a note about the disagreement. That note is scenario enrichment.

**If the Platform would not seed the top choice.** The Platform actor in the room, which may be you, says so now, plainly, and why: it is not catalyzable, or it is the wrong scale. The room can still choose it and find another way, and the Platform's declining to seed is a legitimate answer. What the Platform may not do is negotiate the room's principles.

## After the meeting

Write the declaration, the ranking, and the disagreements into the FedWiki page for each chosen scenario and for each one carried forward. Tell everyone who was in the room what was chosen and what happens next: in Phase 4, a small team will form around each chosen scenario, write it properly, and charter itself, and the room will be invited to that chartering.

## Handoff to Phase 4

Phase 3 is done when: the principles are weighted and the weights are recorded; every candidate is scored by everyone who was in the room; the outliers have been talked through; one to three scenarios are declared as the cycle's priorities, in writing, on the wall and in FedWiki; and the carried-forward scenarios have their notes.

## Worked example — Phase 3

**Honesty note.** The method is CMG's, as described in the Medford Linkage Mapping playbook (weight the principles, distributed input, adjudicate outliers). The scores, the meeting, and the choice are constructed.

Eleven people weight the five principles. "Nothing about us without us" and "one person the family already trusts" take the most points; "it should cost the school less" has the widest spread, 0 to 7, because the school nurse gave it 7 and the mothers gave it 0.

The scores come in. The refugee student's first year ranks first; the clinic first-contact scenario second; the CHW's own load third; what happens when a program ends fourth, mostly on "it has to still be there next year," since nobody could see how to work on a program ending. The widest outlier is the CHW's load: the two CHWs scored it 5 across the board and everyone else scored it 2 or 3.

At the meeting the CHW says, when asked, that she is the one person the family already trusts, and that if the first-year scenario is chosen and she is the navigator, her load is the same scenario from the other side. The room hears it. Two people raise their scores on the CHW scenario; nobody changes the ranking. The convener asks whether the CHW's load should be written into the first-year scenario as one of its compound needs rather than left as a separate one, and the room says yes. The father whose daughter is at the center asks that "going well" be defined by the mothers, and that goes on the wall.

The declaration: this cycle's work is the refugee student's first school year, with the CHW's load written into it. The clinic scenario is carried forward with a note that three people wanted it now. The convener, who is the Platform actor here, says out loud that the first-year scenario looks catalyzable to her, small enough to launch with a few hundred dollars and a year of people's time, and that the program-ending scenario would not be, which is one reason it ranked last.
