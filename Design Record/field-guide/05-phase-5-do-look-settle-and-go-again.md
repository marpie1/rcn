# Phase 5 — Do, look, settle, and go again

**Purpose.** The ME does the work at zero distance to the residents it is for. The neighborhood looks at what happened on its own measures. The charter is settled: residents say whether value was recognized, the shares are paid in their currencies, the seed's gates are passed or not. The scenario goes back to the repository enriched, the portfolios gain their entries, and the ME dissolves, continues, or becomes something durable. Then the next cycle starts, with less of you in it.

**Conversation type.** Action throughout the work; the retrospective is assertions about what happened and declarations about what was learned; settlement is a declaration by the residents and then by the ME.

**Reasoning.** Design Record Sections 3 (e-VSM before and after as S3\*), 7 (conversations for action, the retrospective's mood), 8 (execution, retrospective and settlement, dissolution or reconstitution, the Platform's memory), 9 (gates, recovery, what counts as return), 10 (the scorecard, recognition as something a person does).

**Time.** Weeks to a year, depending on the scenario. The retrospective and settlement take one evening. The first cycle should choose a scenario whose gate two falls within a term, so the neighborhood sees the whole loop close once.

## Doing the work

The ME runs itself. Your job as Platform actor is to be available and to stay out of the way.

**Keep the promises visible.** The charter is a set of conversations for action, each with a customer, a performer, a condition of satisfaction, and a date. Once a week, the primary-ME walks the list: which promises are fulfilled, which are in progress, which need a counteroffer or a cancel. A promise that is quietly not going to be kept is the thing that breaks trust; a promise that is cancelled out loud, early, with a reason, keeps it. Teach the ME to say "I need to revoke that and here is why" as an ordinary move. CfA-dSC tracks these conversations one pair at a time today; until it carries the whole charter, the sheet on the garage wall does.

**Stay at zero distance.** The residents whose scenario it is are on the ME or a phone call away from it, and what they say changes what the ME does. If the work drifts toward what the institutions find convenient, the primary-ME's job is to say so.

**Watch the mood of the ME.** Resignation in week six sounds like "the school isn't really going to change." Name it and go back to the charter: the principal promised; has the promise been requested again? Ambition in week two sounds like adding three more targets; hold the ME to the ones it declared.

**Watch the gate.** Gate two has a window. Before it closes, the primary-ME asks the residents, in their own words, whether anything is different yet. If it is, the second tranche is released. If it is not, the seed stops, and the ME and the Platform sit down to say what was learned. That is not a failure of the people; it is the discipline that keeps the money catalytic, and the ME was told at chartering.

**When the work is inside an institution.** Some of an ME's promises change a process inside a school, a clinic, or an agency, and that work has its own tools, which hand off to each other. Map the process as it actually runs, not as people describe it, with Value Stream Mapping in the Graph Tool's VSM mode: lanes for who does what, the observed times, the storm bursts where the flow breaks. Ask five people how a referral moves and you get five sincere, incomplete answers; watch it instead. Then take the worst burst to an A3: one page, in pencil, by the person who does the work, with the problem as a gap between what is supposed to happen and what does, the root cause as a broken process and never a person, and one measure with a baseline, a target, and a date. Hand that measure to the SPC tool and chart it over time; two numbers before and after prove nothing, a run chart shows whether the change held. When the A3 closes, the next burst is already named on the map. The ME does this with the institution's own staff, and the institution's lent person leads it.

**Mentor on request.** When the ME asks, bring what the Platform has: a contact at the district, another neighborhood's scenario that solved a similar problem, help wording a request to an institution, a pattern you have seen before. Do not offer what was not asked for. Technical assistance dumped on a lawn is the failure this whole design exists to avoid.

**Do the small end-to-end test the record asks for.** If this is the first ME in this neighborhood, or the first anywhere under this design, it is also the test of every red box on the lifecycle diagram: the VAM on one page, the gates agreed in conversation, the settlement declared in a meeting. Keep notes on what was clumsy by hand. Those notes decide which tool gets built next.

## Looking: the retrospective

When the work reaches the completion the charter named, or when the window for gate two or three closes, hold the retrospective. One evening, the founded commons, the ME, the residents whose scenario it is, the institutions that committed, the Platform actor, and whoever from the Phase 2 room wants to come.

**Run the e-VSM survey again first**, on the same spheres and the same thirty-three pairs, with the same worlds, a week or two before the evening, and put the before and after side by side in the aggregator's compare mode. Add the handful of scenario-specific items Phase 4's targets suggest ("new families know where to turn when something goes wrong at school"). Read the synthesis before the evening; bring the comparison to it.

**Lay the two ecosystem sketches side by side**, the one from Phase 4 and one drawn now, and the two value networks. Did the red arrow get a return? Did a new one appear?

**Then the evening, in this order.**

1. **What happened.** Assertions only. The primary-ME walks the charter: each target, each promise, what was done. No interpretation yet.
2. **What residents received.** The residents speak first, and at length, in their language. This is the recognition, and it is something a person does: they say what they experienced, whether they connect it to the ME's work, and in what words. Everyone else listens. The scorecard, if the neighborhood has built one, is read after they have spoken, never before.
3. **What the numbers say.** The e-VSM comparison, the scorecard, the sketches. Where the numbers and the residents' words disagree, the words win and the numbers get a question mark.
4. **What was learned.** Declarations. Each person says one thing they now know that they did not, including the institutions.
5. **What next.** New promises, if any, for a second cycle; or the declaration that the work is done; or the recognition that the ME needs to become something durable.

**Watch the mood of the room.** A retrospective in resignation cannot declare what was learned, because the mood says learning does not matter; a retrospective in premature celebration cannot either. Your job is to hold the room in acceptance: this is what happened, and we can work with it.

Write the retrospective into the scenario's FedWiki page the same week, under existing attempts: what was tried, what worked, what did not, what the next ME should know. Whether the ME succeeded or failed, the scenario is richer now.

## Settling

Settlement follows the retrospective and follows the residents.

**The residents declare.** Did the neighborhood receive value it recognizes from this ME's work? Yes, partly, or no, in their own words, and the mothers' definition of "going well" from Phase 2 is the standard. Their determination is final. The ME does not appeal it to the Platform or to anyone.

**The VAM pays out.** Against the page agreed in Phase 4, each party's share in its currency: the fiat that was in the seed or that the work earned; the time and gifts acknowledged as given and received; the reputation, which is the residents' attestation written into each person's SODOTO portfolio. If recognition was partial, the shares are partial; if none, the settlement is small and the scenario still gains its enrichment. The Platform's share is paid last, and only if residents recognized value.

**The seed closes out.** Gate three: can the ME say how it continues without the Platform's money, or is its work done? Either answer closes the seed. If a tranche was not needed, it goes back to the pool for the next launch; if the work earned money and the VAM gives the Platform a return share, that goes back too. Skills that spread, parts of the scenario completed, and anyone the ME's members taught along the way are counted as return as well; write them on the settlement sheet.

**The portfolios gain their entries.** Each person who promised gets an entry: the ME, their role, what they did, and the residents' attestation. Today SODOTO carries this as a badge issued by hand; the extension that carries ME work properly is committed. Do not skip it because the tool is not ready; the entry on paper is what the next bid will read.

## The ME after settlement

Three honest endings.

**It dissolves.** The chartered work is done; people return to their groups and jobs with their portfolios richer. This is the normal ending and a good one.

**It charters again.** The scenario needs another cycle; the same people, or some of them, charter afresh against the enriched scenario with a new VAM and new gates. Say explicitly that this is a new charter and not the old one extended, so that the seed stays catalytic.

**It becomes something durable.** The work turned out to need a standing form: a navigator service, a cooperative, a small business, an association with its own life. That is a success. The test is that it keeps earning its place: it runs on its own metabolism, and the neighborhood's scorecard can say each year whether it should continue. What the design refuses is the fourth ending, the standing committee that persists because nobody declared it finished.

## Going again, with less of you

The cycle repeats. Phase 2's next input is the enriched scenarios, the carried-forward ones from Phase 3, and whatever new ones the neighborhood has named since. The map on the wall is updated rather than redrawn. The room is larger by the people the first ME touched.

Your aim from the second cycle on is to do less. The neighborhood can run Phase 1's conversations itself once it knows what they are for. Someone in the room can hold the Phase 2 meeting with you watching. The matrix can be run by whoever ran it last time. If the neighborhood cannot continue without you, the guide has failed its own test, catalyzability, and the honest response is to notice which phase still depends on you and hand that one over next.

**Graduation.** When the neighborhood is running the cycle, writing its own scenarios, chartering its own MEs, and settling on its own scorecard, it has become self-organizing and self-funding in the practice. You move to another neighborhood if there is one, or rest, or mentor the next conveners. Raise your kids and set them free.

## Handoff to the next cycle

Phase 5 is done when: the work reached its chartered completion or its gate; the e-VSM after-survey and the second sketches exist; the retrospective is written into the scenario page; the residents' determination is recorded; the VAM is paid in its currencies; the seed is closed out and any return is back in the pool; every promiser has a portfolio entry; and the ME has declared which of the three endings it took.

## Worked example — Phase 5

**Honesty note.** Entirely constructed. No ME has run under this design; this is what the guide expects the phase to produce, written so that a convener can see the loop close once.

The ME runs from September to January. The intake conversation happens for four new families in the first six weeks, with the agency's one hour each, the nurse, the navigator, and the family; the third one is the first time the school has heard from the agency before a child arrived. The teacher's hoped-for check-ins happen twice and then stop when her contract load rises; the nurse's promised check-ins happen at two weeks and six weeks for all four families. In week seven the primary-ME hears the district is again announcing a newcomer program, and the room's resentment surfaces in the ME's mood; the convener, asked, brings the principal's charter promise back into the room and the ME requests, rather than assumes, that the intake continue whatever the district does. The principal renews the promise.

Gate two, end of term. The primary-ME asks the four families. Three say the first weeks were different: they knew whom to call, and someone called them. One says nothing changed, because her son's problem was with the bus, which no one on the ME had thought of. The second two hundred dollars is released; the bus goes on the scenario's list of interlocking needs.

The retrospective, late January, in the garage, twenty-two people. The e-VSM comparison shows the "awareness within" to "coordination" pair moving from Disagree to Agree among the newer-arrival respondents and unchanged among longtime residents, which the room reads as the right people noticing. The second value network has a return arrow on the navigator: the school's two hours and the mother's standing at the mosque are now flowing back. The residents speak first. The mother who is primary-ME says the year went well for three of four and says what "well" was. The father says his daughter has a name to say when something is wrong. The fourth mother says the bus.

Settlement. The residents declare: yes, for three families; the bus is a new need. The VAM pays: the CHW's stipend is complete and her portfolio carries four attestations; the nurse's portfolio carries the intake redesign; the principal's promise, renewed under pressure, is written as the school's contribution; the mother's and father's entries record that they wrote the definition the neighborhood now uses. The Platform's ten percent is of nothing, because no money flowed beyond the seed, and the convener writes that down as expected. The third tranche is not needed and goes back to the pool. The skills that spread: the nurse can now run an intake with a navigator, and two of the mothers have said they will navigate for the next families.

The ME declares the second ending. It charters again in February against the enriched scenario, with the bus and the fourth family's experience written in, a new VAM, and the two mothers as navigators-in-training. The convener does not run the Phase 2 update; the mechanic does, with her in the room.
