# Section 13 — Open Questions and Ongoing RCN Grappling

## Why this section closes rather than concludes

A design record that presented itself as complete would be lying. The design this record describes is in active development, with several load-bearing questions still unresolved and several others whose answers will only emerge from the first years of operation. Naming what is open, and how open it is, is more honest than performing closure and more useful to future readers than a summary that pretends the work is done.

This section was first drafted before Sections 1 through 11 were rewritten against Marc's edits, and it has been rewritten to match them. It holds three kinds of open material, kept apart because each asks something different of a future reader.

First, questions RCN is actively grappling with: matters where thinking is underway but has not settled, and where the design record makes provisional commitments that may need revision as the grappling produces answers. These invite continued design conversation.

Second, questions that wait on the RCN foundational tools: matters where the design's operational form depends on tools, contracts, or a data schema not yet fully built, and where the transitional state runs by hand while the mature mechanism is made. These invite building. Section 9's end-to-end diagram is the standing map of this category, one ME from scenario to enriched scenario with each step colored by the state of the tool that carries it; what is amber or red on that drawing is what this category lists.

Third, questions whose answers can only come from doing: matters where thinking cannot substitute for experience, and where the first ME, the first scorecard, and the first years of a Platform's operation will teach what no amount of design conversation can predict. These invite operation and observation.

## Questions RCN is actively grappling with

### How the county-scale method translates to neighborhoods

Section 8 says plainly what the earlier draft of this record did not: the Medford and Spokane work was county-scale, among institutions with staff, budgets, and formal authority, and how far it translates to a neighborhood is unknown. A neighborhood's equivalent of a between-institution project may be how its associations coordinate or cooperate; the role of process work at that scale is not clear; and the two founded commons the record leans on point in different directions, Jerry's many new small businesses at The Fledge and Chris's gardens, home repair, and fire prevention at Leo's. Section 8 also named four forms of neighborhood value creation, associations forming, MEs dissolving, MEs becoming institutions, institutions changed by MEs, and asked whether each needs its own treatment. Marc's own shift from thinking in metasystems to thinking in RenDanHeYi and network terms is part of the same question. The record's commitment is to hold this open and let the first neighborhood MEs answer it.

### Who is promised to, and who declares satisfaction

Section 8 gives the design's answer to Marc's question about speech acts in an ME: the resident is the primary Customer, with their declaration registered through the scorecard and the e-VSM survey; the Platform is a secondary Customer; the EMC members are Performers to the primary-ME, which holds the Customer role on the residents' behalf. Marc said this is important to get right, and no charter has yet exercised it. It stays open until one has.

### The gates for catalytic seed capital

Section 9 researched how Haier stages its investment in a new ME and proposes three RCN gates against it: launch, first resident recognition, self-sustaining metabolism, with a missed gate stopping the seed and discarding no one. Marc called this critical. The gates are a proposal for the first real ME to test, and the thresholds, in four currencies rather than profit, are unwritten.

### The Value Added Mechanism in a neighborhood

Every section from 6 onward leans on the VAM, and Section 8 says the research and design work to use VAM contracts in neighborhoods is real and not done. Haier's VAMs are negotiated among businesses with revenue; a neighborhood's will settle in fiat, time, gifts, and reputation on a scorecard the neighborhood built. Section 8 proposes writing the VAM against a value network drawing of the ecosystem, so that each missing red return becomes a specified future flow with a named party and a share. Untested. The CfA-dSC enhancement session is where it gets worked.

### The founded commons and its succession

Section 4 named this as one of the two questions the record must hold open rather than pretend to answer. A founded commons is a living thing with a mortal span; new ones emerge from participation in aging ones through imitation, adaptation, mutation, and selection, which is biological rather than mechanical thinking. What favors emergence, what impedes it, and whether the pattern can be catalyzed without over-specifying it remain open. Section 4 added two things to grapple with: whether a federation of existing associations can produce a founded commons by ceding a space to the neighborhood, and what patience means, operationally, while one filter is present and the other is not. Highlander 3.0, the cohort initiative in which neighborhood actionists learn by immersion in one another's founded commons, is the generative response, and how it scales is itself open.

### The Platform's own S4 function

Section 3 and Section 6 say that the Neighborhood-Catalyzing Industry Platform exists at higher recursion than the neighborhoods it serves and needs its own S4, its own scanning of what is coming across the neighborhoods it serves and its own Idealized Design at that scale. Marc's Ripple ReThink model is available for it when a question needs it; cross-neighborhood pattern recognition and mentorship of the next conveners are two more of its functions. Beyond those, what the Platform's S4 practice looks like, its cadence, convening forms, documentation, and retrospective, is ongoing Platform work rather than field-guide work, and remains open.

### WWHA's charter and governance

Section 10's six anti-capture properties are a checklist for WWHA's charter: outsiders cannot originate the scorecard; grantmakers can only decline to fund; neighborhoods with rejected scorecards can still function on diversified funding; revisions cannot be triggered by funder pressure; recognition cannot be manufactured by item design; the neighborhood's "no" is respected. Section 9 adds three: no single funding source may control seeding; Platform actors' compensation stays outcome-dependent even when capital is guaranteed; any non-catalytic category of spending has its proportion fixed in advance and held to. Section 2 says the whole set belongs in the charter as founding limits, and that holding it will be challenging for the grantmaker, possibly impossible. WWHA's actual charter, its legal form, board, decision procedures, and the transition from founder-led to successor-led, is being drafted by Marc, Dave Chase, Gil Lund, Elizabeth Hampton, and collaborators. Marc has asked that the anti-capture properties be revisited once the field guide exists and they have met real cases. Reputation against money in Platform actors' compensation also waits on feedback from the WWHA designers.

### What RCN is, among its Platforms

Section 6 keeps RCN and its Platforms apart: Platforms are locally autonomous operating entities, WWHA in Whatcom, Leo's in Superior, The Fledge in Lansing, and RCN is the pattern language, the foundational tools, the scenario repository, and the peer and mentorship network they share. What RCN is organizationally and legally remains open: a federation with a coordinating function, a shared set of standards without organizational form, a support ME in RenDanHeYi terms that serves the Platforms with tools, a pattern language that names how the work is done without instantiating an entity, or some combination. The RCN Constitution, with its superordinate ethical metasystem and real veto, settles more of this than the tools suggest and should be read before anything is asserted. The case that will force the question is the one Section 6 reserves the word polycentric for: two Platforms with overlapping constituencies.

### The mappings in Section 3

Section 3 now carries two first passes offered for correction: the correspondence between Beer's five systems and e-VSM's eleven spheres, stated by layer with Beer's S1s placed one recursion down, and the mapping of RenDanHeYi onto the VSM, MEs as S1, Industry Platform as S3 and S4, the RenDanHeYi philosophy as S5, the VAM as Beer's negotiated link, resident satisfaction as the algedonic channel. Both are Claude's reasoning against Marc's tools and texts, and both are open until Marc has corrected them.

### Writing scenarios well

Section 5 holds the material, the two scenario levels, the fourteen-element template, the Story Structure model, and Marc's own FedWiki pages on the customer scenario. Section 8 commits to writing the school-refugee-transition scenario in full as the first real one, and to a playbook for how an Experience-ME writes one. Section 10 adds the method for validating a scenario with an e-VSM survey before an ME forms around it. None of the three exists yet; the first written scenario is the natural first test of all of them together, and of the value-network-to-VAM sequence.

### The book, and the tool kit after it

Late in the drafting of Section 8, Marc observed that this is a book, and the observation reshapes the record. Open, and recorded in the book plan rather than here: its title; whether Section 1 does the work of an introduction for a reader who has never heard of RCN; whether Section 8 splits, with the Ackoff case and the RCN Workbench as candidates for short sections of their own; whether the five-phase Linkage Mapping structure, the field guide's spine, needs a section of the record; what the second deliverable is called, the field guide of the settled vocabulary or the tool kit Marc reaches for in conversation; and the pattern-language book Marc has proposed, on Alexander's model, as the companion that shows how the tools and methods interact. A curriculum in SODOTO for the ME formation sequence and its tool skills is a fourth deliverable nobody has designed.

## Questions that wait on the RCN foundational tools

### CfA-dSC: the multi-party charter, the VAM, the gates, and settlement

CfA-dSC exists for dyadic conversations for action. Sections 7, 8, and 9 commit it to more: an ME's charter with named promises from many parties and institutions' separate commitments, the VAM in four currencies with lower and upper limits per party, the tranches and gates of seed capital, speech-act instrumentation across the ME's whole web of promises, and settlement on resident recognition. That is the second of the two committed Claude Code sessions, and Marc and Claude do the preparation for it together before it starts. Until it is built, chartering and settlement run by hand, and the design says which structural properties the manual form must preserve.

### SODOTO: portfolios that carry ME work

SODOTO carries teaching and learning credentials, signed and held by the learner. Sections 8 and 9 need it to carry ME participation, scenario authorship, resident attestations of value, and the pay-it-forward chain of who taught whom, so that a participant's history is available when bids go out and so that the spread of skills can be counted as return on seed. That is the first committed Claude Code session. Section 3 also leaves open how the recursions of e-VSM surveys are named, related, and queried in Neo4j, which is data-schema work of the same kind.

### The one graph and the RCN Workbench

Section 8 describes the RCN Workbench as five views over one Neo4j graph into which the foundational tools already write: a scenario browser, an ME chartering view, a portfolio viewer, a convener facilitation view carrying the shared background of understanding from Section 7, and a Platform dashboard. Loaders exist for drawings and value networks; the rest of the graph, and all five views, are design. The Overall Schema that the graph runs on is at v0.1 moving toward v0.2 with open decisions flagged for Kerry Turner's review. The Workbench's two standing constraints are settled: no decision routes through Claude or any single gate, and every view is a cave drawing a group can read untrained.

### The EIP Stage Sketch and the weighted-selection matrix

Section 8 makes the EIP Stage Sketch the tool for ecosystem sketching and Section 9 wants a storyboard of them as retrospective evidence; the stage underlay, the print path readable from across a room, and the storyboard are on the EIP Stage's own to-do list. Section 9 needs a weighted-selection matrix for choosing which scenarios to seed, distributing the decision across participants; the 2014 matrix exists on paper and RCN has no tool. Vester's sensitivity model is the other candidate for project selection and is also unbuilt in RCN form.

### e-VSM: measurement, the environment, and validation

Three projects Section 10 names. Integrating Rasch with e-VSM results, so that the survey yields measures and not only counts, which was Bill Mahoney's standing complaint, and which requires revising the survey's questions so that they lend themselves to measurement in neighborhoods; Marc can imagine Claude asking to run the calibration when a hundred respondents are in. The scenario-validation experiment: sixty synthetic respondents across five worlds for the school-refugee scenario, run through the real aggregator, to learn whether the four readings Section 10 describes can be made from the tool as it stands. And the environment interface that RCN's own graph of e-VSM draws, ten kinds of environment entity linked to the interface-layer spheres, which the survey does not yet assess.

### FedWiki as the scenario repository

The scenario pages exist and the customer scenario template is Marc's own. What the mature repository looks like, page structure, field standards, the aggregator queries that surface scenarios by neighborhood or pattern, cross-Platform federation configuration, and the rendering of this record's diagrams as editable rcngraph items rather than flat pictures, develops through use. Ward Cunningham's own critique of FedWiki's affordance gaps is part of this work.

## Questions whose answers only come from doing

### One ME, end to end, by hand

Section 9 commits to the test that answers more of this section than any other: run one ME from scenario to enriched scenario at small scale, as soon as possible, simulating every red box on the lifecycle diagram, a VAM on one page, a gate agreed in a conversation, a settlement declared in a meeting and written into a FedWiki page. It is the fastest way to learn which of the missing tools should be built first, whether the gates hold, whether the speech-act roles hold, and whether the value network to VAM sequence works. Everything below is easier to answer after it.

### Whether the four principles hold under sustained operation

Section 2's four principles, facilitator variety distributed rather than replaced, Industry Platform actors compensated through the value chain, catalytic seed capital, and value created by residents for residents and their neighbors, are designed to structurally resist the failure patterns that collapsed prior attempts. Whether they hold under the pressure of real funders, real institutions, real political pressure, real personnel turnover, and real economic stress cannot be determined in advance. The first years of WWHA's operation are as much learning as delivery, with willingness to revise the principles if operation reveals weaknesses the design conversation could not anticipate. Revising the principles would be a substantial redesign; revising the operational forms downstream of them is expected.

### What ME formation actually looks like at neighborhood scale

Section 8's formation sequence, visibility, interest, ecosystem sketching, value chain specification, chartering, execution, retrospective and settlement, dissolution or reconstitution, is a working hypothesis drawn from Haier scaled down, from Spokane and Medford, and from the design conversation. The pacing, the negotiations, the failure modes, and how teams self-organize or struggle will only be known from first-generation MEs in East County, Superior, Lansing, and Whatcom neighborhoods. Whether institutions actually commit by lending people, as Section 8 expects, is part of this.

### What the scorecards contain when neighborhoods build them

Section 10 commits to neighborhood-authored balanced scorecards whose dimensions are discovered from the neighborhood's own responses by Rasch calibration. What those dimensions turn out to be in Superior against Lansing against Whatcom, how much they vary, whether the variation is generative or unwieldy, and whether cross-neighborhood comparison holds are empirical. So is Marc's hypothesis, recorded in Section 10, that every successful neighborhood ME raises the Civic Activation Measure scores of the people it touched; the CAM item pool is drafted and unfielded, and can be fielded whenever the result would have a use.

### Whether the Platform's portfolio economics work

Section 6 commits Platform actors to compensation downstream of resident recognition, in four currencies, and Section 9 commits seed capital to cycling back through VAM shares, skills spread, scenario parts completed, and pay-it-forward teaching. Whether the portfolio economics across successes and failures sustain Platform actors at a level that lets them do the work, and whether reputation matters more to them than money as Marc expects, will only be known from operation. The structural properties are non-negotiable; the percentages, sizes, and timelines will be learned.

### How Platform actors hand their roles on

Section 9 says the people who hold Platform, convener, and ME-leader roles release them, usually by mentoring the younger neighborhood people who take them up, and that holding on past the release point is the same failure as year-three subsidy. Whether that happens, and whether the successors take the roles up well, will be seen as Carl, Chris, Jerry, and Brent and their peers begin it.

### Whether the record and the field guide are usable

This document and the field guide to be drafted against it are themselves under test. Whether the four conveners and those who follow them pick up the field guide and use it, whether the record's reasoning helps new participants understand what they are joining, whether the moods-and-speech-acts vocabulary earns its way in through use, whether the customer scenario as origin object actually gets written in FedWiki as the design contemplates, are all empirical. Sections that turn out confusing, prescriptive where they should be open, gestural where they should be operational, or theoretical where they should be practical will be revised. The record is a working document that gardens itself as the work matures.

## What Section 13 commits the design to

The open questions are named honestly. Future readers can engage them as active work rather than assumed answers, and RCN participants can see which questions are their grappling to do, which wait on a tool, and which wait on a first ME.

The record commits to updating as questions close. Future versions will refer back to this section and mark which questions moved from open to settled, and how; questions that turn out to be poorly posed will be revised or replaced. The book plan in index.md is where the moves are recorded between versions.

The four principles from Section 2 remain the load-bearing architecture: the current best design and the specific structural response to the failure patterns prior work documented, and not eternal truths.

Two Claude Code sessions are committed, SODOTO portfolios and the CfA-dSC enhancement, and five smaller projects: the first written scenario and its playbook, the Rasch-and-e-VSM integration, the scenario-validation experiment, the weighted-selection tool, and the end-to-end test by hand that precedes all of them.

The field guide drafted against this record is the next major deliverable, and the pattern language of tools and methods and the SODOTO curriculum are the ones after it.

The reader who has arrived at this section has read the current best form of the design as of this drafting. The design continues. The reader is invited to participate.
