# e-VSM — Advanced

A SODOTO credential. Pattern 5 in the Kit, e-VSM Survey Before and After. Issued by your NDC after three witnessed gates.

## What this credential says about you

You can use e-VSM as a measure of a neighborhood over time and not only as a picture of it once. You can write scenario-specific items for a survey so that it tests what a customer scenario claims; run the same survey before and after an ME's work and compare the two on the same thirty-three pairs, across worlds; read the homeostatic-pairs report for a neighborhood and say which couplings moved and which did not; use the results to validate a scenario before an ME forms around it; and judge when there are enough respondents, and enough at stake, to calibrate the results with Rasch, and hand them to someone who can.

## Before you start

e-VSM Intermediate. Read Section 3 of the RCN Design Record, "From VSM to e-VSM" and the shared reference, and Section 10, "The e-VSM survey layer" and "Using e-VSM to validate a scenario." Read the aggregator manual's sections on compare mode and the Full Report. Read the Kit's pattern 22, Neighborhood Scorecard with Rasch, and `tools/rcn-rasch-intro.html`, to know what calibration would add and what it needs. Two or three hours.

## Gate 1 — See One

Watch your mentor do one of the three Advanced things in real work: write scenario-specific items for a survey and field them; run a before-and-after comparison for a group whose work has been going on; or validate a written scenario against survey results. Watch how they write an item so that a respondent can disagree with it, how they choose which pairs to compare across time, and how they read the pairs report: not sphere by sphere but coupling by coupling, both directions. If it is a validation, watch them make the four readings the Design Record names, does the need appear, is it interlocking across systems, whose experience does it reflect, what does it miss, and watch what they decide about the scenario because of them.

Write your See One page: which of the three you saw, what the items or the comparison were, what your mentor read off the report, and what you now understand about the difference between a picture and a measure. Your mentor asserts showed; you assert understood.

## Gate 2 — Do One

Do one of the three, for real, with your mentor watching, and it must be one that matters to a group.

Either write scenario-specific items for a scenario a neighborhood is considering, field them with the standard survey, and make the four validation readings and a recommendation: survives, revise, or archive, and why. Or take a group that has a baseline survey and work since, run the after-survey on the same pairs and worlds, and present the comparison: which couplings moved, in which worlds, and what the free text says about why, with the honest note about what the numbers cannot say. Either way, write one paragraph on whether Rasch calibration is warranted here, and if it is, what the item pool and the respondent count would need to be, and who you would hand it to.

The mentor confirms the gate when: the items could be disagreed with, or the comparison was on the same pairs and worlds as the baseline; the reading was made coupling by coupling and across worlds; the recommendation or the presentation was honest about what the survey does and does not show; and the Rasch paragraph gives a reasoned answer either way. Write your Do One page with the items or the comparison, the readings, and the recommendation attached. The mentor asserts satisfied; you assert has done.

## Gate 3 — Teach One

Teach someone who holds e-VSM Intermediate to do one of the three for the first time, with your mentor watching you teach. Their Do One page, for a real validation or comparison, is the evidence.

Your mentor asserts satisfied; you assert has taught. The coordinator signs the badge.

## Where this leads

There is no e-VSM credential above this one. From here the work goes sideways: Neighborhood Scorecard with Rasch (pattern 22) for turning survey results into measures; Retrospective (pattern 21) and Recognition and Settlement (pattern 23), where the before-and-after comparison is used; and the two projects the Design Record names, integrating Rasch with e-VSM and the scenario-validation experiment on synthetic respondents, which need people who hold this credential to do them.
