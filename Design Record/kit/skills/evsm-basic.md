# e-VSM — Basic

A SODOTO credential. Pattern 5 in the Kit, e-VSM Survey Before and After. Issued by your NDC after three witnessed gates.

## What this credential says about you

You can answer an e-VSM survey about your own neighborhood or organization honestly and completely, and you can read your own report and say in your own words what it shows. You understand what the eleven spheres and the relationships between them are asking, well enough to explain the survey to a neighbor who has never seen one.

## Before you start

No prior credential. Read the introduction, `docs/evsm-intro.html`, and the first half of the survey manual, `docs/evsm-manual-survey.html`, as far as "Survey mode." Twenty minutes. If you want the reasoning, Section 3 of the RCN Design Record, "From VSM to e-VSM," is where it lives; you do not need it for this gate.

## Gate 1 — See One

Watch your mentor fill in an e-VSM survey about a place they belong to, for real, not one made up for you. Sit beside them. Watch what they do at each sphere: how they choose among Go, Caution, Stop, and Unsure; how they decide Agree, Disagree, or Unknown on a relationship; and, most of all, what they write in the evidence and suggestion boxes, because the words are what the aggregator's synthesis reads. Ask them, when they pause, what they are weighing. Watch them choose their worlds at the start and ask why those. Watch them export the response file and open their own report.

Afterward, write your See One page: what the survey is asking, what you saw your mentor do at two or three spheres, what surprised you, and what you now understand about why the evidence boxes matter. Your mentor reads it and asserts that they showed you; you assert that you understood.

## Gate 2 — Do One

Answer a real e-VSM survey about a place you belong to, with your mentor watching. It should be a survey that is actually being fielded, your neighborhood's before-survey in Phase 1 of the Field Guide, a group's own assessment, anything real. Choose your worlds. Go through all eleven spheres and every relationship. Write evidence in your own words wherever you have any; write "I don't know" as Unsure or Unknown where you do not, and say why in the box. Export your response and open your report.

Then read your report to your mentor and say, in a few sentences, what it shows: where you said Go and where you said Stop, which relationship you most disagreed with, and what you would want someone to do about it.

The mentor confirms the gate when: every sphere and relationship has a response; the evidence boxes hold your own observations rather than generalities; you chose worlds you actually belong to; and you can read your own report back. If the evidence boxes are thin, the mentor says so and you do it again on the next real survey. Write your Do One page: what you answered, what you noticed about answering, what you would change. The mentor asserts satisfied; you assert has done.

## Gate 3 — Teach One

Find someone newer than you who has never answered an e-VSM survey and walk them through their own See One and Do One, with your mentor watching you teach. Show them a survey being answered, yours or another real one; then sit beside them while they answer a real one, and help them without answering for them: what a sphere is asking, what belongs in an evidence box, how to choose worlds. Have them read their own report back to you.

Your student's Do One page is the evidence that you taught. Your mentor asserts satisfied with your teaching; you assert has taught. The NDC coordinator records the three gates and signs the badge into your portfolio.

## Where this leads

e-VSM Intermediate: setting up and running a survey for a neighborhood, not only answering one. The Basic credential is what every respondent in a Phase 1 or Phase 5 survey ideally has, and it is the credential the Field Guide's Phase 1 asks a convener to be able to teach.
