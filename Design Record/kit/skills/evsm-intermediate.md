# e-VSM — Intermediate

A SODOTO credential. Pattern 5 in the Kit, e-VSM Survey Before and After. Issued by your NDC after three witnessed gates.

## What this credential says about you

You can set up an e-VSM survey for a neighborhood or group: put the spheres in that place's own words, choose three or four worlds that will show where its people see things differently, snapshot the survey to one file, get it to respondents and the responses back, load them into the aggregator, run the synthesis, and read the result to the people it is about. You can do this for a Phase 1 baseline in the Field Guide and again for the Phase 5 comparison.

## Before you start

e-VSM Basic. Read the whole survey manual, `docs/evsm-manual-survey.html`, with attention to Setup mode and Snapshot; the workflow, `docs/evsm-workflow.html`; the aggregator manual, `docs/evsm-manual-aggregator.html`; and the Excel tool manual, `docs/evsm-manual-excel.html`, since preparing labels in a spreadsheet is the easier way. Read the Field Guide's Phase 1 section "The e-VSM survey, before," for the list of worlds and for what the results are for. An hour.

## Gate 1 — See One

Watch your mentor set up and run a survey for a real group, start to finish. Watch them decide the vernacular labels for the eleven spheres with someone from that group, and notice which of the default labels they kept and which they replaced, and why. Watch them choose the worlds, and ask which divergences they are hoping to be able to see. Watch the snapshot made, sent, and the responses come back as files. Watch them load the files into the aggregator, filter by a world, compare two worlds side by side, run Synthesize All, and read the homeostatic-pairs report. Watch them decide what to show the group and what not to.

Write your See One page: the choices you saw made, the reasons given for the labels and the worlds, and what the synthesis showed that the raw responses did not. Your mentor asserts showed; you assert understood.

## Gate 2 — Do One

Set up and run a survey for a real group, with your mentor watching. A neighborhood entering Phase 1, a small group that wants to see itself, an organization your NDC works with. With one or two people from the group, choose the vernacular labels and write the sphere descriptions in their words. Choose three or four worlds and be able to say which divergence each is for. Prepare the labels in the Excel tool if that is easier, import them, snapshot the survey, and get it to at least ten respondents. Collect the response files. Load them into the aggregator; filter by each world; run the synthesis. Prepare what you will show: the worlds side by side, where they agree and where they diverge, and a page of the free text in the respondents' words. Then present it to the group, or to the convener who will, as what the group has already said and not as findings that settle anything.

The mentor confirms the gate when: the labels are the group's words and the group agreed to them; the worlds were chosen for a reason you can state; at least ten responses were collected and loaded; the synthesis was run and read; and the presentation showed several views rather than one averaged one, and showed the free text. If fewer than ten responses came back, or the labels were the defaults, the mentor says so and you run another. Write your Do One page: the group, the labels and why, the worlds and why, how many responded, what the synthesis showed, what you presented and how it landed. The mentor asserts satisfied; you assert has done.

## Gate 3 — Teach One

Teach someone who holds e-VSM Basic to set up and run their first survey, with your mentor watching you teach. Sit with them while they choose labels and worlds with their group; let them make the choices and ask them why; watch them snapshot, collect, load, synthesize, and present, and help only where they are stuck. Their Do One page, for a real survey they ran, is the evidence that you taught.

Your mentor asserts satisfied; you assert has taught. The coordinator signs the badge into your portfolio.

## Where this leads

e-VSM Advanced: designing scenario-specific items, comparing across time, reading the pairs report for a neighborhood, and knowing when Rasch calibration is warranted. The Intermediate credential is what a convener needs to run Phase 1's baseline and Phase 5's comparison in the Field Guide without help.
