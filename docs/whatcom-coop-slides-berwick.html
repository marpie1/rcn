Most of the choices and actions that create and preserve individual health occur in the home.
This is not a new idea. It is the idea we have never fully organized around.
The organizing insight

Neighborhoods are the true health system.
They always have been.

The relationships, habits, knowledge, mutual support, and sense of purpose that exist at neighborhood scale — these determine the majority of health outcomes. The medical system responds to illness with skill and dedication. But it does not produce health. Neighborhoods do.

What has been missing is not the right clinic, the right technology, or the right payment model. What has been missing is giving people and their communities everything they need and want to do what they were already doing — and to do it far better.

That is what we are building.

My Shared Care Plan — the founding act

The first personal health record
untethered from the medical system

Every health record that existed before this one was owned by a clinic, a hospital, or a health system — and could only be read, written, or moved with that institution's permission. My Shared Care Plan is different at its foundation: a federated, decentralized record held by the person, not by any institution.

Interfaced — not owned

Connects to the medical system when needed

When a problem requires physician attention, the record speaks clearly to that. Every piece of evidence, every gap, the specific question being asked. The medical system receives exactly what it needs — and nothing more.

Person-owned — always

Belongs to the person, period

No institution holds it. No clinic owns it. The person carries it into every relationship — with their health partner, their physician, their care team, their family. It follows them, not the other way around.

Weed's Problem Knowledge Coupler — realized

Larry was pointing here.
We found the path he couldn't.

Larry's vision was always that the person should own their health knowledge — structured, evidence-linked, actionable, accessible to anyone who was trusted to help. My Health Picture realizes his structured problem list. My Health Choices is similar in intent to the coupler — connecting each active problem to the relevant medical knowledge, options, and evidence. Together they are the direct realization of what Larry pointed toward, with everything that blocked the original designed out.

What Larry had right

The knowledge belongs at the point of care

Problems stated at their level of certainty. Evidence linked to each diagnosis. Gaps made explicit. The person and their helper as the center of the record.

What we added

Open source · person-owned · CHW-operable

No IP. No license. No institutional owner. A community health worker can operate these tools in a person's home today — no physician required to run them.

The missing piece Larry never had

A cooperative that pays for health

When the tools live inside a system that earns more as problems are resolved, using them is how you get paid better. The incentive and the tools finally point the same direction.

The actual clinical record — diagnoses, medications, vitals, care team, visits, directives, history, lab results. Clinical ground truth. Person-owned, decentralized, no institutional owner. Every other tool draws from this.

My Health Choices

The option explorer

AI-assisted exploration of a specific problem against the medical literature. What are the options? What does the evidence say? What are the tradeoffs? Informed decision-making at home, before the appointment — not a signature after the fact.

The organizing philosophy · 人单合一

人

Person. Not a role, not a unit of capacity — a moral agent with dignity and initiative. The patient, the health partner, the physician: each a full person.
单
Dān
Order. A specific, concrete need — not "chronic disease management" but this person's open problem, today, in their home.

Active merging. The living process of person and need coming together — not yet resolved, but in genuine motion. The care relationship itself.

The person and the order are in the active process of becoming one. The neighborhood microenterprise earns when 一 is achieved — which means the health partner, the tools, and the economics all point toward the same thing: health.

Whatcom Wealth and Health Association

A neighborhood economic structure —
organized around what neighborhoods do

The cooperative is not a healthcare improvement project. It is an economic structure that makes the neighborhood health system financially sustainable and self-reinforcing. Large Whatcom employers and governments enter as self-insured members. Health partner microenterprises — self-managing teams of 5 to 15 people from the neighborhoods they serve — are paid for problems resolved and health maintained.

Entry point

Employer self-insurance

Businesses and governments who self-insure through the cooperative gain fiduciary benefits design, direct primary care, and transparent pricing. Dave Chase's Health Rosetta model — 20–40% cost reduction documented.

The care unit

Neighborhood microenterprise

5–15 health partners, self-managing, their own P&L, paid for outcomes. Physician partners contracted for specific expertise. No management above them — only the commitments they have made.

Open to all

The whole population

Starts with employers. Extends to every person in Whatcom County who chooses to join. Any member can become a health partner. The cooperative grows stronger as it grows larger.

What happened to Larry's tools — and what we did about it

Critical health infrastructure
must belong to the commons

When Larry's IP was sold, distribution was strangled and the tools died with the ownership complications. That is the cautionary case. Every tool I build is and will continue to be open source, Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 — permanently, structurally, by design. This is not a policy choice. It is the founding architecture.

Permanent commitment

Every RCN tool is open source CC 4.0. None are for sale. None ever will be. The cooperative is the financial sustainability model. The tools belong to the commons.

Open infrastructure + cooperative economics = a system that cannot be captured by any single owner. The person's record, the neighborhood's tools, the community's health — held in common.

Where things stand —
July 2026

The tools

Built and working

My Health Picture, My Shared Care Plan, My Health Choices, My Support Network, and SODOTO are open source, running, and in use. The person's record is untethered. Appointment prep, PANAS activation scoring, and AI-assisted option exploration are active today.

The cooperative

Organizing now

Whatcom Wealth and Health Association — Marc, Dave Chase, a local insurance broker, two Whatcom community business leaders — actively organizing employer self-insurance entry with large local employers and county government.

The path is clear

There have been unexpected obstacles, as you know. But the design is right, the tools are real, the cooperative structure sidesteps everything that stopped WIDS in 1995. We are not asking the system to change. We are building alongside it. Others will follow when they see what is possible.

Give people and their communities everything they need and want — the knowledge, the tools, the economic support — and watch what human beings do with their own health.
