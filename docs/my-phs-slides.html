Tool 1
My Health Picture
The health record
My Health Picture is the person's active problem view — organized by Subjective, Objective, Assessment, and Plan. It draws from the Shared Care Plan to show every active problem with three flags: how certain it is, how urgent it is, and what evidence is still missing. A PANAS activation score helps focus the session.

What you do with it

  • Check the PANAS activation score at each visit — it shows whether to focus on priority problems or work through the full list
  • Walk through the active problem list together — certainty, urgency, next best question
  • Add your own observations — what you saw, what they told you
  • Flag problems that look urgent or have changed
  • Tap "Explore choices" on any problem to open My Health Choices with the full context pre-loaded
  • Hand a structured package to the physician when needed

What it gives the person

  • A clear view of every active problem — at its actual level of certainty
  • An activation score that shapes how the session is paced
  • The "next best question" for each problem — what is most needed to know
  • A direct path into My Health Choices for any problem
  • The ability to say "not pursuing this" for problems they have chosen to set aside
Tool 2
My Health Choices
The option explorer
My Health Choices is an AI-assisted conversation that opens pre-loaded with the person's full clinical record and focuses on a specific active problem. It presents the evidence, the tradeoffs, and the uncertainties in plain language — not to tell them what to do, but to make sure they understand what they are actually choosing between. When the AI discusses specific options with quantified evidence, it triggers an Option Box: 1,000 dots where teal = benefit, red = harm, grey = unaffected.

What you do with it

  • Open from "Explore choices" in My Health Picture — context is pre-loaded
  • Ask questions together in plain language — AI responds from the medical literature
  • Walk through the Option Box together when it appears
  • Use suggestion chips to guide the conversation
  • Press Save Summary at the end — saves the exploration to the Shared Care Plan
  • Record key decisions back in My Health Picture

What makes it different

  • Full SCP context pre-loaded — no setup, no re-entering information
  • AI draws on the medical literature in real time — not pre-authored option sets
  • Option Box makes evidence visible at a glance — 1,000 dots, three colors
  • Suggestion chips guide the conversation forward
  • Available at home, before the appointment, without time pressure
Tool 3
My Shared
Care Plan
The living record
My Shared Care Plan is a living document that every member of a person's care team — health partner, physician, family member they choose — can read and contribute to. It is updated after every significant interaction. No phone call disappears. No recommendation gets lost. Everyone reads the same record.

What you do with it

  • Write a brief note after every visit or check-in
  • Record what you observed, what was discussed, what was decided
  • Share it with the physician when you escalate
  • Read it before every visit so you know where things stand
  • Keep it current — it is only as useful as its last entry

What it prevents

  • Information that exists only in someone's memory
  • Physician advice that no one wrote down
  • The person having to repeat their story every time
  • Care team members working from different information
  • Decisions made without knowing what was already tried
Tool 5
SODOTO
See · Do · Teach
SODOTO is how your skills are recognized, verified, and rewarded. Every skill you demonstrate earns a verifiable digital credential. Every skill you teach earns a higher-level credential. The network gets smarter and stronger every time someone teaches — and the economics reward that.
See One

Watch a skilled partner

Observe a health partner complete a task — a Health Picture review, a physician escalation, a Health Choices conversation. Learn the skill.

Do One

Demonstrate competency

Perform the task. A credentialed partner or physician confirms your competency. You earn a verifiable digital credential and move to a higher pay tier.

Teach One

Teach a peer — earn more

Guide another health partner through the skill. Earn a master credential. The network grows. Your earnings grow. No training department required.

Tool 4
My Support Network
Who is around the person
My Support Network shows the person's care team against a maximal list of 27 possible roles across five categories. A completeness ring shows what fraction of the network is filled. A network graph puts the patient at the center — current care members in the inner ring, empty categories as ghost nodes in the outer ring. Absence is as visible as presence.

The five categories

  • Medical — physicians, specialists, pharmacy
  • Therapy and Rehab — PT, OT, speech, mental health
  • Community and Navigation — health partner, social work, care coordinator
  • Informal Support — family, friends, faith community
  • Practical Support — transportation, meals, housing

What it shows you

  • Who is present in the person's network — and who is missing
  • Whether the gaps are clinical or social
  • How complete the network is against all 27 possible roles
  • A visual that the person can see and understand immediately
  • A starting point for the conversation: "Who else is in your corner?"

What this looks like — Rosa

You visit Rosa on Tuesday. You take the PANAS score — she's at Band 2, so you focus on the top two urgent problems. Her blood pressure reading from last month was never followed up. You tap "Explore choices" on that problem — My Health Choices opens with her full record pre-loaded. You explore the options together; an Option Box shows the evidence. You save the summary. You write a note in the Shared Care Plan. You check My Support Network — her practical support is empty; no one to help with transportation. On Thursday you send a structured question to her physician. The answer comes back Friday and goes straight into the Plan. Rosa reads it at your next visit.

When you need a physician partner

You are not asking for an appointment.
You are sending a structured question.

When a problem in the Health Picture needs physician input, you do not start from scratch. You send a package — everything the physician needs, in the form that makes the fastest and best response possible.

Growing as a health partner

Every skill you learn,
every skill you teach

Your SODOTO credentials grow with you. Each new skill earns a credential. Each skill you teach earns a higher credential and a higher pay tier. There is no ceiling — the more you know and teach, the more the cooperative values your contribution.

Foundation skills

Getting started

Health Picture review. Appointment preparation. Shared Care Plan note-writing. Basic escalation protocol. These are the first credentials you earn.

Advanced skills

Going deeper

Health Choices facilitation. Physician package preparation. Diagnosis audit support. Evidence gap identification. Each earns a higher credential.

Master skills

Teaching others

Teach any skill to a peer and earn a master credential for it. The network learns. Your earnings reflect your contribution to the whole team.

First steps

How to begin — as a
neighborhood health partner

In the first week

  • Learn to open and read a Health Picture with a person
  • Write your first Shared Care Plan note after a home visit
  • Complete your first SODOTO foundation credential
  • Meet your physician partner — understand how they prefer to receive packages
  • Identify three people in your neighborhood who would benefit from a Health Picture review

In the first month

  • Complete Health Picture reviews for your enrolled households
  • Send your first structured physician package
  • Facilitate a Health Choices conversation
  • Earn your Health Choices credential
  • Begin teaching the Health Picture review to a newer partner
Remember

You do not need to know medicine. You need to know the people in your neighborhood — and how to use these tools well. The tools carry the knowledge. You carry the relationship. That combination is what makes this work.
