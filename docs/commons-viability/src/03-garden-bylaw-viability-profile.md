# Garden Bylaw Viability Profile

A first try of [[Ostrom Design Principles And Agreement Coding]] on a real-looking agreement. The bylaw is the community-garden example used throughout the Agreement Checker, after the group has answered every question the checker asked. By the checker's own standards it is complete and clear. Read against Ostrom's design principles, it has more gaps than lines filled in.

## The bylaw

1. Gardeners must not water on days not assigned to them, unless hand-watering seedlings.
2. When the tank is below half, gardeners must not water their plots with sprinklers.
3. Gardeners may hand-water seedlings on any day.
4. The water keeper must suspend watering for one week for any gardener who breaks these rules.
5. Each spring, a water keeper must be chosen from the garden members.
6. Anyone who holds a plot counts as a gardener.
7. Each spring, the water keeper must assign watering days to every gardener.

## By level

Sentences 1, 2, 3, 4 and 7 are operational: they are about watering and about enforcing the watering rules. Sentences 5 and 6 create things: the water keeper and who counts as a gardener. No sentence says how any rule can be changed, or how the members decide anything together. The garden can follow its rules. On paper, it cannot change them.

## The profile

**1A User boundaries · partial.** Sentence 6 says who counts as a gardener: anyone who holds a plot. Nothing says how someone gets a plot or loses one, so the boundary has a door with no handle.

**1B Resource boundaries · absent.** The rules are about the water and the tank, but nothing says what the shared water is: which tank, which taps, whether rain barrels count.

**2A Fit with local conditions · can't tell from the text.** Ask the gardeners, and watch the tank through a dry summer.

**2B Costs in proportion to benefits · absent.** Gardeners get water. No sentence asks anything of them in return: no dues, no work hours, no share of filling or fixing the tank.

**3 Collective choice · absent.** Sentence 5 draws the keeper from the members, but does not say who chooses. Nothing says how the rules can be changed or who may propose a change.

**4A Monitoring users · partial.** The keeper suspends rule breakers, which assumes someone notices them. No sentence gives anyone the job of watching, and nothing says the keeper answers to the members.

**4B Monitoring the resource · absent.** Sentence 2 switches on "when the tank is below half". Nobody is named to read the tank or to tell the gardeners when it drops.

**5 Graduated sanctions · absent.** Every breach, first or fifth, gets the same week without water.

**6 Conflict resolution · absent.** A gardener who thinks a suspension was unfair has nowhere to take it.

**7 Recognition of rights · can't tell from the text.** The bylaw never says what gives the garden its right to the land or the water: a lease, a city program, a landowner's goodwill.

**8 Nested enterprises · not applicable yet.** The bylaw mentions no larger body. If the garden belongs to a neighborhood council or a city program, that link belongs here.

## What this shows

Of eleven lines, two are partial, six absent, two can't be told from the text, and one does not apply yet. None is fully present. Many gardens like this one run well for years on goodwill and unwritten habits, so this says nothing final about any real garden. It is a list of conversations this group has not had in writing yet.

Three of the gaps would be cheap to close. Who can change these rules, and how. Who reads the tank and tells everyone. Where a gardener takes a dispute.

## What the checker would need

Most of these lines need nothing the checker does not already collect. They need new questions asked of the same answers: does any DO WHAT? say amend, vote, or decide; does any DO WHAT? say monitor, read, or record; does any rule have more than one OR ELSE, stepping up. Lines 2A and 7 need evidence from outside the text, and the profile should say so plainly.
