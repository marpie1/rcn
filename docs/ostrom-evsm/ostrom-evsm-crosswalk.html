Ostrom and the eVSM

Where a theory of collective-action institutions and a model of any viable institution overlap, and what each supplies that the other lacks
Marc Pierson and Claude Opus 5.5 · September 2026

The question

Ostrom's model, as Eric Jones redrew it in 2003, is about what lets a collective-action institution come into being, last, and perform. The eVSM (extended Viable System Model) is about what any institution must do to stay viable. If both are right, they should overlap heavily. This note sets out where, using two sources: Jones's figure as redrawn in the RCN Graph Tool (Ostroms Collective Action in 3 phases) and Marc's eVSM plus environment for RCN.

The short answer: the overlap is large. It sits mostly in Ostrom's Survival phase, partly in Performance, and there is almost none in Origin. The VSM assumes the system already exists; Origin is about whether it forms at all.

Jones puts Common Understanding and Decision-making Costs in the users' panel, not the institution's. In a commons the users do their own steering. Beer says the same: the metasystem is a set of functions, not a separate class of people. Ostrom's design principle 3 (the people affected by the rules help make them) is what that looks like in practice.

Three positions settled in discussion

1. Trust, Similarities, Experience and Open Communication live on the eVSM's edges

Matching nodes to nodes, these four looked weakly covered. They are not properties of any sphere; they are properties of the channels. Every sphere-to-sphere link in the eVSM runs both ways (33 of the 55 possible sphere pairs are linked, 50 two-way pairs in all), and the quality of those two-way links is where Ostrom's social variables sit:

  • Experience is Ostrom's "mutually beneficial transactions over time": the history of a two-way channel settling into balance.
  • Trust reduces how much each side must check the other. A trusted channel does not need auditing.
  • Open Communication is how much a channel can carry.
  • Similarities is how little translation a channel needs where two parts meet (Beer's transducers).

RCN's own instruments already read these edges: the Agree / Disagree / Unknown edge ratings and the Block G "who you work with" network in the eVSM measures specification.

The specific channels that carry them between users are the informal OU-to-OU links, and that is where most of the variety is handled. The current eVSM drawing links OU-1 to OU-2 and OU-2 to OU-n but not OU-1 to OU-n. Marc will redraw it with an informal channel between every pair of operating units.

Two limits. Asset Structure is about how holdings are divided, not about a relationship, so it belongs with Resources & Accounting (S-7), in who pays and who receives. And Origin comes before the edges exist: Ostrom's left panel describes how channels get built; the VSM describes channels that are working.

2. Coordination and monitoring: combine the watching, not the sanctioning

Ostrom supplies the argument for folding monitoring into Coordination (S-1). In working commons the monitors are the users themselves or answer to them (design principle 4). That makes monitoring a sideways, peer activity with the same shape as coordination. Jones's own causal chain runs the same way: Info on Others' Resource Use → Common Understanding ↔ Conflict Resolution.

The condition is to combine the watching, not the sanctioning. Sanctions stay with Managing (S-6) and Policies & Legal (S-10). Beer kept coordination (System 2) and audit (System 3*) apart for a reason: operations share information freely with a coordinator who serves them, and stop once the coordinator is also the inspector. If peers collude and nobody reports, Ostrom's backstop is Dual Enforcement, with Regulators outside.

Beer describes two levels of coordination: operating units coordinating directly with each other, and above them a coordination function that System 3 runs. That second level is an S-1 to S-6 link, which the current drawing does not have. It is needed; how to draw it is still open.

Open: how to show the inspection function without losing the simplicity, symmetry and readability of the eVSM metasystem. The candidates are adding edges rather than a node (for example return edges from each operating unit to the Hub, which are one-way in the current file), marking the watching as a property of S-1's existing edges, or a small marker on S-1. Nothing is chosen yet.

  • Each band writes the rules for the band below. In Ostrom, operational rules are made in the collective-choice arena, so S-6 and S-7 make them. The constitutional band decides who sits in the collective-choice arena and how its budget rules can change.
  • Why System 4 sits in both: constitutional change is usually set off by what Research sees, a changing environment or collective choices that keep failing. That is Jones's Rate of Change → Survival link. Planning/Research also sits in a balancing pair with Managing, and that pair is itself a collective-choice arena.
  • Recursion: every operating unit has its own metasystem and its own three levels. The larger system's collective-choice rules limit the local system's constitutional choices. That is Ostrom's nesting seen through Beer's recursion.

The Markov-blanket reading

Marc reads the eVSM as a Markov blanket that minimizes free energy (Friston). The sensory / motor states are S-4, S-7 and S-9; the tuning layer is S-1, S-2, S-5, S-10 and S-11; S-3, S-6 and S-8 are the internal core, the layer the eVSM introduction already calls the "internal core layer." Every operating unit is a smaller blanket of its own, one level of recursion down. The drawings mark each node's blanket role.

The blanket holds in the current file: every environment node connects only to S-4, S-7 or S-9. That is also why Regulators should reach Policies & Legal through Research rather than directly. Internal states may not touch the outside.

Ostrom fits this reading closely:

  • Common Understanding, in Jones's words "information storage with a comparator inside it," is a prior plus a prediction-error signal, the core of free-energy minimization.
  • Ostrom's revised rule, "the smallest and simplest set of rules relevant to the variety in the environment," is Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety, and it is free energy's trade-off between complexity and accuracy: the simplest model that still fits the world.
  • Rate of Change → Survival is a world that changes faster than the model can update, so prediction error cannot be brought down.
  • Jones's claim that outside factors reach origin and performance only indirectly is the blanket condition. His government arrows cross the boundary at a few points: Legal Status, de facto vs. de jure, Dual Enforcement, Technology for Monitoring and Speed of Access to Info. Those are Ostrom's sensory states. Where Jones draws government reaching into the users' panel, the blanket reading says the arrow lands on a sensory state first.

What each supplies the other

What Ostrom gives the eVSM

  • Origin: how the channels get built before they can carry anything.
  • Equity: Asset Structure puts who gains and who pays into the budget question at S-7.
  • The state as a source of legitimacy, not just another item in the environment. In the eVSM it arrives through S-9.
  • Dual enforcement as the backstop behind peer monitoring.

What the eVSM gives Ostrom

  • Balancing the present against the future (Beer's System 3–System 4 pair). Jones's map has nothing that plans or innovates; Perceived Alternatives is its only forward-looking node.
  • Separating coordination from management. Ostrom merges them into "rules plus enforcement."
  • Alarm signals (Beer's algedonic channel) that go straight to the top. Ostrom's map has no fast path for alarms.
  • Recursion stated outright. Ostrom implies it with a single node.
  • Blanket discipline: every outside influence lands on a sensory state first.

Changes to the eVSM

  • Agreed: an informal channel between every pair of operating units (Marc will redraw).
  • Agreed: an S-1 to S-6 link for Beer's second level of coordination. How to draw it is still to decide.
  • Agreed: Coordination carries the watching; sanctions stay in S-6 and S-10.
  • Not needed: a direct S-10 to Regulators edge. S-9 carries that traffic, as the blanket requires.
  • Open: how to draw inspection without breaking the metasystem's symmetry.
