Ostrom and the eVSM

Where a theory of collective-action institutions and a model of any viable institution overlap, and what each supplies that the other lacks
Marc Pierson and Claude Opus 5.5 · September 2026

The question

Ostrom's model, as Eric Jones redrew it in 2003, is about what lets a collective-action institution come into being, last, and perform. The eVSM (extended Viable System Model) is about what any institution must do to stay viable. If both are right, they should overlap heavily. This note sets out where, using two sources: Jones's figure as redrawn in the RCN Graph Tool (Ostroms Collective Action in 3 phases) and Marc's eVSM plus environment for RCN.

A later section sets this work beside Jon Walker's own VSM analysis of Ostrom, which reached many of the same conclusions from her eight design principles rather than from Jones's figure.

The short answer: the overlap is large. It sits mostly in Ostrom's Survival phase, partly in Performance, and there is almost none in Origin. The VSM assumes the system already exists; Origin is about whether it forms at all.

Jones puts Common Understanding and Decision-making Costs in the users' panel, not the institution's. In a commons the users do their own steering. Beer says the same: the metasystem is a set of functions, not a separate class of people. Ostrom's design principle 3 (the people affected by the rules help make them) is what that looks like in practice.

Three positions settled in discussion

1. Trust, Similarities, Experience and Open Communication live on the eVSM's edges

Matching nodes to nodes, these four looked weakly covered. They are not properties of any sphere; they are properties of the channels. Every sphere-to-sphere link in the eVSM runs both ways (33 of the 55 possible sphere pairs are linked, 50 two-way pairs in all), and the quality of those two-way links is where Ostrom's social variables sit:

  • Experience is Ostrom's "mutually beneficial transactions over time": the history of a two-way channel settling into balance.
  • Trust reduces how much each side must check the other. A trusted channel does not need auditing.
  • Open Communication is how much a channel can carry.
  • Similarities is how little translation a channel needs where two parts meet (Beer's transducers).

RCN's own instruments already read these edges: the Agree / Disagree / Unknown edge ratings and the Block G "who you work with" network in the eVSM measures specification.

The specific channels that carry them between users are the informal OU-to-OU links, and that is where most of the variety is handled. The current eVSM drawing links OU-1 to OU-2 and OU-2 to OU-n but not OU-1 to OU-n. Marc will redraw it with an informal channel between every pair of operating units.

Two limits. Asset Structure is about how holdings are divided, not about a relationship, so it belongs with Resources & Accounting (S-7), in who pays and who receives. And Origin comes before the edges exist: Ostrom's left panel describes how channels get built; the VSM describes channels that are working.

2. Coordination and monitoring: combine the watching, not the sanctioning

Ostrom supplies the argument for folding monitoring into Coordination (S-1). In working commons the monitors are the users themselves or answer to them (design principle 4). That makes monitoring a sideways, peer activity with the same shape as coordination. Jones's own causal chain runs the same way: Info on Others' Resource Use → Common Understanding ↔ Conflict Resolution.

The condition is to combine the watching, not the sanctioning. Sanctions stay with Managing (S-6) and Policies & Legal (S-10). Beer kept coordination (System 2) and audit (System 3*) apart for a reason: operations share information freely with a coordinator who serves them, and stop once the coordinator is also the inspector. If peers collude and nobody reports, Ostrom's backstop is Dual Enforcement, with Regulators outside.

Beer describes two levels of coordination: operating units coordinating directly with each other, and above them a coordination function that System 3 runs. That second level is an S-1 to S-6 link, which the current drawing does not have. It is needed; how to draw it is still open.

Open: how to show the inspection function without losing the simplicity, symmetry and readability of the eVSM metasystem. Jon Walker's analysis (below) separates monitoring into kinds that sit in different places, and suggests most of it can be drawn as edges rather than a new sphere. Nothing is chosen yet.

The Markov-blanket reading

Marc reads the eVSM as a Markov blanket that minimizes free energy (Friston). The sensory / motor states are S-4, S-7 and S-9; the tuning layer is S-1, S-2, S-5, S-10 and S-11; S-3, S-6 and S-8 are the internal core, the layer the eVSM introduction already calls the "internal core layer." Every operating unit is a smaller blanket of its own, one level of recursion down. The drawings mark each node's blanket role.

The blanket holds in the current file: every environment node connects only to S-4, S-7 or S-9. That is also why Regulators should reach Policies & Legal through Research rather than directly. Internal states may not touch the outside.

Ostrom fits this reading closely:

  • Common Understanding, in Jones's words "information storage with a comparator inside it," is a prior plus a prediction-error signal, the core of free-energy minimization.
  • Ostrom's revised rule, "the smallest and simplest set of rules relevant to the variety in the environment," is Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety, and it is free energy's trade-off between complexity and accuracy: the simplest model that still fits the world.
  • Rate of Change → Survival is a world that changes faster than the model can update, so prediction error cannot be brought down.
  • Jones's claim that outside factors reach origin and performance only indirectly is the blanket condition. His government arrows cross the boundary at a few points: Legal Status, de facto vs. de jure, Dual Enforcement, Technology for Monitoring and Speed of Access to Info. Those are Ostrom's sensory states. Where Jones draws government reaching into the users' panel, the blanket reading says the arrow lands on a sensory state first.

What each supplies the other

What Ostrom gives the eVSM

  • Origin: how the channels get built before they can carry anything.
  • Equity: Asset Structure puts who gains and who pays into the budget question at S-7.
  • The state as a source of legitimacy, not just another item in the environment. In the eVSM it arrives through S-9.
  • Dual enforcement as the backstop behind peer monitoring.

What the eVSM gives Ostrom

  • Balancing the present against the future (Beer's System 3–System 4 pair). Jones's map has nothing that plans or innovates; Perceived Alternatives is its only forward-looking node.
  • Separating coordination from management. Ostrom merges them into "rules plus enforcement."
  • Synergy: System 3's job of making the whole produce more than its parts. Jones's map has no path for gains: Performance is fed only by Decision-making Costs, Enforcement Costs and Survival, so the map explains how a commons avoids losing value, not how it creates it. Ostrom's own word for it is provision (see Walker, below).
  • Alarm signals (Beer's algedonic channel) that go straight to the top. Ostrom's map has no fast path for alarms.
  • Recursion stated outright. Ostrom implies it with a single node.
  • Blanket discipline: every outside influence lands on a sensory state first.

Jon Walker's reading of Ostrom

Walker's section "The Commons and the Work of Elinor Ostrom" (7.2.5 of a longer work; it refers to "the authors" and cites Espinosa & Walker 2013, so it is probably written with Angela Espinosa) tests Ostrom's eight design principles against Beer's criteria for viability: all five systems present, connected correctly, and with enough capacity.

What he finds

  • Direct matches: nested enterprises (principle 8) are recursion; clear boundaries (1) are the system in focus; rules fitted to local conditions (2) are the VSM's environmental niche; recognized self-determination (7) is operational autonomy.
  • System 1 is the users together with the resource and their equipment.
  • System 2 is conflict resolution (principle 6), informal ("the local coffee house") and formal (Valencia's water court). He adds further System 2s: shared values, time-sharing of maintenance, and social control.
  • System 3: graduated sanctions (5) are its enforcement side. Its main job, creating synergy, is not among the principles, though it runs through Ostrom's cases.
  • System 4 is not among the principles either. His clearest case is the Los Angeles West Basin water producers commissioning an engineer to survey the threat. He proposes adding it as a principle.
  • System 5 is the meeting of all users and its constitution.
  • Only System 2 is explicit in Ostrom's list.
  • How the systems connect: autonomy is limited only by the cohesion of the whole. The resource bargain is a closed loop: System 3 allocates resources and the users send accountability information back. Graduated sanctions are Beer's intervention rules. Systems 3, 4 and 5 usually meet as one general meeting of all users, and because the users take part they identify with the whole commons, which he thinks explains high compliance despite low sanctions.
  • His result is a table of 11 VSM principles for long-lasting commons. Three have no Ostrom counterpart: System 3 works from a real-time model of the operations; System 4 is present; Systems 3 and 4 interact continuously.

Where he confirms this work

Nesting as recursion, boundaries as identity, conflict resolution as System 2, and Ostrom lacking a forward-looking System 4 all match. His general meeting is our point that in a commons the users are the metasystem. His principle that System 3 works from a real-time model built from the users' collective knowledge matches Jones's Common Understanding, the store with a comparator inside it. Two independent readings reach the same map.

Synergy

Beer gives System 3 two jobs. One, keeping each unit within the rules through monitoring and sanctions, is in Ostrom's principles. The other, making the whole produce more than the units could separately, is not. Coordination (System 2) prevents loss: it damps oscillation so units don't undo each other's work, as in Alanya's rotation of fishing spots. Synergy (System 3) creates gain: something no unit could have alone.

  • Tibi dam: Alicante's farmers built it together in 1594, and it doubled their water.
  • Torbel: grazing the alpine pasture in common beat fencing it into plots. Privatizing it would have added fences, markets for grass, and duplicated water systems, so part of the synergy is simply what the commons avoids spending.
  • West Basin: the producers pooled money for a survey no single producer would have paid for.

Ostrom's vocabulary covers this under another name. Principle 2 speaks of appropriation and provision rules. Appropriation rules say who may take what; provision rules say who contributes labour and materials to build and maintain the resource. Provision is her word for synergy, and it links the two frameworks more directly than Walker's text does.

In the eVSM, synergy runs through the collective-choice band and then becomes infrastructure. Managing (S-6) sees the whole set of operating units and finds combinations that pay. Resources & Accounting (S-7) has two halves: redistribution is the resource bargain, and pooling for a shared investment is synergy. Innovation (S-5) proposes joint projects. Once built, a dam is infrastructure: the shared services (S-2, S-3, S-4) are synergy made permanent, which is why they sit between System 3 and the operating units. In Ashby's terms synergy often reduces variety: one dam instead of a hundred wells, one survey instead of twenty guesses.

It also bears on legitimacy. System 3 may limit autonomy only to get a greater payoff for the whole, so the payoff has to be visible. If users cannot see what they gain from the constraints, the constraints read as control rather than coordination. One eVSM question for a neighbourhood group: "What do we have that none of us could have alone?" If nobody can name anything, collective choice is running on enforcement alone.

Keep the kinds apart. If peer channels start carrying reports upward, users stop talking freely on them; that is why only the watching was combined into Coordination, not the sanctioning. Drawn this way, peer watching is already agreed, accountability is a set of missing return edges (which keeps the eVSM's pairing and symmetry), and only audit might need something new, possibly just a marking on the accountability edge.

Where this work goes further

  • Ostrom's three levels. Walker does not use them. He does say that when Systems 3, 4 and 5 are one meeting, everything works through that meeting, and that separate bodies need more design. For a small group run as one meeting, what separates the levels is which rules the meeting is changing, not which body meets.
  • Origin and trust. He works from the 1990 principles, not Jones's variables, so he says nothing about origin. His nearest point, that participation builds identification with the whole, is a tuning-layer effect in blanket terms and fits the reading of trust as a property of the edges.
  • The Markov-blanket reading is not in his text.

Two slips in his text: he cites principle 8 where he means principle 7 (self-determination), and the table does not survive extraction from the PDF cleanly, though its three "not stated" rows are clear.

Changes to the eVSM

  • Agreed: an informal channel between every pair of operating units (Marc will redraw).
  • Agreed: an S-1 to S-6 link for Beer's second level of coordination. How to draw it is still to decide.
  • Agreed: Coordination carries the watching; sanctions stay in S-6 and S-10.
  • Not needed: a direct S-10 to Regulators edge. S-9 carries that traffic, as the blanket requires.
  • Open: how to draw inspection without breaking the metasystem's symmetry. Leading candidate, after Walker: accountability as return edges OU → Hub → S-6 / S-7, with audit as a marking on those edges rather than a new sphere.
