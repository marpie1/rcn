e-VSM Measures — Item Specification

Twenty-three added items that give the e-VSM survey what Stafford Beer's three indices need: an environment channel for Latency, an observed channel for Actuality, and a rule that separates what a neighborhood can fix from what must be fixed above it. For managing, and for making an investment case to grant makers that can be checked.

v0.1 · 2026-09-19 · items file: tools/evsm-measures-items.json · loads via Setup → Extra Items in evsm-svg-v3.html

1. Purpose

Two audiences, one instrument. A neighborhood center needs measures it can manage by: what is weak, whether it is getting better, whether a change worked. A grant maker needs the same numbers to answer a different question: is there latent performance here that money would release, and did it? The survey as it stands — eleven spheres, sixty-six relationships — measures how residents believe the neighborhood is functioning, corrected for how generous each resident is (the Rasch pass documented in eVSM and Rasch). That is a real measure of belief. It is not yet a measure of Latency, because it never asks about the environment, and it is not yet checkable against anything observed.

This specification adds three blocks:

  • Block F — Beyond the boundary. Ten environment couplings, already drawn on the e-VSM diagram, now asked. This is the Latency channel.
  • Block G — Who you work with. Two sociometric questions. Observed coordination, reach and leadership, on a network rather than a rating.
  • Block H — The center's record. Eleven counts, one per sphere, answered once per wave from the center's own records. Observed Actuality.

With these, each sphere has a belief and an observation, a productivity gap and a latency gap, and a place on a chart wave by wave. Section 5 says what a funder should be shown and what they should refuse to accept.

2. The measurement model

Beer's three measures of capacity, in his own definitions, and their three ratios:

Actuality — what we are managing to do now, with existing resources, under existing constraints.
Capability — what we could be doing, still now, with existing resources under existing constraints, if we really worked at it.
Potentiality — what we ought to be doing by developing resources and removing constraints, within what is known to be feasible.
Productivity = Actuality / Capability  ·  Latency = Capability / Potentiality  ·  Performance = Actuality / Potentiality = Productivity × Latency

Three consequences follow from the definitions, and the items below are built on them.

The split rule

Capability is bounded by existing constraints; Potentiality requires removing them. So a sphere's shortfall divides into the part the neighborhood can close by working at it and the part that requires something the neighborhood does not control. On the e-VSM diagram those are two kinds of input: internal edges (sphere to sphere, the sixty-six already asked) and environment couplings (Financiers to Resources, Regulators to Awareness Beyond, and so on — Block F).

Productivity gap of sphere Y  =  blocked internal inputs to Y  →  the neighborhood's own work
Latency gap of sphere Y  =  blocked environment inputs to Y  →  work for the level above (Section 2, routing)

Belief and observation

Every existing item is a belief. Beer's Actuality was an observation — tons produced today — while his Capability and Potentiality were negotiated beliefs. Blocks G and H add one observation per sphere. When belief and observation move together, the measure is corroborated. When they diverge, that divergence is itself a finding: a neighborhood that rates its coordination go while its groups share no work has learned something.

The chart

Wave by wave, each sphere's rater-corrected measure goes on its own process behavior chart (rcn-spc.html). Beer's three quantities are then on the chart: Actuality is this wave's point; Capability is the center line, what the sphere demonstrably does when nothing special is happening; Potentiality is the ceiling. Variation inside the limits is the productivity band; the distance from center line to ceiling is the latency gap; a point below the limits that does not recover within an agreed number of waves is Beer's algedonic signal and goes up a level.

Routing: which level above

Several recursions apply at once (Pérez Ríos): a neighborhood sits in a geographic container, in a sectoral platform, and in a network of affiliation. The environment type on a blocked coupling says which one owns the constraint. That is what the route column in Block F records. A blocked R->9 is the city's; a blocked F->7 is the capital platform's; a blocked I->9 is the RCN's own System 4.

The catalytic test, in this arithmetic. The RCN's job is not to absorb a neighborhood's latency but to convert it: to move a constraint from the Potentiality side of the line to the Capability side, and then withdraw. Success is a latency gap that closes and stays closed for two waves after the support ends — the center line moves toward the ceiling and holds.

3. The items

4. Computation

Per sphere, per wave. Nothing here needs a new estimator; it needs the fixed core of relationships (every respondent answers the same set) and at least two waves.

Beer transformed his bounded, skewed ratios with an inverse sine before filtering, to get something Gaussian enough to test. The logit does that job properly, which is why the chart carries the logit and the ratio is for reading.

5. What makes these valid, and what a funder should require

Validity here is not a claim; it is a set of checks the data either pass or do not, each reported with the numbers.

CheckWhat it showsPass
ContentEvery item is a function or a coupling of the model. Nothing is asked that is not on the diagram.By construction
Item fit (infit, outfit)Whether an item measures the same thing as the rest. A misfitting sphere is contested, not middling — that is a finding to read, not a defect to hide.0.7–1.5, or explained
Thresholds orderedWhether stop/caution/go, and the four agree categories, are used in the order written.Ordered; collapse if not
SeparationHow many levels the instrument can tell apart. Below two strata, rank nothing.≥ 2 strata
Invariance across WorldsWhether the same item means the same thing to a funder, a renter, a lifelong resident. Differential functioning is the equity check.Reported per item
Belief–observation agreementWhether rater-corrected belief and the observed count move together over waves.Direction agrees, or the divergence is reported
StabilityThe chart: limits from ≥ 6 waves, signals by Wheeler's rules, not by eye.Reported with SE

Minimum reporting standard

  • At least 20 respondents per wave; the number stated.
  • Rater-corrected sphere measures with standard errors; never summed statuses.
  • The fit table and thresholds, every wave.
  • Both channels for the sphere the investment targets: belief and the Block H count.
  • For an investment case: the sphere's latency input index and which couplings are blocked, with the route. That is the investable gap.
  • For a result: at least two waves before and two after, on the chart; released latency as the movement of the center line toward the ceiling, and the coupling that unblocked. Sustained two waves after support ends.
  • No claim from a single wave.
Limits, stated. Belief items are self-report. Seven or ten respondents give conversation material, not numbers. JMLE spreads estimates 10–20% wider than the truth; read spacing as approximate, order as reliable. Many-facet Rasch, which puts neighborhoods on one ruler, is not in the tool yet and should wait for two neighborhoods with twenty respondents each. Counts can be gamed; that is why each is paired with a belief and why the known failure is written next to it.

6. Tool changes

The items file loads today. The following are small additions, in order of value.

  1. Survey — count scale. An ITEM_SCALES entry rendering a whole-number input (two inputs where the item has two questions). Until then, Block H answers are free text and the aggregator parses the number.
  2. Survey — pick scale with a roster in the config. Multi-select of group names plus a free line. Until then, Block G answers are free text, one name per line.
  3. Aggregator — per-sphere input indices. The productivity input index (internal edges) and the latency input index (Block F) beside each sphere, with the blank rate; blocked couplings listed with their route.
  4. Aggregator — Block G network. Ties from G-1 and G-2, coded; density, components, in-degree distribution, reciprocity; never a named tie in the report.
  5. SPC — ceiling line and the three indices. A ceiling for the selected series and, for the selected point, Actuality, Capability (center line) and Potentiality with Beer's ratios read off.
  6. Convention, no code. One respondent per wave tagged Center record-keeper answers Block H; the aggregator treats that World as the record.
