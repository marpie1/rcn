What we are supposed to do

The substrate is built and working. What is left is not code — it is a queue of decisions only you and Kerry can make, and each one is a small, concrete act in a tool you already have open.

Written 2026-08-05 · revised the same day, after Marc identified the ERD→CLD variabilization as the reason the unsigned edges exist
Before anything works: Neo4j Desktop must be running the RCN SCHEMA server, and then cd ~/rcn && python3 substrate/api.py. If a projection returns cannot reach http://localhost:7474, the database is stopped — Claude Code has full admin over it but cannot start it. That is the one thing only you can do.

What was built, in one paragraph

One Neo4j graph holds the meaning of every RCN drawing — what each concept is, which way each arrow points, whether it is positive or negative, and who drew it. The diagram tools became lenses over that one graph instead of openers of separate files. You can now open any of the 16 EIP aspect drawings straight from the database, edit it in the Graph Tool, and save it back — and the save updates both the database and the source file, so neither can silently contradict the other.

The rule the whole thing follows: the substrate owns meaning, the file owns appearance, and anything derivable is computed rather than stored. Every bug found while building it was a violation of that sentence.
Neo4j owns — meaning
  • schemaLabel — the merge key
  • the variabilized label
  • polarity (+ / −)
  • relation family
  • sources — who drew it
The file owns — appearance
  • x / y positions
  • colour, font size
  • node ids (n0, n1…)
  • duplicate placements
  • instance props

Gold is computed, never stored. A concept is gold when more than one drawing contains it — size(sources) > 1, evaluated fresh on every request. That is the whole federation payoff: when two neighborhoods independently draw the same thing, the agreement surfaces rather than being declared.

Why edge families exist

This is the part worth understanding, because it is the one that makes federation actually work.

A harvest of every graph JSON in the repo found 375 labelled edges carrying 202 distinct labels, 115 of them used exactly once. And already drifting: create and creates. support, supports, Supports. constitute and constitutes. lead_to and leads_to.

Two people drawing the same relation land on different strings. So nothing merges, and no edge ever goes gold. The federation payoff fails in exactly the place it matters most.

The fix is a shared vocabulary of seven relation families in tools/edge-families.js, alongside the eight node families that families.js already provides:

FamilyMeansExample verbs
InfluenceA changes the level of B. Polarity says which waycreate, drive, amplify, undermine, erode
ProvisionA supplies what B depends on. A stops, B stopssupport, require, pay for, steward, hold up
CompositionA is part of Bencompass, aggregate, constitute
ClassificationA is a kind of Bis a, generalize, expressed as
TransformationSubstance moves or converts from A to Byield, produce, lead to, consume
AgencyA acts in Bplay, enroll, oversee, participate, inhabit
SequenceA comes before B in a procedurethen, next, escalate, review

The distinction that earns its keep is Influence versus Provision. Influence is incremental — more of A, somewhat more of B. Provision is structural — if A stops, B stops. "The grant funds the program" is not "the grant increases the program." Only the second is a CLD arrow; only the first tells you what collapses when the funding is cut. Merge them and you lose the fragility map, which for neighborhood work is most of the value.

Declared, not drawn

The first version of this proposal put the seven families on the arrowhead. Rendering the Cave Drawing exemplar killed that idea. It tells its two edge kinds apart with colour and weight and dash — three redundant channels for two distinctions, which is why it reads across a room. Seven kinds on one small mark is the opposite trade, and it spends the reader's attention to serve the database.

So the family is declared as data on the legend entry, and your local styling stays free and loud. Superior's red dashed "acts on them" and Whatcom's orange dotted "grinds them down" both declare linkFamily: Influence and merge into a gold edge, while each drawing keeps whatever reads best for its own group.

Better still, your own suggestion: the family can be shown as words on the edge. The Aa Text button cycles label / family / both / none. Words have no ceiling and no colour-vision risk — and crucially they make a wrong family visible and correctable. A group can point at the word "Provision" and say "no, that one's Influence." A dash pattern nobody can read is never challenged.

What is left — the decision queue

None of this is code. Each item is a judgement only you and Kerry can make.

1 Seventeen unsigned edges — but they are not gaps

These drawings began as entity-relationship diagrams and were variabilized into CLDs. That gains a causal reading and costs the structural one, because the two often run in opposite directions — and both are true. 
Org --exists_for--> Purpose      an org exists for a purpose        STRUCTURAL
Purpose --(+)--> Org             coherence of purpose raises
                                 effectiveness of org               CAUSAL

That is not a suspicion. Across the 16 drawings, an edge carries a sign if and only if its ERD direction and its causal direction agree:

agrees with the CLDreversednot in the CLD
signed4610
unsigned095

The single signed-and-reversed exception was one Claude Code introduced on 29 July and has since reverted. The original author's work is 46 for 46. Whoever variabilized these hit exactly this problem and handled it correctly: they signed the edges where both readings pointed the same way, and left blank the ones where they conflicted.

So a blank is a marked conflict, not an omission. Do not choose a direction — keep both, as two edges. The structural one stays unsigned; the causal one is added pointing the other way, with its sign. Nine additions, no deletions, and the CLD already tells you what nine of them should be.

The seventeen break down as:

  • 10 ERD/CLD conflicts — the CLD supplies the causal direction and sign for each. These need confirming, not inventing
  • 5 genuinely open — the CLD has no reading for them at all. Real work for you and Kerry
  • 2 merge artefacts — the two self-loops. Not decisions at all

Full list in substrate/unsigned-edges.md.

1b The structure lens

Because the ERD reading is real and worth keeping, it now has its own projection:

/projection/structure    the ERD — part-of, is-a, acts-in, depends-on
/projection/causal       the CLD — signed influence

Same nodes, different edges, nothing stored twice. Polarity is deliberately emitted as none on the structure lens: a structural relation is not the kind of statement that has a sign, and drawing one would be a claim nobody made.

For the same reason ?± Gaps now skips structural families and tells you how many it skipped. On the org drawing it went from "5 unsigned causal edges" to "3 unsigned causal edges · 2 structural edges not flagged" — because constitutes (Composition) and require (Provision) were complete all along.

2 Twenty-two edges with no relation family

Of the 57 edges in the signed CLD, 35 took a family automatically from the author's own wording. The other 22 did not, and nothing was guessed — an unmatched edge is left blank rather than assigned a plausible family, on the same principle as the mismatches file: a wrong answer nobody can see is worse than a gap everybody can.

Twelve of the 22 carry no label at all. Those cannot be inferred by any method — they need someone who remembers what the arrow meant. List in substrate/eip-composite-edge-families.md.

3 Two verb pairs still split in the data

constitute/constitutes and create/creates are each two separate edges in the substrate, and neither half can ever go gold. The typeahead stops new drift; these two existing pairs need fixing by hand. Two minutes each.

How to do the work

  1. Start Neo4j Desktop — the RCN SCHEMA server. Then cd ~/rcn && python3 substrate/api.py
  2. Open a drawing at localhost:8768/tools/graph-tool-v22.html?url=/projection/subgraph/org — it arrives in your own layout, with gold nodes the file never knew about
  3. Click ?± Gaps — every unsigned edge gets a ?, so you fix a whole drawing in one pass instead of hunting
  4. Click an edge → PROPERTIES → Polarity + / − / ∅. Alt+click the edge to flip its direction if you settle that too
  5. Set the family — Aa Text in both mode shows label and family together; that is the mode this work is meant to be done in
  6. Click → Substrate — writes the database and the source file, and tells you what changed
  7. Run git diff and commit. The file is canonical, so git is the history
Nothing you do can destroy someone else's work. Saving replaces only that drawing: the substrate drops your aspect from every sources list, deletes only what is left with no witness at all, then writes what you sent. A concept another drawing also contains keeps its other witnesses and survives. This was tested by submitting a completely empty org drawing — zero concepts left the union.

Two things this work already settled about the data

The two self-loops in the mismatches file are merge artefacts, exactly as that document suspected. Org --relate_with--> Org and ActiveGoal --require--> ActiveGoal run, in the source file, between two different nodes that happen to share a label — the org drawing places some concepts twice so its edges do not cross. Nobody drew a self-loop; merging invented it. That closes 2 of the 23 open items, and they are not decisions.

The anomalous edge-direction switching is not a bug. Fifty-four edges agree with the CLD's direction and eleven are reversed, scattered across eight drawings with correct edges sitting alongside them. A reverseAll() flips a whole drawing at once — this is the opposite shape. What looks like switching is composing from the aspect subgraphs versus reading the signed CLD: the same relation genuinely points two ways in two artifacts. The substrate makes that visible for the first time, because both readings now sit in one place and can be diffed.

Honest limits

  • Provenance grain is "which drawing", not who or when. sources: ['org','person'] means the concept appears in those two files. Making it "Kerry, 3 July" needs the drawings to carry that — a data-collection change, not a schema one
  • No concurrent-edit protection. Two people saving the same drawing: last write wins, silently. Fine for two people talking to each other; not fine for a public commons
  • The 26-node composite is read-only in practice. Its provenance was reconstructed rather than recorded — good enough to show gold, not good enough to slice apart. Round-trip against the 16 drawings instead
  • Patient data is out of scope. SCP and Epic FHIR get a separate database with federation and gold-merging switched off. Nothing here applies to them
