# The Six Nested Questions

The shelves are not invented. They are Marc's own frame from [[RCN Modeling Theory]], and they appear in nearly every framework read for [[Six Questions Five Pathways]].

The order is load-bearing. Answering What first is the standard failure, and it produces solutions nobody will carry.

## The tools placed against them

- **When and Where** — Settings and context. RCN Map, Issue Polygon Map, RCN Timeline, [[Story Structure]]
- **Why** — Purposes, motives, beliefs. Graph Tool in CLD mode, [[Vester's Sensitivity Model]], the 14 [[Leverage Points]], BGTE
- **Who** — Roles and agency. Sofi-VSM, [[SODOTO]], CfA-dSC Contract Creator, My Support Network, [[IAD]]
- **How as strategy** — Strategies and interventions. [[Cynefin]], Graph Tool in CLD mode, EIP Integration Explorer, Vester, Graph Composer
- **How as mechanism** — Design and implementation. Graph Tool in OPM mode, Graph Tool in SFD mode, IAD, NRM Tripod Beta, Wardley Map Generator
- **What** — Outcomes and affordances. RCN Process Behavior Charts, eVSM, Positive Affect Score, Conversation Navigator, Cave Drawings

## Two things fall out

The What shelf is where the newest tool landed. Process Behavior Charts arrived fully documented and is exactly a What-shelf instrument: it tells you whether an outcome really changed or whether you are reading noise.

The Who shelf is the thinnest in documentation despite holding SODOTO, contracts and Sofi-VSM — a real gap, because Who is where self-organizing actually happens. It is built out in [[WHO Collective Action After Ostrom]].

## One tool, several shelves

The Graph Tool answers Why in CLD mode, How-as-strategy in EIP mode, and How-as-mechanism in OPM and SFD modes. That is not untidiness. A hammer is used in many processes and does a different job in each, which is why shelving is a property in the catalog rather than a folder on disk.
