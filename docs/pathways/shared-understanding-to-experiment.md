# Shared Understanding to Experiment

**Problem:** The situation is genuinely complex — cause and effect are visible only in hindsight — and the group needs to intervene without pretending to predict.

**Context:** Entered from [[Systemic Problem Solving]] when [[Cynefin]] says complex.

Kind: production. Confidence: one star. Transmissible via causal loop diagramming, which is the one Active skill in the registry.

## 1. Do we see the same system?

Climb [[The Causal Ladder]] in order: campfire conversation, then cave drawing, then a causal loop diagram in the Graph Tool.

Shared understanding is shared only when people who disagree can point at the same picture and say where they disagree. The criterion for a cave drawing is that it is fit for finger pointing, not that it is right.

## 2. Where would a small push do the most?

Work the 14 [[Leverage Points]] — Meadows' twelve plus Time Horizon and Awareness of Place — from the top down, not the bottom up.

Parameters and numbers are the weakest places to intervene. Paradigm, goals and the power to self-organize are the strongest. Time and Place sit at the top with world view, feeding goals directly.

## 3. What do we think will happen?

Model the innovation before committing to it, using [[Vester's Sensitivity Model]] or the Graph Tool in stock-and-flow mode. Which variables are most influential given this feedback structure — not what the numbers will be.

**Gate — the numbers question:** do not move to simulation until you have earned it. Causal loop diagrams have no numbers, and Meadows warned that numbers are the weakest leverage. A simulation built before the feedback structure is understood is garbage dressed in numbers.

## 4. What actually changed?

Do the experiment, then read the result honestly with Process Behavior Charts.

Designed experiences and experiments are a way for humans to listen to systems as they communicate back to us.

Process Behavior Charts are what separate a real change from noise. Without them a group will claim both success and failure from the same data.

**Closes to:** back into step 1 with a revised picture. The loop is the method; a single pass is not.
