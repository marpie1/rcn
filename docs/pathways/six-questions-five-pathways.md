# Six Questions Five Pathways

Twenty-one of Marc's frameworks read together. What governs all of them, what is invariant across them, and the five pathways worth writing first.

## Two models govern the use of all the others

> Beauty is the basis for using all the models. That is why I put it at the top along with the most general model of humans taking action together: the Social Action Tetrahedron. All the models below these two must be informed by these or they will lead to no good. — [[Models for Seeing Systems]]

This is a precondition, not a pathway. See [[The Two Governing Models]].

## One structure runs through everything below

Under the two governing models, one structure appears in nearly every framework: the Six Nested Questions — When, Where, Why, Who, How, What.

They are the ordered chain in [[Neighborhood Systems Patterns]]. They are the first node of [[Another Sequence of Tools]]. They are what [[Story Structure]] exists to answer in narrative form. They are the tag on every one of the [[15 Ps]]. They are the extension of Meadows, with Time and Place added to contextualize the twelve leverage points.

> The Story Structure diagram places the modeling tools not in a linear sequence but in a topology organized around the Six Questions. This is the most important organizational insight in the conversation. — [[RCN Modeling Theory]]

So the shelves for the storefront were not invented. See [[The Six Nested Questions]].

## The sequence is not a pipeline

> The sequence is not a pipeline. It is a progression of question types, and the tools are matched to questions. — [[RCN Modeling Theory]]

A step is therefore never "now open the Graph Tool". A step is a question you are now ready to ask, with a tool named as the instrument that answers it. Every pathway below is written that way.
