# The Causal Ladder

[[Causal Models]] sets out the history of causal representation as a ladder of increasing sophistication, and then gives a rule for climbing back down it.

## The rungs

1. Conversation — the most primitive causal model, the one that arises in talk before any drawing. [[Campfire Conversations]].
2. Drawing — diagrams or paintings, fit for finger pointing and conversations. [[Cave Drawing]]. The criterion is pointing, not accuracy.
3. [[Causal Loop Model]] — a huge leap designed in the 20th century. The primary language.
4. [[Stock Flow Model]] — where the numbers enter, and only here.
5. [[System Dynamics Model]] — the grammar itself, a language to understand real world systems.
6. Everything built on that grammar — [[VSM]], [[Sofi]], [[Process Maps]], [[Linkage Mapping]], [[Value Stream Mapping]], [[A3 Problem Solving]].

## The rule for climbing down

> Whenever we get mixed up and confused using our more sophisticated tools and methods, then we should go back and "diagram our sentences" in system dynamics, especially the primary language of causal loop diagramming. — [[Causal Models]]

This is not a gate. A gate blocks forward motion until a condition holds; this is a recovery, available from any depth, at any time. Every method should declare one, and for RCN the answer is the same: drop back to a causal loop diagram.

It works because of the ladder. The sophisticated tools are built up from the grammar of system dynamics, so returning to CLD is not starting over — it is returning to the language the other tools are written in.

## And on experiments

> Designed experiences and experiments can be seen as a way for humans to "listen to systems" as they communicate back to us. — [[Causal Models]]

That is the justification for running an experiment rather than a simulation, and it is the last step of [[Shared Understanding to Experiment]].
