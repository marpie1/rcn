# WHO Collective Action After Ostrom

[[Collective Action, Ostrom]] gives the Who shelf a structure the other shelves do not yet have. Thirty-one variables from decades of common-pool-resource research, and an ordered spine of three stages.

DIAGRAM_rcn-who-ostrom

## Three stages, in order

The top-level sequence is Origin, then Performance, then Survival. The colouring names what each stage is: green for forming, blue for managing, red for surviving. The Who question is not one question but three, asked in order.

- Origin, forming. Will this collective action come into being at all? Driven by Perceived Alternative Strategies, Decision-making Costs, Interdependence and subtractability, Asset Structure, Resource Scarcity.
- Performance, managing. Does it actually work once it exists? Driven by Enforcement Costs, Decision-making Costs, Dual Enforcement, Technology for Monitoring Resources, Speed of Access to Info.
- Survival, surviving. Will it persist when conditions change? Driven above all by Conflict Resolution, plus de facto versus de jure, Small Simple Set of Rules, Larger System of Institutional Support, Rate of Change.

## Two chains are drawn heavy, and they are the leverage

Governmental Agencies leads to Legal Status and Local Prerogative, which leads to Perceived Alternative Strategies, which leads to Origin.

What government grants by way of legal standing and local prerogative determines which strategies a neighbourhood can even perceive as available. That determines whether the collective action originates at all.

This is the highest-leverage claim in the model, and it says something uncomfortable: a great deal of neighbourhood capacity is foreclosed upstream, before anyone in the neighbourhood makes a choice.

The third heavy edge is Conflict Resolution to Survival. Not performance, not origin — survival. Groups do not usually die of poor execution; they die of unresolved conflict.

## Common Understanding is the hub

Counting edges, Common Understanding is the highest-degree node in the model — seven in, three out — and it sits in a mutually reinforcing pair with Trust, each feeding the other. It also feeds Conflict Resolution, which feeds back into it.

Nearly every RCN instrument that produces shared pictures — [[Campfire Conversations]], [[Cave Drawing]], the Graph Tool, MORE Outliner, FedWiki itself — is acting on the single most connected variable in Ostrom's model. The tools were not chosen with this model in mind, and they land on its hub anyway.

It also means this model is exactly what a Vester run is for. Thirty-one variables with a dense feedback structure and no numbers is the Sensitivity Model's native input.

## Why three tools overlap on this shelf

Jon Walker correlated the VSM with Ostrom's work, and Marc correlated Sofi with the VSM. Ostrom, VSM and Sofi are in tight concordance, so Sofi-VSM on the Who shelf is not a competing framework — it is the same structure seen through an organizational lens. A neighbourhood can enter at whichever of the three is handiest, if they are made handy enough.

Applied to a real commons, this shelf becomes [[Collective Action Assessment]].
