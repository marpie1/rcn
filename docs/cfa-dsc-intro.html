Conversations for Action — Dyadic Smart Contract

A commitment-tracking and economic settlement system for service agreements between two parties. Based on Fernando Flores' Conversations for Action, extended with a three-currency settlement model.

What CfA-dSC Is

A CfA-dSC is a digital contract that does two things simultaneously: it tracks the state of a commitment between two parties (using Flores' Conversations for Action state machine), and it specifies how that commitment will be settled economically across three currencies — fiat money, time-dollars, and gift.

The canonical RCN example is a Community Health Worker (the performer) contracting with a patient (the customer) for a home visit. The contract begins when the customer makes a request. It passes through negotiation, agreement, and performance. When the customer is satisfied, the contract closes and settlement is recorded — a signed document showing exactly how much was paid in each currency.

Like SODOTO credentials, CfA-dSC contracts are signed with Ed25519 keys via Veramo and verified entirely in the browser. No blockchain, no central server. Each signed history entry is append-only — the full conversation record is tamper-evident.

The "dyadic" in dSC means the contract is between exactly two parties. Multiparty agreements are handled as networks of dyadic contracts, not as single multi-party documents.

Roles

Role Who What They Do
Customer The party making the request (e.g., patient, community member, NDC) Initiates the contract. Sets or confirms Conditions of Satisfaction. Declares satisfaction (or dissatisfaction) when the work is done. Signs each move they make.
Performer The party committing to do the work (e.g., Community Health Worker, practitioner) Reviews and accepts the request. May propose changes during negotiation. Declares when performance has begun and when it is complete. Signs each move they make.
NDC Coordinator Technical facilitator at an NDC organization Creates DIDs for parties who don't have them. Runs the Veramo signing script to embed Ed25519 proofs. Facilitates contract creation when parties are not yet technical.

The CfA State Machine

Every CfA-dSC contract moves through a defined sequence of states. Each state transition is a "move" — a speech act by one of the parties, signed with their DID. The history of all moves is append-only and embedded in the contract document.

Full state list

The diagram above shows the happy path and primary branches. The complete state machine includes additional terminal and remediation states:

initiated → negotiating → contracted → performing
                                    → breakdown → renegotiating → performing (loop)
                                    → declared_complete → satisfied → closed  ✓
                                                        → dissatisfied → remediation
                        → declined   (terminal — performer declines)
                        → cancelled  (terminal — either party, before contracted)

declared_complete is a distinct state between performing and satisfied — the performer declares the work done, and the customer then confirms or disputes. remediation is entered when the customer is dissatisfied after satisfaction was declared. declined is the terminal state when a performer refuses the request outright. A planned (:Move) node type in Neo4j will persist each transition as a graph node for full temporal querying.

Two hard design rules

CCS+T is a gate. Conditions of Satisfaction plus Time (deadline) must be explicitly set before the contract can move from negotiating to contracted. This comes from a prior Conversation for Possibility. If CCS+T is missing or vague, the move to contracted is blocked.

SBU is tracked, not gated. Shared Background of Understanding can be confirmed, developing, or absent. developing is acceptable if both parties explicitly acknowledge the gap — but absent is a warning that the contract may fail. The system records SBU status; it does not block contracting if SBU is developing.

History entries

Every state transition produces a history entry recording: the prior state, the new state, the move name, the actor's DID, a timestamp, and an Ed25519 signature. History is append-only. No entry can be modified after it is signed.

The Three-Currency Settlement Model

Every CfA-dSC specifies how the contracted service will be paid. Payment is not limited to fiat money — the system models three currencies simultaneously. Parties agree to ranges, not fixed amounts, which accommodates the variability of real community economies.

Currency 1

Fiat (USD)

A minimum floor — the fiat amount that must always be paid regardless of how the other currencies settle. The floor is non-negotiable once contracted. Example: $50 of a $100 service.

Currency 2

Time-Dollars

A ceiling expressed as a percentage of the total contract value. Parties assess their own time-dollar liquidity risk — the platform shows current NDC membership count as information but does not enforce a threshold. Example: max 25% = 1 hour at $25/hr exchange rate.

Currency 3

Gift

Can be unconditional (no strings) or conditional (tied to a specific outcome). Critically, gift has a direction — it can flow either from performer to customer (a fee reduction) or from customer to performer (a bonus or gratuity). Example: $25 gift, performer→customer, fee reduction.

The Contract JSON Structure

A CfA-dSC is a single JSON document that serves as both the living contract and, once settled, the settlement credential. Key top-level fields:

{
  "id": "urn:uuid:...",
  "version": "0.1",
  "type": ["VerifiableCredential", "DyadicSmartContract", "ConversationForAction"],

  "parties": {
    "customer":  { "did": "did:key:...", "name": "..." },
    "performer": { "did": "did:key:...", "name": "..." }
  },

  "preconditions": {
    "shared_background_of_understanding": {
      "status": "developing | confirmed | absent",
      "attestation": { ... }
    },
    "conditions_of_satisfaction": {
      "met": true,
      "description": "...",
      "deadline": "ISO date",
      "attestation": { ... }
    }
  },

  "payment_spec": {
    "total_value_usd": 100,
    "fiat_min_usd": 50,
    "time_max_pct": 25,
    "fiat_per_hour": 25,
    "gift": { "type": "unconditional", "amount_usd": 25,
               "direction": "performer_to_customer" }
  },

  "cfa_state": {
    "current": "negotiating",
    "history": [
      { "state": "...", "actor": "did:key:...",
        "timestamp": "...", "move": "...", "signature": "..." }
    ]
  },

  "settlement": null,        ← null until satisfied; then holds three-currency breakdown

  "context_refs": [],        ← links to prior CfT/CfP artifacts (FedWiki page slugs)

  "proof": {
    "type": "Ed25519Signature2020",
    "signatories": [ ... ]   ← both party signatures at contract formation
  }
}

When the contract reaches satisfied, the settlement field is populated with the actual amounts paid in each currency, both parties sign the settlement, and the document becomes a settlement credential — a permanent record of what was done and how it was paid.

Workflow: Creating and Executing a Contract

  1. Prior conversation establishes CCS+T. Before the CfA-dSC is created, a Conversation for Possibility or a preparatory meeting must establish the Conditions of Satisfaction and the deadline. This is not recorded in the contract itself — it is referenced via a context_refs link to a FedWiki page that captures the prior conversation.
  2. Customer opens the contract creator. Open ~/rcn/tools/cfa-dsc-creator.html in a browser. Enter both party names and DIDs, the payment specification, the CCS+T description, and the SBU status. The tool produces a contract JSON with a base64-encoded share link.
  3. Share link sent to performer. The share link encodes the full draft contract. The performer opens it in their own browser — no server needed. They can review the terms, propose changes (which the tool encodes as a new share link), or accept.
  4. Coordinator runs the signing script. Once both parties agree on terms, the coordinator runs node ~/veramo-rcn/sign-contract.js with each party's key to replace [pending: sign with Veramo] placeholders with real Ed25519 signature values.
  5. Contract stored in Neo4j. The finalize endpoint creates a Contract node and two Party nodes in Neo4j, linked by CUSTOMER_IN and PERFORMER_IN relationships. The full contract JSON is stored as a node property.
  6. FedWiki pages created. Two FedWiki pages are generated — one for each party's view of the contract. Each page shows the full state history, payment terms, and CCS+T. The pages cross-reference each other.
  7. Work is performed. As moves occur (performer starts, customer raises a concern, parties renegotiate), each is recorded as a signed history entry and appended to the contract document.
  8. Customer declares satisfaction. When the work is complete and the customer is satisfied, they make the declare_satisfaction move. The settlement field is populated with the agreed currency breakdown. Both parties sign the settlement.
  9. FedWiki pages updated. Both party FedWiki pages are updated to show the completed status and settlement record. The contract is now a settlement credential — a verifiable record of commitment made, work done, and payment settled.

What Is Not Yet Built

cfa-dsc-schema.json and cfa-dsc-validator/ not on disk. The formal JSON schema and the TypeScript validation library (with 25 passing tests) were built in a prior session but are not currently present in ~/rcn. They need to be reconstructed or relocated and committed. The schema is fully documented in cfa-dsc-handoff.md and can be written directly from it.
Verification script (stated next step). A CLI tool that takes a signed contract JSON, extracts the signing payload for each history entry, and verifies each Ed25519 signature against its DID's public key. The signing infrastructure is complete; verification is the mirror. This was the stated next step in the April handoff.
Move nodes in Neo4j. The full audit trail of CfA state transitions is currently stored only in the contract JSON's history array (and in FedWiki pages). A planned (:Move) node type would persist each speech act as a graph node, enabling temporal queries across contract lifecycles.
Hosting cfa-dsc-creator.html publicly. The share link model requires both parties to be able to open the same URL. Currently both parties need the HTML file locally. Hosting it on relocalizecreativity.net would make share links work without coordination.
DID registry for party lookup. The contract creator currently requires both parties to paste their DID strings manually. A DID registry in Neo4j or FedWiki would allow name lookup — type a name, get the DID.
CHW alias reconciliation. The performer DID z6MkjUQMCEXs71me... was created with alias "CHW Bellingham" but the contract creator UI used "Community Health Worker A". The signature is cryptographically valid (tied to the key, not the alias) but the registry alias should be reconciled.

Connection to SODOTO

CfA-dSC and SODOTO are designed to interlock at several points. They share infrastructure; they also inform each other conceptually.

Credentials authorize contract roles. A SODOTO credential for e-VSM Site Manager, for example, establishes that someone is qualified to perform e-VSM engagements as a professional service. A CfA-dSC contract for that engagement would reference the credential as part of the performer's qualification. Credential → role authorization → contract.
TeachOne creates a gift relationship. When a SODOTO TeachOne completes, the student has received something of real value from the mentor/learner. The three-currency model can record this: a gift flowing from learner to student (knowledge) and a reciprocal debt flowing from student back (time, future teaching). The Neo4j graph can hold this relationship permanently.
Shared DID infrastructure. Both systems use the same Veramo agent and the same Ed25519 key pairs. A person's DID in SODOTO is the same DID they use to sign CfA-dSC contract moves. The dids.json and people.json registries serve both systems.
Both surface in FedWiki. SODOTO credentials appear as badge pages; CfA-dSC contracts appear as party-view contract pages. Both use the same FedWiki server. A practitioner's FedWiki presence can eventually show both their credential portfolio and their contract history in one place.

For the full picture of how these systems relate to each other and to the rest of the RCN tool stack, see the RCN Stack Overview.
