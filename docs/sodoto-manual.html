SODOTO

See One, Do One, Teach One — a credentialing system that recognizes your skills, rewards your teaching, and grows the network. Every skill you earn is yours. Every skill you teach makes the whole cooperative more capable.

What SODOTO Is

SODOTO stands for See One, Do One, Teach One — a phrase from medical training that describes how skills have always been passed on between practitioners. You watch an expert do it. You do it yourself under supervision. You teach it to someone else. At each step you are accountable — not just to a test, but to a real person who witnessed what you did.

In My Personal Health Supporter, SODOTO is the system that turns that process into a formal credential — a verifiable digital badge that proves you have a skill, how you learned it, and whether you have taught it to others. The credential is yours to keep and to share. It belongs to no institution. It travels with you wherever you work.

SODOTO credentials are tied directly to compensation tiers in the cooperative. Every new credential you earn moves you to a higher pay level. Every skill you teach earns a master credential — the highest tier. The economics of the cooperative are designed so that teaching others is the most valued contribution you can make.

You do not need to be an expert to start. Every credential begins with See One — watching someone who already has the skill. That is available to you from day one. The path from newcomer to master teacher is a series of small, achievable steps, each one witnessed and recognized.

The Three Gates

Every SODOTO credential passes through three gates in sequence. You cannot skip a gate. Each gate requires a real person — a mentor who already holds the credential — to witness what you did and confirm it.

Gate 1

See One

Watch a mentor — someone who already holds this credential — use the skill in a real situation. Not a demonstration for your benefit alone, but actual work with an actual person. You observe closely and document what you understood about what you saw.

Gate 2

Do One

Perform the skill yourself, with your mentor present to watch and evaluate. This is real work — a real health picture review, a real Health Choices session, a real physician package. Your mentor confirms that you did it well. If not, you do it again — there is no penalty for more than one attempt.

Gate 3

Teach One

Guide a newer health partner through their own See One and Do One. Your mentor watches you teach. The newer partner's completed work is the evidence that you actually taught — you cannot claim this gate without a real student who did the skill.

Multiple attempts are not failure. If your mentor is not satisfied at Do One, the attempt is recorded honestly and you try again. The record of trying more than once is evidence that you take the standard seriously — not evidence that you are not capable. There is no limit on attempts.

The People in a SODOTO Credential

Every credential names three people: the learner, the mentor who witnessed them, and — at the Teach One gate — the student they taught. This chain of witness is what makes the credential meaningful. A certificate that says "passed a test" is easy to fake. A credential that names who showed you, who watched you, and who you taught — and where those people's own credentials can be verified — is a different kind of proof.

Role What they do Who they are
Learner Goes through all three gates. Does the skill at Do One. Teaches a student at Teach One. You, working toward a new credential
Mentor Demonstrates the skill at See One. Watches and evaluates at Do One and Teach One. Confirms at each gate that the standard was met. A health partner who already holds this credential, or a physician partner for clinical skills
Student The person you teach at Teach One. They complete their own Do One session as the evidence of your teaching. A newer health partner beginning their own path to this credential

My PHS Skill List

The following skills have SODOTO credentials available for My Personal Health Supporter health partners. Foundation skills are where everyone begins. Advanced skills require completing related foundation skills first. Master credentials require completing the Teach One gate for a skill.

Foundation Skills — start here
  • Health Picture Review Walk through a person's complete Health Picture with them at home. Read each section, identify what has changed, add health partner observations, and document the session in the Shared Care Plan.
  • Shared Care Plan Note-Writing Write a complete, dated, factual care plan note after a home visit — observations, what was discussed, what was decided, and what happens next.
  • Vitals Recording Measure and record blood pressure, heart rate, weight, and blood glucose accurately. Document conditions and compare to prior readings. Identify readings that require follow-up.
  • Appointment Preparation Use the Appointment Prep chat tool with a person before a medical encounter. Help them identify topics, formulate questions, and record the Pre-Visit Summary.
Advanced Skills — after completing relevant foundation skills
  • Gap Identification and Flagging Identify diagnoses in the Health Picture that lack supporting evidence, flag them accurately in health partner observations, and prepare a gap report for the physician package. Requires: Health Picture Review.
  • Health Choices Facilitation Open and walk through a My Health Choices option set with a person. Explain the dot display. Stay neutral. Document the person's decision and their reasoning. Requires: Health Picture Review.
  • Physician Package Preparation Prepare a complete, structured physician package — specific problem, supporting evidence, gaps, health partner observations, and one clear question. Send through the appropriate channel and record the response. Requires: Health Picture Review, Gap Identification.
  • Not Pursuing — Documentation Support a person who is choosing to set aside a health problem. Ensure they understand the implications, document their decision accurately in the Health Picture and Shared Care Plan, and handle the conversation with respect and clarity. Requires: Health Picture Review.
Master Credentials — awarded at Teach One completion
  • Master — [any skill above] A Master credential is awarded for any skill when you complete Teach One — when you have successfully guided a newer health partner through their own See One and Do One for that skill, with your mentor present to confirm your teaching. Master credentials represent the highest pay tier for that skill and are the foundation for becoming a mentor yourself.
  • Health Choices Authoring Create a new My Health Choices option set — from identifying the health situation and locating evidence, through authoring the dot displays, to peer review and publication. This is a standalone advanced skill with its own three-gate path. See the Authoring Guide for details.

Your Path — Step by Step

  1. Choose your first skill. Start with Health Picture Review. It is the foundation of everything else and the skill you will use most. Talk to your team leader or NDC Coordinator about who holds this credential and can serve as your mentor.
  2. Arrange your See One. Ask your mentor to let you observe them doing a real Health Picture review with a person they work with. Bring this manual. Observe carefully — you will be asked to document what you understood about what you saw.
  3. Document your See One. Immediately after observing, write a brief narrative on your FedWiki portfolio page: what the skill involved, what you saw the mentor do, and what you now understand about how to do it. Your mentor reviews this and adds their confirmation.
  4. Arrange your Do One. Schedule a home visit where your mentor will observe you doing a full Health Picture review. Choose a person in your neighborhood who you know well and who is comfortable with a second person present. Tell the person who your mentor is and why they are there.
  5. Do the skill. Conduct a complete Health Picture review as described in the My Health Picture guide. Your mentor observes without intervening. Afterward, your mentor gives you feedback and either confirms the gate or schedules another attempt.
  6. Document your Do One. Write the narrative of what you did — the visit, the approach, what you noticed, what you would do differently. Your mentor adds their assessment. The NDC Coordinator records the gate.
  7. Arrange your Teach One. Find a newer health partner who wants to earn this credential. You become their mentor for the See One and Do One. Your own mentor observes you teaching. The student's completed Do One is the evidence for your gate.
  8. Receive your credential. Once all three gates are confirmed, the NDC Coordinator signs the credential and it appears on your portfolio. You are now eligible to serve as a mentor for this skill for others.

Becoming a Mentor

Once you hold a Practitioner credential — completing all three gates including Teach One — you are eligible to serve as a mentor for that skill. Being a mentor is one of the most valued roles in the cooperative. Every health partner who earns a new skill because of your mentoring strengthens the whole network.

What mentors do

  • Demonstrate the skill in real working conditions for learners at See One
  • Watch and evaluate learners at Do One — with honest, specific, constructive feedback
  • Watch and evaluate learners who are teaching at Teach One
  • Confirm each gate in writing, with their name attached
  • Keep their own skills current — a mentor whose skills have lapsed cannot credibly witness others

The mentor standard

When you confirm a gate, you are staking your own credential and your reputation on the assessment that the learner met the standard. Confirm only what you genuinely observed. If a learner did not meet the standard, record it honestly and schedule another attempt. A mentor who passes learners who are not ready harms the person those learners will care for.

Example — Elena's path to master credential

Elena joined the cooperative as a new health partner in February. Her first credential was Health Picture Review, with James as her mentor. She observed James do a full review with one of his enrolled households in week two. She did her own review in week three with James present — he asked her to come back for a second attempt after the first, because she had missed reviewing the medications section. Her second attempt was complete and James confirmed it.

In May, a newer partner named Rosa joined the team. Elena offered to be Rosa's mentor for Health Picture Review. Over three weeks, Elena demonstrated the review for Rosa, watched Rosa complete her own, and gave feedback that helped Rosa pass. Elena's own mentor observed the whole teaching sequence. Elena's Master credential for Health Picture Review was signed in late May.

Elena now sits at the highest pay tier for Health Picture Review. She is working toward her Advanced credential in Physician Package Preparation — she just completed her See One with James last week.

Credentials and Compensation

SODOTO credentials are directly linked to compensation tiers in the Whatcom Wealth and Health cooperative. The cooperative's compensation structure recognizes that a health partner with more skills can take better care of more people — and that a mentor who grows the skills of the whole team is contributing at a level that deserves higher recognition.

Credential level Gates completed Compensation
Foundation Practitioner See One + Do One (any foundation skill) Base pay tier — eligible to work independently with enrolled households
Advanced Practitioner See One + Do One (any advanced skill) Intermediate pay tier — eligible for more complex case assignments
Master All three gates including Teach One Highest pay tier for that skill — eligible to serve as mentor

Credentials accumulate. Each new skill you earn, at any level, adds to your compensation. The cooperative publishes its current compensation tiers; your NDC Coordinator can show you exactly how your current credential set translates to your current pay level and what is within reach.

The design behind this: A cooperative that pays more for teaching creates a fundamentally different environment than one that pays only for individual performance. When your income grows by making others more capable, you have a direct personal stake in the success of everyone around you. That is the design — and it is deliberate.

Questions About Your Credentials

  • Can I lose a credential? No. A credential, once issued, is yours. It cannot be revoked by the cooperative after the fact. If your skills lapse or you stop working in the field, your credentials remain on your portfolio — they simply reflect when they were earned.
  • What if my mentor leaves the cooperative? Your credential stands. It was signed by the NDC — the organization — not by the individual mentor. The mentor's name is in the record as a witness, but their departure does not affect the validity of what they witnessed.
  • Can I use my credentials outside the cooperative? Yes. Your FedWiki portfolio is publicly verifiable. Any employer, care organization, or cooperative that understands the SODOTO system can read and verify your credentials. You own your portfolio.
  • What if I disagree with a mentor's assessment? Talk to your NDC Coordinator. There is a review process. A single mentor's opinion is not final — the gate can be re-evaluated by a different mentor if there is a genuine dispute.
