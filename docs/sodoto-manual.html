SODOTO in One Minute

SODOTO stands for See One, Do One, Teach One — a phrase from medical training that describes how skills have always been passed between practitioners. You watch an expert do it. You do it yourself under supervision. You teach it to someone else. At each step you are accountable to a real person who witnessed what you did, not just to a test.

SODOTO turns that process into a verifiable digital credential — a badge that records that you have a skill, how you learned it, who witnessed you, and whether you have taught it on. The badge is cryptographically signed by your cooperative (the NDC). It is yours to keep and to share, it belongs to no institution, and anyone can verify it in a browser with no login. It travels with you wherever you work.

You do not need to be an expert to start. Every credential begins with See One — watching someone who already holds the skill. That is available to you from day one. The path from newcomer to teacher is a series of small, witnessed steps.
SODOTO is not tied to any one field. Anything that requires training can use it — a systems-mapping tool, a facilitation method, a trade, a clinical practice. Wherever this manual uses an example skill, the same three gates apply to your skill.

The Three Gates

Every credential passes through three gates in order. You cannot skip a gate. Each gate needs a real mentor — someone who already holds the credential — to witness what you did and confirm it.

Gate 1

See One

Watch a mentor use the skill in a real situation — actual work, not a demonstration staged for you. Observe closely and write down what you understood about what you saw.

Gate 2

Do One

Perform the skill yourself with your mentor watching. This is real work. Your mentor confirms you did it well — or, if not, you simply do it again. No penalty for more than one attempt.

Gate 3

Teach One

Guide a newer person through their own See One and Do One while your mentor watches you teach. That person's completed work is the evidence that you actually taught.

Multiple attempts are not failure. If your mentor is not satisfied at Do One, the attempt is recorded honestly and you try again. A record of trying more than once is evidence that you take the standard seriously — there is no limit on attempts.

Who's Who

Every credential names the people involved. This chain of witness is what makes it meaningful: a certificate that says "passed a test" is easy to fake; a credential that names who showed you, who watched you, and who you taught — each of them verifiable in turn — is a different kind of proof.

If You're a Learner — Your Path Step by Step

These steps use Causal Loop Diagramming as a stand-in skill. Substitute whatever skill you are actually earning — the sequence is identical.

  1. Choose a skill and find a mentor. Ask your coordinator or team who holds this credential and can witness you. That person is your mentor for all three gates.
  2. Arrange your See One. Ask to observe your mentor using the skill in real work — not a demo staged for you. Watch what they actually do and why.
  3. Document your See One. Right afterward, write a short narrative on a FedWiki page: what the skill involved, what you saw the mentor do, and what you now understand. Your mentor reviews it and confirms.
  4. Arrange your Do One. Schedule real work where your mentor can watch you do the skill yourself. Pick a genuine task, not a rehearsal.
  5. Do the skill. Your mentor observes without taking over. Afterward they give feedback and either confirm the gate or schedule another attempt — both are fine.
  6. Document your Do One. Write what you did, what you noticed, what you would change. Your mentor adds their assessment; the coordinator records the gate.
  7. Arrange your Teach One. Find a newer person who wants this credential and become their mentor for See One and Do One. Your own mentor watches you teach. The student's completed Do One is your evidence.
  8. Receive your credential. Once all three gates are confirmed, the coordinator signs the badge and it appears on your portfolio. You are now eligible to mentor others in this skill.
Worked example

Dana wanted the Causal Loop Diagramming credential, with Ken — who already holds it — as mentor. In week two Dana watched Ken build a real CLD with a working group (See One) and wrote up what they understood. In week three Dana built their own diagram with a different group while Ken observed (Do One). Ken asked for a second attempt — the first had reversed a feedback polarity — and the second was clean, so Ken confirmed it.

A month later a newcomer, Priya, wanted the same skill. Dana mentored Priya's See One and Do One while Ken watched Dana teach (Teach One). Priya's finished diagram is the evidence that Dana taught. The coordinator signed Dana's full credential, and Dana can now mentor this skill for others.

If You're a Mentor

Once you hold a credential — all three gates, including Teach One — you can mentor that skill for others. Being a mentor is one of the most valuable roles in a cooperative: every person who gains a skill because of your mentoring strengthens the whole network.

What mentors do

  • Demonstrate the skill in real working conditions for learners at See One.
  • Watch and evaluate learners at Do One — with honest, specific feedback.
  • Watch and evaluate learners who are teaching at Teach One.
  • Confirm each gate in writing, with your name attached.
  • Keep your own skills current — a mentor whose skills have lapsed cannot credibly witness others.

The mentor standard

When you confirm a gate, you stake your own credential and reputation on the judgment that the learner met the standard. Confirm only what you genuinely observed. If a learner did not meet it, record that honestly and schedule another attempt. A mentor who passes learners who are not ready weakens every credential downstream.

If You're a Coordinator

The coordinator records gates and signs badges for the NDC using the browser-based issuer tool. Everything happens in your browser — no scripts, no git. The Issuer Tool Guide has per-tab detail; here is the whole loop.

  1. Open the tool at sodoto.ndcgroup.relocalizecreativity.net/tools/sodoto-issuer.html. On first use it asks for the write passphrase, kept only in your browser.
  2. Registry tab — confirm the learner is listed. If not, add their name, DID (Decentralized Identifier), FedWiki site, and portfolio slug.
  3. Contract tab — pick the issuing NDC, the skill, and the learner, then Open / Resume. The tool tracks gate state per issuer–skill–learner.
  4. Gate tabs — for each gate, enter the date, mentor, both assertions, outcome, and narrative slug, then Record Attempt & Write to FedWiki. The badge-in-progress updates on the learner's and mentor's portfolios immediately.
  5. Sign tab — review the preview, choose Full (all gates complete) or Partial, enter the NDC's 64-character hex key seed, and Sign & Issue. Signing happens in your browser; the key is cleared right after and never sent to the server.
  6. Verify — open the learner's portfolio and click Verify on the new badge. Confirm ✓ Verified. A failure means the key seed did not match the NDC's DID.
Guard the NDC key seed. The 64-character hex seed is what signs on behalf of the cooperative. Keep it in a password manager or Keychain — the tool never stores it. If it is lost the NDC can no longer sign new credentials under that DID, but every credential already issued stays valid forever (it verifies from the public key inside the DID).

Each badge shows the skill and the issuing NDC's seal, the three gate dates, the mentor's name (linked to their own portfolio), the full attempt history for each gate, and a Verify button. Long badges collapse into an accordion — click a heading to expand it.

To verify a badge, click Verify. The check runs entirely in your browser: it reads the signed credential, recovers the issuer's public key straight from its DID, and confirms the signature. ✓ Verified means the badge is authentic and unaltered. No account, no server call, no third party — and it works permanently, for anyone, anywhere.

Optional Add-ons (Not Part of SODOTO)

Some cooperatives layer other systems on top of SODOTO. These are separate and optional — SODOTO works fully without any of them.

  • Pay tiers or roles. A cooperative may decide that holding certain credentials moves someone to a higher pay tier or unlocks a role. That is a cooperative policy choice attached to the credential — not something SODOTO defines or requires.
  • Agreements & value exchange. A separate system (CfA-dSC) lets people make agreements and exchange value with a three-currency settlement. Anyone exchanging value can use it, and because it shares the same DID identity a cooperative can connect it to SODOTO — for instance recording a Teach One as a gift of value — but it stands on its own and is not needed to earn or verify a credential.

Questions About Your Credentials

  • Can I lose a credential? No. Once issued it is yours and cannot be revoked after the fact. If your skills lapse, the credential simply reflects when it was earned.
  • What if my mentor leaves? Your credential stands. It was signed by the NDC — the organization — not by the individual mentor. Their name remains in the record as a witness.
  • Can I use my credentials outside my cooperative? Yes. Your portfolio is publicly verifiable. Any organization that understands SODOTO can read and verify it. You own your portfolio.
  • What if I disagree with a mentor's assessment? Talk to your coordinator. A single mentor's opinion is not final — a gate can be re-evaluated by a different mentor if there is a genuine dispute.
  • Do I need any special software? No. Everything happens on the web — the issuer tool and your portfolio both run in an ordinary browser over HTTPS. Nothing needs to be installed, and nothing runs on any one person's machine.
