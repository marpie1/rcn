# Check Every Sentence

Step 7, a gate. Put every sentence of the draft through the Agreement Checker before anyone signs. Gaps go back to whichever step wrote the sentence.

## What the check looks for

- Things nobody created. A rule about "gardeners" with nothing saying who counts as one. A rule about "the tank" with nothing saying which tank.
- Rules with no "or else". Without one, a sentence is a hope, and people will drift from it.
- Triggers nobody reads. "When the tank is below half" assumes someone reads the tank.
- Penalties nobody carries out. Who applies the "or else", and who do they answer to?
- The eight things long-lasting groups do: which are present, partial, missing, or can't be told from the text.

## At Elm Street

The first draft said "no sprinklers when the tank is below half" but gave nobody the job of reading the tank. The check sent it back to step 6, which added the keeper's Monday reading.

## In the RCN Constitution

A first reading, not yet a full run of the Checker. Who's in is answered, and so is how the founding rules change. How everyday decisions are made is not: "Decisions are made ad hoc" (Article III §3). Most of the house rules are expectations and habits rather than rules with an "or else", which the Constitution itself says (Article III §5). The ethical veto has its conditions spelled out in the Ethical Oversight Framework rather than in the Constitution. Running the Checker on the full text is the next step.

## Ostrom's words

The checker codes each sentence with the Institutional Grammar (ADICO and IG 2.0): attribute, deontic, aim, object, conditions, or else; and creating sentences (constitutive statements). The profile is by design principle.
