# Start The House Rules Small

Step 6. Write only the everyday rules you need now. Add the rest when a real problem shows up, and check each one as it goes in.

## The everyday rules a group usually needs, in time

- Taking and giving: who may take what, and what each person puts back, in fair proportion.
- Keeping an eye out: on each other and on the shared thing, by people who answer to the group.
- A word first, then step up: small consequences first, bigger for repeats.
- Settling it close to home: a cheap, quick place to take an argument, including an argument about the rules.
- Who gets told what, and when.

## What gets written

A short page of house rules, each one sentence. Each new rule is added when a real problem calls for it, and says when it applies, who it's for, must or may or must not, what, and or else what.

## At Elm Street

Water on your assigned days. No sprinklers when the tank is below half; the keeper reads the tank every Monday and posts the level on the gate. Four work hours a season per plot. A word from the keeper first; a second time, a week without water. Arguments go to the keeper, then to the spring meeting.

## In the RCN Constitution

Article III §2 to §5 and Article IV: everyone shares innovations and challenges; lightweight facilitation in Thursday meetings, where everyone can be heard; communication by WhatsApp, Zoom and FedWiki. Article III §5 says it honestly: "Our operating norms are largely implicit." That fits a small network that mostly needs coordination. An NDC holding land or money will need more of these written down.

## Ostrom's words

House rules: collective-choice and operational rules. The list follows design principles 2B (proportional costs and benefits), 4A and 4B (monitoring), 5 (graduated sanctions) and 6 (conflict resolution), plus information rules.
