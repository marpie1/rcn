# Seven Points Of View

The ethical check in practice, from the RCN Ethical Oversight Framework, after Mick Ashby's Ethical Regulator Theorem. Seven standpoints from which the group's actions look different. Together they give the watcher enough ways of seeing.

## The seven

- Future Generations: what does this foreclose or open for people not yet born?
- Adjacent Communities: who is affected but not at the table?
- The Living Environment: is this place more or less alive because of what we are doing?
- The Marginalized Insider: who among us has the least voice, and whose needs are assumed rather than asked?
- The Informed Outsider: what can't we see about ourselves?
- The Historical Record: what has happened before when people did this?
- The Systemic View: what does this do to the whole?

## How it runs

About five people hold each point of view, at least three, and each person sits in at least two, so the whole stays visible in each part. Groups set their own rhythm and meet together at a regular convergence.

They produce three things. Warnings, before a line is crossed: the system working. Vetoes, when a line will be or has been crossed: rare, real, naming what would lift them. Assessments: dated, so they fade and must be renewed. All are written plainly, signed by the group rather than by individuals, and posted on FedWiki. Whoever a warning is about has two weeks to answer in writing; silence goes to the convergence.

## At Elm Street

Four points of view are enough to begin: the watershed (The Living Environment), the next block (Adjacent Communities), the waiting list (The Marginalized Insider), and the children who will garden later (Future Generations).
