# Founding A Commons Step By Step

How a group goes from a first conversation to working rules it wrote for itself, one step at a time. The picture below is the whole path. Click any box to open its page.

Founding moves down the layers of deciding. First people notice what they share and find each other. Then they say what they take for granted, write the founding rules, and build in an ethical check. Then they write the house rules, small. Everything passes two gates, a check of every sentence and an agreement by everyone at the table, before the work begins. Once the work is going, the dashed arrows climb back up whenever something isn't working.

## Two examples all the way through

The Elm Street garden is imagined: 30 plots, one rain-fed water tank, a water keeper, a spring meeting, land leased from the city. It keeps every step concrete.

The RCN Constitution is real. It was written with the [Constitution Builder](https://ndcgroup.relocalizecreativity.net/constitution-builder.html), which organizes a constitution by Donella Meadows' leverage points, and it carries an ethical safeguard taken from Mick Ashby's Ethical Regulator Theorem, made operational in the RCN Ethical Oversight Framework. The framework says it will be worked out in RCN first and then applied to NDC constitutions. Each step page shows what the RCN Constitution already does at that step, and what an NDC would still have to decide.

## Two lessons that shape the path

Start small. Ostrom found that rules that work grow piece by piece, fitted to their place. A complete set of rules written in one sitting is mostly rules on paper. So the path writes only three things at the start, who's in, how we decide, and how we change these rules, and grows the house rules as real problems show up.

Mind the founding puzzle. You can't vote on how to vote. The first rules can only be agreed by everyone at the first table, which makes who sits at that table the most important decision of all. That is why gathering the founders comes before any rule is written.
