# Write The Founding Rules

Step 4. The few rules that decide who sets all the other rules. Start with three, and add only what you need.

## The three rules to start with

- Who's in: who counts as a member, how you join, how you leave.
- How we decide: a vote, two-thirds, everyone agrees, one person. Say it out loud; most quarrels come from people assuming different ways of deciding.
- How we change these rules: who may propose, how it is decided, how often they are reviewed.

## Then, as needed

- What seats there are: roles, and how people come to hold them.
- Who outside has to recognize us: a lease, a legal form, a permit.
- What groups we belong to that are bigger than us.

## At Elm Street

Members are plot holders, and anyone on the waiting list may attend the meeting. The spring meeting decides by two-thirds of those present. These rules change only at the spring meeting, by the same two-thirds. The city leases the land year to year.

## In the RCN Constitution

Who's in: "All participation in the Network is voluntary. Every voice carries equal weight" (Article III §1), one class of member. How we change these rules: "a living document... reviewed annually and may be amended through consensus of active participants" (Article V §3). Who outside must recognize us: no one; RCN is an unincorporated network (Article II §1). How we decide everyday matters: "Decisions are made ad hoc" (Article III §3), which is the one founding rule the Constitution leaves open.

Also open, and noted on the RCN constitutional-arena drawing: who may teach, and what classes of member an NDC has. The Constitution governs RCN, not the enterprises it helps form.

## Ostrom's words

The founding rules: constitutional rules. Who's in: boundary rules. How we decide: aggregation rules. What seats: position rules. Outsiders recognizing us: recognition of rights (design principle 7). Groups within groups: nested enterprises (principle 8).
