# Build In The Ethical Check

Step 5. Give someone the standing to warn, and if needed to stop, when the group drifts toward harm. Only the founding rules can give that standing.

## Why it belongs in the founding rules

Mick Ashby's Ethical Regulator Theorem says every organization that pursues goals needs a watcher above it, with enough ways of seeing to notice ethical drift, and the authority to step in before harm is done. Personal ethics needs no permission: I can decline to act. Ethics beyond the personal means stopping somebody else, and that is standing only founding rules can grant. A watcher created by the house rules can be overruled by the house rules.

## The plain questions

- Who watches, from which points of view?
- What can they do: warn, stop, require an answer?
- How are warnings answered, and in what time?
- Who watches the watchers? (The members, by changing the founding rules.)

## At Elm Street

Once a season, a few gardeners each speak for someone not at the meeting: the watershed downstream, the neighbors on the next block, the newcomers on the waiting list, the children who will garden here later. They can warn the meeting, and they can stop a decision until it has answered them.

## In the RCN Constitution

The Preamble's Ethical Regulation section requires a superordinate ethical metasystem with authority over management, the variety to see drift, independence, the power to halt, redirect or constrain, and accountability. The RCN Ethical Oversight Framework makes it operational as [[Seven Points Of View]]. Warnings come first; vetoes are rare and real; everything is posted publicly. The framework says it will be worked out in RCN and then applied to NDC constitutions.

## Ostrom's words

Ostrom has no direct term for this; it sits at the constitutional level, as a seat with standing over every arena below. Ashby's terms: ethical regulator, requisite variety.
