# Live It Watch It Change It

Step 9. Do the work, keep track of how things really work, and take problems up to the layer that can fix them.

## Three habits

- Watch: keep simple records over time of the shared thing and of who did what, kept with people, not about them.
- Compare: how things really work against what was written. A rule nobody follows has gone stale; a habit everybody follows is a rule nobody wrote down.
- Take it up a layer: a house rule that isn't working goes to the group (step 6); a problem that keeps coming back goes to the founding rules (step 4).

## At Elm Street

A chart of the tank level hangs on the gate, read every Monday. In the second August the keeper notices the same three plots run dry every week; the spring meeting changes the watering days. In the third year the waiting list has no voice at the meeting at all; that goes to the founding rules, which add a seat for it.

## In the RCN Constitution

Weekly Thursday calls and daily check-ins between Kerry and Marc (Article IV §3); goals reviewed with the same tools RCN builds for neighborhoods (Article I §4); the eVSM to assess the health of each organization and the whole network (Article V §2); a yearly review of the Constitution itself (Article V §3); and the regular convergence of the [[Seven Points Of View]].

## Ostrom's words

Doing the work: the operational level. How things really work: rules-in-use. Keeping track over time: monitoring (design principle 4), in RCN's case with SPC charts.
