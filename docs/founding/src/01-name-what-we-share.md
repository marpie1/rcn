# Name What We Share

Step 1. Before anyone writes a rule, say plainly what is shared and what goes wrong with it.

## The plain questions

- What is the shared thing? What do people take from it or put into it?
- Does it run out when people use it? Can we keep people out?
- Who uses it now, and who is affected but doesn't use it?
- What goes wrong today, and when?

## What gets written

A short description everyone can agree is true, before anyone argues about what to do. The answers to the two questions (does it run out, can we keep people out) tell you the kind of trouble to expect: things taken too fast, or nobody pitching in.

## At Elm Street

One rain-fed tank, 30 plots, and in August not enough water for everyone. The water runs out, and it's hard to stop people watering, so the trouble is both taking too much and not putting back. Neighbors without plots are affected too: the garden is on their street.

## In the RCN Constitution

What RCN shares is mostly know-how and tools for neighborhoods, given away freely (Article II §1, Article IV §2: everything produced is open). Know-how doesn't run out and nobody is kept out, so the trouble is the other kind: getting enough people to contribute. RCN also shares something that does run out: time, attention, and the cost of the Zoom and FedWiki servers, which the Constitution says Marc covers (Article IV §2).

For an NDC: what does the neighborhood share? Land, a fund, a building, care, food? Each answer brings a different kind of trouble.

## Ostrom's words

The shared thing and the bits taken from it: resource system and resource units. Does it run out: subtractability. Can we keep people out: excludability. Something that runs out and is hard to fence off: a common-pool resource.
