# Gather The Founders

Step 2. Who sits at the first table? Before any rule exists, this decides whose voice shapes all the others.

## The plain questions

- Who is here now, and why?
- Who uses the shared thing but isn't here? Who is affected but isn't here?
- Who has the least voice, and how will they get to the table?
- How many of us is right to start? Enough to do the work, few enough to know each other.

## Why this comes first

You can't vote on how to vote. The first rules can only be agreed by everyone at the founding table, so whoever is missing from that table is missing from every rule that follows. A seat left out now is the hardest kind to add later.

## What gets written

A list of founders, and a plain note of who was invited and couldn't come, and who is still missing.

## At Elm Street

The neighbors who asked the city for the lot. Missing at first: the renters on the next block who would garden if they knew, and the family across the street whose kids play there.

## In the RCN Constitution

RCN is self-declared: "an unincorporated network of local activists" where each member and neighborhood decides for itself (Article II §1). "At present we are seven," and groups of 5 to 15 work well (Article II §2).

For an NDC: who are the founders, and who speaks for the people with least voice from the very first meeting? This is where a community health worker's seat is won or lost.

## Ostrom's words

Who is at the founding table: the first boundary decision, made before any written boundary rule. The people with least voice: in the RCN Ethical Oversight Framework, The Marginalized Insider.
