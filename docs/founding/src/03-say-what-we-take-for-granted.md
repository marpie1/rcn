# Say What We Take For Granted

Step 3. Write down the unwritten: what feels fair, who counts, what this is for. Once it's written, people can point at it and argue with it.

## The plain questions

- What do we believe is fair?
- What are we for, and what will we never do?
- Who counts? Who do we owe something to who isn't here (the land, the neighbors, people not yet born)?
- How far ahead are we looking?

## What gets written

A preamble: a page of plain beliefs and a purpose. It isn't a rule; nothing in it can be enforced. But every rule written later can be checked against it.

## At Elm Street

"Everyone who wants to grow food here should be able to. The water belongs to the garden, not to whoever gets there first. We leave the soil better than we found it."

## In the RCN Constitution

The Preamble states seven beliefs: the primacy of the local; the necessity of working together; a view of human nature (diversity is strength, participation is the antidote to isolation and domination); our relationship to the environment; our time horizon; our system boundaries; our theories of change. Then the commitment that matters most for everything after: "Fairness is our superordinate condition for action. Nothing proceeds at any level without checking for fairness." The Constitution Builder files this under Meadows' leverage points 1 and 2, paradigm and the ability to transcend it.

For an NDC: what does this neighborhood take for granted, and is any of it unfair to someone who isn't at the table yet?

## Ostrom's words

What we all take for granted: the metaconstitutional level. It is the strongest layer because it is unwritten, which is why writing some of it down matters.
