Above everything else

Models for Seeing Systems

This is a precondition, not a pathway — and it is the correction that matters most to my first draft. I had the tetrahedron as one diagnostic among four, sitting beside the others. It does not sit beside them. It sits above them, together with Beauty Good Truth Economy, and the claim attached is an ethical one: a model used without these will lead to no good.

Social Action Tetrahedron · POIA What humans taking action together are made of

Social System Tetrahedron · BGTE What makes the use of a model come to good

BGTE is not decorative and it is not a values statement bolted on the front. In Neighborhood Systems Patterns it feeds all six of the questions directly, and it feeds Stories, which feed Body-Mood-Language. It is upstream of the whole apparatus.

Practically, this means every method page carries a line most method documentation never has: what would make running this beautiful, good, true and economical — and what would make running it none of those. A method that passes its gates and fails BGTE has not succeeded.

The spine

They are the ordered chain in Neighborhood Systems Patterns (5→6→7→8→9→10: Time → Place → Paradigm → Politics → Design → Implementation). They are the first node of Another Sequence of Tools. The thin shelf, built out

WHO — collective action, after Ostrom

Ostrom's Collective Action model gives the Who shelf a structure the other shelves do not yet have. Thirty-one variables from decades of common-pool-resource research, and — crucially — an ordered spine of three stages.

The top-level sequence is Origin → Performance → Survival, and your colouring names what each stage is: green for forming, blue for managing, red for surviving. That is the shelf's organizing question, and it is sharper than “roles and agency”: the Who question is not one question but three, asked in order.

Green · forming Origin Will this collective action come into being at all? Driven by Perceived Alternative Strategies · Decision-making Costs · Interdependence and subtractability · Asset Structure · Resource Scarcity Collective Action Assessment · Graph Tool (CLD) · IAD · Six Questions
Blue · managing Performance Does it actually work once it exists? Driven by Enforcement Costs · Decision-making Costs · Dual Enforcement · Technology for Monitoring Resources · Speed of Access to Info Sofi-VSM · CfA-dSC Contract Creator · SODOTO · eVSM
Red · surviving Survival Will it persist when conditions change? Driven by Conflict Resolution · de facto vs. de jure · Small Simple Set of Rules · Larger System of Institutional Support · Rate of Change IBIS Map · Conversation Navigator · NRM Tripod Beta · RCN Timeline · SPC

Two chains are drawn heavy, and they are the leverage

Three edges in your diagram carry extra weight, and two of them form a single chain:

Governmental Agencies → Legal Status and Local Prerogative → Perceived Alternative Strategies → Origin. What government grants by way of legal standing and local prerogative determines which strategies a neighbourhood can even perceive as available — and that determines whether the collective action originates at all. This is the highest-leverage claim in the whole model for RCN's purposes, and it says something uncomfortable: a great deal of neighbourhood capacity is foreclosed upstream, before anyone in the neighbourhood makes a choice.

The third heavy edge is Conflict Resolution → Survival. Not performance, not origin — survival. Groups do not usually die of poor execution; they die of unresolved conflict.

Common Understanding is the hub

Counting edges, Common Understanding is the highest-degree node in the model — seven in, three out — and it sits in a mutually reinforcing pair with Trust, each feeding the other. It also feeds Conflict Resolution, which feeds back into it.

That matters for tool selection more than anything else on this shelf. Nearly every RCN instrument that produces shared pictures — Campfire Conversation, Cave Drawing, the Graph Tool, MORE Outliner, FedWiki itself — is acting on the single most connected variable in Ostrom's model. The tools were not chosen with this model in mind, and they land on its hub anyway.

It also means this model is exactly what a Vester run is for. Thirty-one variables with a dense feedback structure and no numbers is the Sensitivity Model's native input, and it would give you systemic roles — which variables are active, reactive, critical, buffering — rather than the eyeballed degree count above. Your own note on the page says it: it would be nice to have this model in Vensim. The Graph Tool's CLD mode plus a Vester pass gets you closer, sooner.

Why three tools overlap on this shelf

Your note explains what would otherwise look like redundancy: Jon Walker correlated the VSM with Ostrom's work, and you correlated Sofi with the VSM. Ostrom, VSM and Sofi are in tight concordance — so Sofi-VSM on the Who shelf is not a competing framework, it is the same structure seen through an organizational lens. A neighbourhood can enter at whichever of the three is handiest.

The two layers

Models are particular; patterns are general

The approach to modeling in ReLocalize Creativity is to reveal rich perspectives and possibilities (leverage points) in particular systems — NOT to create generalized models. Our generalizations will be published as Patterns.

Models for Seeing Systems

This is the cleanest statement of the whole architecture and it arrived independently of anything we designed last week. It draws a hard line:

  • A model is a particular. This neighbourhood, this hospital's pricing, this coop's finances — drawn to reveal leverage in that system, and not expected to travel.
  • A pattern is the generalization, abstracted from particulars and published so someone else can use it.

Which settles a question the toolshelf plan left slightly loose. The example files sitting in tools/ — whatcom-homelessness-cld.json, rba-hospital-pricing.rcn.json, the pomr-*.json set — are models. The method pages are patterns. An example is not decoration on a method page; it is the particular the general was abstracted from, and the link between them should be explicit in both directions.

It also names the act that connects the two layers. Step 16 of the 15 Ps — Posterity, Pattern Language, the one you colour orange for danger — is the move from the left column to the right. That is not a coda on the production pathway. It is the mechanism by which the shelf grows.

Whenever we get mixed up and confused using our more sophisticated tools and methods, then we should go back and “diagram our sentences” in system dynamics, especially the primary language of causal loop diagramming.

Causal Models

This is a third schema element, and it is not a gate. A gate blocks forward motion until a condition holds. This is a recovery — a return path available from anywhere, at any depth, when the group has lost the thread. Every method should declare one, and for RCN the default is the same: drop back to a causal loop diagram and diagram your sentences.

The reason it works is on the ladder above. The sophisticated tools are built up from the grammar of system dynamics, so returning to CLD is not starting over — it is returning to the language the other tools are written in.

One more line from that page earns a place in Pathway 3: designed experiences and experiments can be seen as a way for humans to listen to systems as they communicate back to us. That is the justification for running an experiment rather than a simulation, and it is a better sentence than any I would write for it.

The distinction that matters

Four kinds of sequence, not one

The sources contain sequences of four genuinely different kinds. Flattening them into a single list of “methods” is the biggest error available here, because they are used at different moments and answer to different tests.

Ontological — what is true of ethical collective action

Not performed. A claim about how persons, ideals, organizations and actions necessarily relate, and about what makes their relating come to good. You check yourself against it and find which relation has failed.

Social Action Tetrahedron · BGTE · Optimum Sequence · AIC

Diagnostic — what to do when you are stuck

A routing procedure. Takes an unclear situation and returns a type, a scale, and a decision about which production sequence to enter. The kind people most need and the kind least often written down.

Systemic Problem Solving · Cynefin · Six Nested Questions · Six Thinking Hats · DSRP

Production — getting a thing built, delivered and taught

Ordered work with resources, handoffs and deliverables. These look like conventional method documentation and are the least transferable — each is fitted to a domain.

15 Ps · Another Sequence of Tools · Vester SensiMod · System Discovery Process

Curricular — the order in which a person learns the skills

The fourth kind, and the one I missed in the first draft. A learning progression is not a work progression: CLD → Stock Flow → System Dynamics → Vester is how you acquire the practice, and nobody would run a project in that order. This is the kind SODOTO actually certifies.

The six pathways already in the SODOTO Skills Registry · Systems Curriculum's four levels

All four are patterns in Alexander's sense, at different scales, connected through COMPLETED_BY. The diagnostic pathway selects a production pathway. The curricular pathways say who is qualified to run either. The ontological frame is what all of it is checked against.

Consequences

What this changes in the plan

The shelves are the Six Questions, under the crown

Replacing the eight I proposed. A tool may sit on several shelves, so shelving is a catalog property rather than a folder. Above the shelves sit the two governing models, which are not a shelf — they are the condition on using anything from any shelf.

methods.json gains three fields

question on every step, because the sequence is a progression of question types and the tool is the instrument, not the point. A step without a question is a tool instruction, and tool instructions belong in a manual.

gate on steps that have one — what must be true before this step is legitimate. Four appear above: the heuristics must have failed; the stakeholders must be in the room; no program without a promise; the numbers gate before simulation.

fallback on the method — where you go when lost. Not a gate, a recovery, available from any depth. For most RCN methods the answer is the same and it comes from you: drop back to a causal loop diagram and diagram your sentences.

Two status axes, not one

Confidence (does it hold?) from method attestations, and Transmissibility (can it be taught?) from the registry's Active / Ready / Candidate. Orthogonal, both needed, and the second one you had five months before I proposed the first.

A third relation between methods

ROUTES_TO, alongside HANDS_OFF_TO and COMPLETED_BY — a conditional branch where a diagnostic outcome selects the next pathway. Cynefin's typing is exactly this, and it is not containment.

Three files, one relation each

The SODOTO Skills Registry holds skills — what a person can be certified in. methods.json holds pathways — ordered uses of tools. catalog.json holds tools — what you can launch. A method names the skills it requires; skills are certified; tools are opened. The registry is the most developed of the three and should seed the others, not be replaced by them.

And on organizing the files

Tools stay flat in tools/ for the file:// reasons already established. But the two-layer split gives the rest of it an obvious shape: methods/ at the repo root, as first-class as tools/, because methods are the other half of the offer and burying them under docs/ would say the opposite; and models/ for the particulars now scattered through tools/ as loose .rcn.json files, each linked to the pattern it was abstracted into. Patterns generalize models — the directory layout should be able to say which.
