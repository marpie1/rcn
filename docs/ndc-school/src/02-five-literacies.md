# Five Literacies

The invariants. Five things every graduate can do, each anchored in a place you can walk to. They are the exterior curriculum, and they pair with the interior one in [[Ferndale Ideal Education Model]].

## Read the land

Why is the copper here. Apache Leap Tuff, the vein, the perlite still being dug, Picketpost, the Superstitions. Geology is time made visible, and the shafts are the only place most people will ever see thousands of feet of it at once. Mathematics comes out of this rather than being applied to it: scale, volume, the block-cave subsidence geometry, the three-dimensional problem of an ore body nobody can see. Boyce Thompson Arboretum is the living half — Sonoran upland ecology, drought adaptation, propagation, and the founding irony that the mine's founder built the garden.

## Read the water

Where does the tap water come from. Queen Creek, the wells, the aquifer, monsoon against drought, what Resolution proposes to draw and where the tailings go. Arizona water law is a full civics course by itself. This is also where measurement lives: the students keep the creek record, the rain gauge and the well levels, year over year, and learn what a control chart shows that a single number hides. A town that measures its own water is a different town.

## Read the ledger

Extraction economics at a scale you can see. What a mine job pays, what leaves town, what stays, what happened when the mine closed, what the town budget is, who owns the buildings on Main Street, why the hospital closed, what a festival is worth. Trace one dollar of copper from the rock to wherever it ends, then draw the counterflow — what came back, to whom, and how fast it left again. That second circuit is measurable, and a local multiplier study is an honest project with a real number at the end.

## Read the rules

Who decides what, at which level. Town council, school board, Pinal County, Tonto National Forest, the Forest Service, the San Carlos Apache and the O'odham, Congress, the courts. Students attend the meetings and map the rules in use rather than the rules on paper. This is the strand that cannot be taught anywhere but a small town where the rooms are open, and it is worked out in [[Seven Questions]].

## Read each other

Oral histories of the mining families — Mexican-American, Slavic, Italian, Cornish. The segregation the older generation remembers in these same schools. The strikes. The Apache Leap story, and how the Apache tell it against how the town tells it. Spanish and English as working languages rather than subjects. The film crews who came and left. What a community health worker does. How an elder is cared for when the nearest hospital is forty minutes away.

## What the invariants are for

They are not a syllabus and nobody teaches them as courses. They are the small set of things the community will check for, and the only claim they make is that a graduate who cannot do one of them has been failed by us rather than by themselves. Everything else a person learns here is their own path, and the paths are not supposed to match.
