# Gap Ledger

Investigative journalism is substantially about the gap between rules in form and rules in use, and that gap is the spine of the Foothills Outlook as a hub rather than a stream. It is also the same instrument the school turns on itself, in [[Curriculum Is A Record]].

## The gap is the spine

The canonical accountability story has four beats. Here is what the rule promised. Here is what happened. Here is the distance between them. Here is who benefits from the distance being maintained. Building codes against what got built. A consent decree against practice. Permit conditions against actual monitoring. Procurement rules against contracts awarded. Once you see the shape you cannot unsee it.

## But it is not the whole body

Three genres fall outside it, and a paper that only does gaps becomes a prosecutor's office.

- Rule against rule. Not form against use but form against form — two rules in conflict, or a rule at one level quietly voiding one below it. A rider attached to a must-pass bill is exactly this, and gap analysis cannot see it because nothing was violated.
- No rule at all. The harm that is entirely legal. Often the bigger story, and structurally invisible to a gap ledger because there is no form to compare the use against. It was all legal may be the most valuable sentence a small-town paper can print.
- Witness. What it is like. Not a gap but a presence. Necessary, and the thing students do better than professionals, because people talk to children.

So four beats for the Outlook: the Gap, the Collision, the Legal Harm, and the Witness.

## The ledger

The paper's primary artifact is not articles. It is a standing ledger in the wiki, one entry per rule, carrying the rule text, its source, what it promises, what was measured, the delta, the status as open or closed or disputed or answered, who is accountable, and the date of the last check. Articles are updates to entries. The front page carries the top open gaps and how long each has been open.

That makes local journalism cumulative, which it almost never is. A story today does not vanish next week, it becomes a line that stays open until somebody closes it.

It also hooks into everything else. Each entry's rule is a node in the rule graph described in [[Seven Questions]]. Each measurement is a control chart series. Each contested entry gets its left and right polygons in the ecology, institutions and politics frame. Each gets a place on the timeline.

## Four rules before the first issue

Superior is 2,400 people and everyone is somebody's cousin, so a gap ledger can become a pillory.

- The subject is the rule, not the person. Entries are titled by ordinance, not by name.
- Right of reply is structural. Every entry carries a reply field the subject can fill, published in the same place with the same weight, rather than a letter to the editor three weeks later.
- The school publishes its own gaps first, in the same ledger, in the same format — its constitutions against its practice. That is what buys the standing to publish anyone else's, and nothing else will.
- Whoever asks answers. A student collecting an oral history or an interview answers the same questions they ask, because trust exists only where the exchange is reciprocal. This is the fifth principle of plática, and the reason our interviews are not extraction. See [[Campfire Not Bonfire]].

## Three tempos

Paper, weekly or monthly, because the elders read paper and paper gets left on a table. It carries the current drawing and the open questions.

Wiki, continuous, holding the history and the forks.

The wall, physical, in the library or the old hotel or the co-op, with pens attached. This is the one everyone forgets, and it is where the arguing actually happens.

Every recurring town conversation — water, the mine, the highway, the clinic, the school — gets a standing page, a standing drawing and a standing slot in the paper. Journalism here means maintaining the drawings rather than producing takes, and the measure of a good issue is that the drawing got better.
