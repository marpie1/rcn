# NDC School Superior

A school that is a Neighborhood Development Center with children in it. Written for Chris — RegenSonora Foundation, Leo's, and the Superior NDC — and therefore written for someone who already accepts the premise, so it spends its length on mechanism rather than persuasion.

## The argument in short

Superior is an unusually complete textbook for a town of 2,400. Within ten miles there is a copper ore body thousands of feet deep, a live sovereignty dispute that reached the Supreme Court, the oldest botanical garden in Arizona, a century of boom and bust, a bilingual mining culture, a creek, a tunnel, a legend, and a town that lost its hospital. Almost nothing a young person needs in order to understand the world is missing here. It is all at 1:1 scale.

A town needs an instrument that can see itself, decide together, and keep its own record. Almost no town can afford one, because it takes time, some neutrality, and a standing reason to publish. A school is the only institution a town already funds that has all three. Children have time. Children are the legitimate askers of naive questions — a fifteen-year-old can ask the water operator something a grown neighbour could not ask without it meaning something. And a school has a reason to publish that is not advocacy.

So the proposal inverts the usual relationship. This is not project-based learning, where the projects are simulations and a teacher grades them. The town actually needs the water record, and nobody is keeping it. The learning is a byproduct of the town's self-knowledge rather than a rehearsal for it. That is also the only version that survives contact with a real place, because invented projects rot within two years and real obligations do not.

## The commitment that makes it possible

The NDC school never enrols a student, never seeks a charter, and never applies for ADM funding. Its students belong to Superior Unified. Everything it builds is offered to the district to adopt.

That sentence is not a concession and it is not modesty. Arizona district money follows average daily membership, and in a small district anything that looks like it pulls students pulls dollars. Leaving the question open means every conversation with the district is secretly about whether we are a charter application in disguise, and they would be right to wonder. Closing it — publicly, permanently, in our own constitution, with a clause that can be made stricter but never looser — defuses most of the latent conflict before it forms and makes everything in [[Credit Ladder]] possible.

## The structure to plan on

The trap is writing a curriculum, because curriculum is a noun where a generator is needed. The plan has five planes, and curriculum is not one of them. Curriculum is the trace a learner leaves crossing them. See [[Curriculum Is A Record]].

- Commons — what is being learned about. The ore body, the creek, the ledger, the rules, each other. See [[Five Literacies]].
- Arenas — where learning happens. Campfire, cave wall, field, shop, meeting room, screen.
- Practices — the repeated moves. See one do one teach one, dialogue, measure, map, model, publish.
- Record — what persists. The badge ledger, the drawings, the datasets, the town's wiki, the paper.
- Constitution — who decides all of the above, and how that changes. See [[Seven Questions]] and [[Constitution Of The Gap Desk]].

Planes are for design. Ostrom's Institutional Analysis and Development framework is for analysis — when we want to understand a situation rather than build one, we use IAD, and the two are kept separate on purpose.

## Invariants, paths, and the record

A school can be self-organising and still be understandable, but only if three things are held apart.

- Invariants — the [[Five Literacies]]. What every graduate can do. Small, fixed, publicly argued.
- Paths — infinite, self-chosen, student-generated. No two look alike. See [[Ferndale Ideal Education Model]].
- Record — proof that some path covered the invariants, on a scale that can compare students who did nothing alike.

The third is the hard one and it is solved. Rasch measurement gives specific objectivity: the comparison of two people is independent of which items each of them took, provided the items sit on one ruler. A common curriculum is not required in order to have a common measure. That is the licence for everything else.

## What gets built first

The order matters more than the list, because each item earns the next.

- A record nobody asked for. The creek gauge, the rain gauge, the well levels, kept weekly, published, never missed. One year of it before anything else is claimed.
- A [[Gap Ledger]] in the wiki, with the school's own constitutions as its first entries, before anyone else's.
- The Ferndale map ported and forkable, so the first thing in the library is a demonstration that a student can make their own curriculum and hand it on. [[Eleven Steps]] is how the next person makes theirs, and it costs an hour and a pencil.
- One fire circle a month, tended by someone who has earned the badge. See [[Campfire Not Bonfire]].
- One thing given away, by every learner, every season.

## The honest difficulties

The school has to teach Oak Flat and Resolution honestly in a town where families sit on both sides. Not settle it. Host it. A school that can hold a contested commons without pretending it is uncontested has taught the one thing the wider world most lacks — and it is the reason the funding structure has to be solved before the curriculum, not after. If the mine funds the school, the school cannot hold that question, and everyone in town will know it.

Capture runs in both directions. If the district adopts our ledger, the district will want to control the ledger, and the entries about the district will be the first to quietly disappear. The protections are self-custodied identity, so a learner holds credentials no institution can revoke, and forking, so that any version of the ledger with entries missing is visibly a fork rather than the record. Both have to be in place at the start. By the time they are needed it is too late to add them.
