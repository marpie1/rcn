# Curriculum Is A Record

The curriculum is what the student learned, or attempted to learn and the system failed them. Take that definition literally and the design of the school follows from it.

## A record, not a plan

If curriculum is what was learned, it can only be known afterwards. It is a record rather than a plan, and it develops with a person across the span of a life rather than being completed at eighteen.

The forward-facing object is therefore something else: an intention. A learner declares what they mean to learn and by when, and a mentor countersigns it. That is the dyadic contract, and it is the only part of the arrangement that points forward.

## The gap that unifies everything

An intention is a rule in form. The record of what actually happened is the rule in use. The distance between them is a gap, and it has exactly the structure the [[Gap Ledger]] applies to the town.

So the school keeps a gap ledger on itself, per learner. Not a gradebook. A ledger whose entries read: intended to learn to read the well log by March; did not happen; cause, the water operator was unavailable for six weeks and nobody arranged a substitute.

That is an institutional entry rather than a student one. The parenthesis in the definition — and the system failed them — becomes a data field with an owner. The failure is attributed to the thing that failed, publicly, in the same format the newspaper uses on the town council.

A school willing to publish its own gap ledger has earned the standing to publish anyone else's. It also puts the method in the same place as the subject matter, because students learn gap analysis by having it run honestly on their own school, about them, where they can read it and argue with it.

One instrument, three scopes: the learner's own intentions, the school's constitutions, and the town's rules.

## The record includes how it felt

[[Eleven Steps]] devotes half its length to recalling, out loud, how each earlier step felt, and says plainly that this is where the big learning is. That is not a wellbeing add-on, it is the part of the record a gradebook cannot hold.

So a learner's record carries two tracks. What was made, which is the artifact and the badge. And what it was like to make it, in their own words, written down at the time. The second is what tells a person years later why a whole class of work felt closed to them, and it is the only evidence that can distinguish did not from would not from was not allowed to.

Every step of the protocol carries a Notes field from the beginning rather than a reflection tacked on at the end, and our ledger should be built the same way.

## Every student generates their own

[[Ferndale Ideal Education Model]] is the evidence that this is not aspirational, and [[Eleven Steps]] is the method. Curriculum-making is itself the first curriculum, and it recurs. Each season a student publishes a map of what they intend to learn and why, in the graph tool, on the wiki.

Because it is FedWiki, a curriculum is a page you fork. Within a few years the school holds hundreds of student-authored, forked and mutated curricula whose lineage is visible, and that library will be better than anything the adults would have written. The adults stop assigning and start watching the aggregate for invariants nobody is choosing, and then offering.

## How progress stays legible

Two readings of one ledger, on one picture.

- The student's own standard is the declared intention, countersigned. It appears on the map as the learner's own marker.
- The community's standard is a threshold the town draws on the same ruler and argues about in public — to be trusted with the water record you need to be here. That cut is a collective-choice decision rather than a psychometric one. It is contestable and amendable, and putting it where the town sets it is more honest than any state cut score.

Rasch measurement is what makes both readable at once. Persons on the left of a Wright map, items on the right, both on the same scale. A student sees exactly which badges sit just above their current position, which is the zone of proximal development rendered as a map rather than a metaphor. A ten-year-old can read it. One Wright map is the whole transcript.

See one, do one, teach one supplies the item structure for free, because teaching is strictly harder than doing, so each competence yields an ordered three-threshold item. The teach step is also the validity check, since nobody can fake teaching in front of the person who taught them. Verification is human and situated rather than proctored.

Misfit is the good data. Someone who earns a hard badge having skipped easy ones is either a mis-calibrated item or a genuinely unusual path, and in a standardised school that is noise to suppress while here it is the most interesting row in the table and worth a conversation.

Two cautions, stated once. Rasch assumes one dimension, so more than one ruler will probably be needed — something like reading the land, making, and dialogue and governance — rather than pretending to a single achievement score. And badge difficulties are estimated from the data, so in the first year with few people they are noisy, and the map should be published as provisional and said to be provisional.

## What replaces grades and classes

Three couplings are cut separately.

- Age to cohort, cut by see one do one teach one. The teacher is whoever holds the badge, so a twelve-year-old teaching a forty-year-old the well log is unremarkable. Mixed age is the mechanism rather than a concession.
- Subject to period to room, cut by rhythm rather than schedule. A day has a shape — morning circle, long field block, making block, publish before dark — not eight bells. Rooms are named for what is made in them: map room, shop, bench, booth, kitchen, quiet room. Nothing is called a classroom. Outside is the default, with the seasonal inversion Superior forces anyway, outdoors early and late in summer and midday in winter.
- Level to graduation, cut by continuous thresholds on one public ruler. Nobody is promoted in June. People cross thresholds whenever they cross them, and the completion of a piece of work is that it is given away.
