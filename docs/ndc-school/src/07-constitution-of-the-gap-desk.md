# Constitution Of The Gap Desk

A worked example of a one-page constitution written with the seven rule types from [[Seven Questions]]. Version 0.4, amending 0.3. Prior versions stay in the page history.

## Boundary

Anyone who has earned Verify and signed this page. You join by shadowing two entries and then verifying one under a Signer. You leave by saying so at any circle: your name comes off, your entries stay. No one is removed except by the amendment procedure below.

## Position

Four roles, held temporarily and never by the same person twice running. Checker does the verification. Signer is an adult who countersigns before publication, at least one, always named on the entry. Keeper maintains the ledger and is the only role that may change an entry's status. Reply Holder carries the draft to its subject, in person, before publication.

## Choice

A Checker may open an entry, gather, measure and draft, and may not publish. A Signer may publish and may refuse, and a refusal is written on the entry with its reason. A Keeper may change status and may not change findings. The Reply Holder must deliver the draft in person and may not publish before the reply window closes. No one may open an entry about a household they live in. Whoever asks answers: a Checker conducting an interview answers the same questions they ask.

## Aggregation

Opening an entry takes any two members. Publishing takes the Checker plus one Signer with the reply window closed. Moving an entry to closed takes the Keeper plus the subject's acknowledgement, or two Signers if the subject will not respond. Amending this page takes consent, meaning no sustained objection after one full circle of speaking.

## Information

Every entry shows the rule text, its source, who checked, what was measured, when, and the reply. Sources are named unless the Desk grants anonymity, and the granting is recorded even when the name is not. Drafts are open to the whole Desk and nothing is confidential from the Desk. The Desk's own failures go in the ledger like anyone else's.

## Payoff

Badges are Verify, Measure, Interview, Edit and Keep. Bylines are real names. The cost is that your name is on it: if it is wrong, you write the correction and you sign it. No entry is opened for money, and the Desk takes no advertising from the subject of an open entry.

## Scope

The Desk may publish about rules, institutions and public decisions. It may never publish about a minor's health or discipline, anyone's immigration status, or a household's finances. This clause may be amended to be stricter but never looser. Entries about this school have the same standing as any other.

## Amendment

Any member may propose, in writing, on this page. One full circle of speaking, then the consent rule above. The old version stays in history with the reason for the change written in. This page is reviewed every season whether or not anyone proposes anything, and reviewed includes asking of each clause whether it has quietly become a rule in form only.

## Two things to notice

The Scope clause has a ratchet — stricter but never looser. That is an entrenchment device, and it is how a minor is protected from a future membership that no longer remembers why the clause exists. No aggregation rule gets around it.

The Amendment clause audits itself for the same gap the Desk audits in everyone else. A constitution that never checks whether it is still in use is exactly the sediment we are teaching students to find.

## The same template, compressed

The Fire Circle, version 0.2, in eight lines. See [[Campfire Not Bonfire]].

- Boundary — open to anyone in town; you enter by being welcomed by a member, and by eating first.
- Position — Tender, rotating and badged; Welcomer; Timekeeper. No chair.
- Choice — speak while holding the piece; no rebuttal in the same circle; the Tender may call silence and must if any two people ask.
- Aggregation — none. The Circle decides nothing. Decisions ripen elsewhere, and the Circle may notice that something has ripened and say so.
- Information — what was learned may be carried out, who said it may not. No recording. The drawing stays and is never signed.
- Payoff — no badge for attending, one badge for tending. Food comes from whoever asked for the circle.
- Scope — any subject, except a matter on a council agenda that same week.
- Amendment — the same consent rule, only at a circle called for that purpose.

If it does not fit on one page, the activity is doing too much or the constitution is hiding something.
