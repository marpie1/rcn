# Credit Ladder

How the NDC school sits next to the existing public schools without triggering the conflict, and how credit eventually flows both ways. The strategy is peripheral participation and adaptive adoption rather than confrontation, and it generalises past the school to every other department of local government.

## Name the conflict before it names you

Funding is the real one and everything else is downstream. Arizona district money follows average daily membership, and in a small district anything that looks like it pulls students pulls dollars. Hence the commitment in [[NDC School Superior]]: never enrol, never seek a charter, never apply for ADM funding.

The rest, honestly.

- Teacher status. Community mentors doing teacher-shaped work without certification reads as deprofessionalisation. The counter is that we badge what the district does not teach, and mentors are framed as the thing teachers chronically lack — a supply of expert adults — rather than as substitutes.
- Liability. Minors off campus, near a creek, near a mine. Real, boring, solvable, and it has to be solved before anyone will say yes to anything else.
- Accountability optics. The district is judged on test scores, graduation rate and chronic absenteeism. If we do not help those numbers we are a curiosity, and if we appear to hurt them we are an enemy.
- Political exposure. Anything touching Resolution is radioactive for a board elected by both sides. The [[Gap Ledger]] will eventually produce an entry somebody on the board hates, and that day should be planned for now rather than then.

## What the district needs and cannot get

- Career and technical education hours and work-based learning, funded in Arizona and impossible for a small district to staff.
- Evidence artifacts. A well log analysis is a science standard and an oral history is an English language arts standard. Districts need artifacts for their own reporting and have no time to generate them.
- Community engagement, demanded by every grant application and beyond the capacity of a district this size.
- Electives. Small schedules cannot be filled. This is the softest entry point and the most underrated.
- Chronic absenteeism, the metric every district is desperate about. A student who will not come to second period will show up for the map crew, and that is a fact the district can use.

## Four rungs, never skipped

- Rung zero, invisible. Weekends, after school, summer. No credit sought, no permission needed, nothing to say yes or no to. About a year. The only success condition is that individual teachers begin referring students unprompted. Rung zero also has to produce a record the institution does not yet know it needs, because a rung one that rests only on enthusiasm does not survive a summer — see the Ferndale outcome in [[Ferndale Ideal Education Model]].
- Rung one, one teacher of record. A single willing teacher accepts NDC work as evidence for their own assignment — the water dataset as the lab report, the oral history as the research paper. No policy, no board vote, no memorandum. The practice enters through a person, which is peripheral participation working on the institution the same way it works on a learner.
- Rung two, elective and technical credit by portfolio. Under mechanisms that already exist: independent study, work-based learning, technical education hours, demonstrated-competency credit. A written agreement and a board consent item rather than a policy rewrite. The badge ledger is the evidence package.
- Rung three, two-way transfer. District courses appear as badges on the ledger and NDC badges appear on the transcript. The membrane opens both directions and the district now has something to lose if the ledger stops existing. This is the rung where they start asking us for things.
- Rung four, adoption. The district runs its own version. Our job is to be copied and to be gracious about it. Success looks like the distinction dissolving.

Each rung is entered because somebody inside wants it, not because we proposed it. The proposal's job at every rung is to state something already obviously true to the person reading it.

## The translation layer

A badge ledger has to render as a transcript line without anyone at the district learning our vocabulary.

Every badge carries a state standard alignment code, estimated hours, an evidence link, the mentor's identifier, the date, and the see-do-teach level. A credit bundle is a named set of badges a counsellor can accept as half or one Carnegie unit. Rasch sets where the bundle's threshold sits on the ruler, but the counsellor never sees a logit. They see that a standard is met, with the hours, the portfolio and a named mentor's signature.

The ledger speaks Carnegie units and standard codes at its edge and Rasch in its interior. That is the whole diplomatic technique in one sentence, and it generalises to every other institution we eventually want to reach.

## The same ladder for local government

The long strategy has the identical shape, and the mechanism at every rung is the same: be the custodian of a record the institution needs and does not have. Not advocacy — custody.

Keep the creek record nobody asked for. One person inside starts citing it. It becomes an input to an official process — a public comment, a board packet, a grant narrative. Then the department maintains it. At that point the department's information flow has changed, which is Meadows leverage point six, reached without ever having fought at level three.

An institution will eventually adopt a record it has come to depend on, and adopting it changes how it sees.

## The risk, stated plainly

Capture runs both ways. If the district adopts the ledger, the district will want to control the ledger, and the entries about the district will be the first thing to quietly disappear.

The protections are self-custodied identity, so a learner holds credentials no institution can revoke, and forking, so that any version of the ledger with entries missing is visibly a fork rather than the record. Both have to be designed in at rung zero. At rung three it is too late to add them.
