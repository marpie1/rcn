# Ferndale Ideal Education Model

In Ferndale in 2019, five students built their own curriculum over six weekly sessions. The artifact is a Plectica map titled IEM Plectica w/Labels: 232 nodes, of which 129 are concepts and 103 are link labels, joined by 167 links and grouped into thirteen regions. It is the best evidence we have that [[Curriculum Is A Record]] and that students can generate their own. The method that produced it is [[Eleven Steps]].

## What they built

Twelve domains, plus a High Level Connections cluster that holds all twelve as one densely linked network.

- Conflict — social pressure, expectations, drama, stories, social media, dealing with authority figures, overcoming obstacles
- Emotions — emotions we feel, love, heartbreak, dream concepts, trauma, hate, desire, peace
- Relationships — connections, people, love, friendships, relationships with family, selfishness, self love and respect, community, groups, awareness of external entities, open dialogue
- Happiness — travel, spontaneity, passion, greater acceptance, freedom, experiencing life, sunrises and sunsets, feeling empowered, expressing individuality, new challenges, purpose, abstract thought
- Health — environmental sustainability, diet, injury or illness, psychologists, first aid, mental health, exercise
- Development — home care skills, goals, problem solving, maintaining organisation, planning, work ethic, self-discipline, financial skills, cultural and social norms, emotion management, emotional development, loss and failure, accepting mortality, accepting change, existential experience, tragedies
- Academics — science, humanities, mathematics, technical skills, teachers, biology, psychology, chemistry, reading and writing
- Assets and Skills — wealth as time and choice, tolerance, cultural immersion, preparation for jobs, preparation for careers, cooking, money, control, external resources, time
- Guidance — opportunity, mentoring, books, an individual's navigation, parenting
- Perspective — belief systems, worldview, ethics and morals, pop culture, culture, perspective, experiences, media, fear concepts, sensibilities, history, politics, selfishness, art, environment, awareness, sustainability
- Scale — self, entities, big picture of life, individuality
- A Better Future — living comfortably, success, increase in community development, how to improve the community from home to world, cross-generational collaboration, posterity, happiness, balance, jobs in environmental development

## What the shape tells us

Perspective and Development are the largest clusters, at eighteen concepts each. Academics is nine, and it is the only cluster that reads like a school. It is also the most conventional and least interesting thing on the map. Everything else reads like a life.

They did not build a tree. The twelve domains carry fifty-four links among themselves, nearly complete and thoroughly cyclic — Conflict to Relationships and Relationships to Conflict, Happiness to Conflict and Conflict to Happiness, Emotions to Perspective and Perspective to Emotions. Adults asked to organise knowledge produce a taxonomy. These five produced a system with feedback in it, unprompted. That is the finding, and it is the argument for the whole approach.

Development is the cluster no school has. Loss and failure, accepting mortality, accepting change, existential experience, tragedies, with propositions such as tragedies teach good and bad about emotion management, and accepting mortality impacts existential experience. Teenagers put death in the curriculum. No district ever has.

The link verbs are the treasure. Most are ordinary systems vocabulary — is part of, allows, leads to, influences, creates, requires. Scattered through Emotions are four that are not: heartbreak envies love, dream concepts ignore hate, trauma listens to hate, desire admires trauma. That is poetry doing analytic work, and any adult ontologist would normalise it into relates to and destroy it. A modelling language that cannot hold envies cannot hold what a sixteen-year-old actually knows.

Two political claims arrived unprompted: selfishness creates politics, and history creates fear concepts. Selfishness is the one concept they placed in two clusters, Relationships and Perspective. They found it mattered twice.

## How it was made

Marc Pierson was introduced to Noah Williams, a disaffected high school student, midway through the first year of a Ferndale experiment: eighteen students who had given up on schooling, invited back as a cohort with one administrator, one facilitator and computer-based content, to educate themselves without a classroom and to come and go as they pleased.

The spread went through the student rather than the institution. Noah did [[Eleven Steps]] at Marc's kitchen table. Then the facilitator did it, separately, at the same table. Then her administrator did. Only then did the administrator and facilitator ask what could be next, and the answer was that Noah should invite four others from the cohort to do it simultaneously but separately.

Five personal drawings then had to become one. Six weekly sessions, sticky notes on a board, and the work turned out to be mostly about language: connecting nodes, long arguments about what words meant, renaming, a few additions, and combining near-synonyms.

That is the finding for our purposes. Integrating personal maps is not a diagramming problem, it is terminology negotiation, and it is slow. Six weeks for five drawings. Any design that assumes merging is a quick step after the fun part has the ratio backwards.

Marc held the same non-intervention through this phase, encouraging and neither judging nor suggesting, which is harder in a group than alone: what is being withheld is no longer the urge to teach but the urge to arbitrate. See the group rule in [[Eleven Steps]].

It also explains the duplicates. Selfishness appears in both Relationships and Perspective, Love in both Relationships and Emotions, Happiness in both A Better Future and High Level Connections. Those are not merge errors and not quite deliberate choices either — they are the residue of a negotiation where two people both raised a thing and nobody in the room had the authority to rule on it. The seams survive because the adult refused to settle the words. Keep them in any port; they are information.

## A caveat I had wrong

An earlier draft of this page said Plectica's built-in DSRP grammar shaped the output, because Scale, Perspective and Relationships read as three of Cabrera's four moves. That is probably wrong, and at most applies to the last step. The generation was analog — pencil, paper, sticky notes, eleven steps that mention no framework at all. Plectica was the transcription medium after the fact, not the thinking grammar. The clusters came out of six weeks of arguing about words.

## What happened next

Noah wrote the experience up and made recommendations to the administrator and facilitator, then built software to support community-based education of this kind. He and Marc gave a talk at Education for the 21st Century in Bogotá, where Marc met Kerry Turner, and ReLocalize Creativity came out of that meeting. Noah is now studying computational cognitive neuroscience in a self-constructed degree programme at Western Washington University, and flourishing.

Nothing stuck in Ferndale.

That is the most useful sentence in this page and it belongs in [[Credit Ladder]]. One student flourished and the institution did not change, because the work reached one facilitator and one administrator and then summer break arrived. There was no record the school had come to depend on, so there was nothing that made stopping costly. Ferndale is the evidence for why rung zero has to produce a record nobody asked for, before anything at all is proposed.

## Interior and exterior

What is structurally absent from the map is as informative as what is present. There is no place, no land, no work as such, no rules or governance beyond the word politics, no money beyond wealth and control, and no history except that experiences make up history.

Now set that beside the [[Five Literacies]]: land, water, ledger, rules, each other. They are complements rather than competitors. Ferndale mapped the inner world. Superior maps the outer one. The only overlap is reading each other, which is where both are richest and which no school subject covers.

A student's own curriculum is a path through both maps, and the school happens at the joins. Conflict meets the land exchange. Scale meets self, household, neighbourhood, town, watershed, county, state, tribe, nation. Guidance meets mentoring made structural. A Better Future meets the town's actual future, which is genuinely undecided. Development meets what happens when the mine closes again.

## What we still need from Ferndale

The map is the artifact. The generator is the method, and the method is not in the map. Who convened it, what the opening prompt was, whether the twelve domains came first or emerged from clustering loose nodes, how many sessions there were, whether an adult ever added a node, and what they threw away. That exists only in someone's memory or notes, and it is worth more to this design than the map itself.

## Porting it

The map should move into the RCN Graph Tool, and not as a copy. Three things are gained. Polarity and loops: their links are unsigned, many are plainly causal, and having students sign the edges and then find the feedback loops in their own map is a better systems lesson than any exercise. Composer levels: the twelve domains are the schema level, the 129 concepts the variable level, and one student's actual experiences the instance level, which is how a seven-year-old and a seventeen-year-old look at the same drawing. Federation: FedWiki gives four hundred forks with visible lineage, where Plectica gives one map with five authors.

The recommended build is a faithful port first, preserving their nodes, their terms and their unsigned links as the baseline everyone forks from, and then a signed causal version published as a visible fork of it. That costs one extra step and makes the first thing in the library a demonstration of the method.
