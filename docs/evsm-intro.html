Introduction to the eVSM Suite

A browser-based toolset for conducting organizational health assessments using an extended Viable Systems Model framework.

What is eVSM?

eVSM stands for extended Viable Systems Model. It is a structured method for assessing how well an organization — a neighborhood, community group, business, or network — is functioning as a coherent, adaptive system.

The framework draws on Stafford Beer's Viable System Model, which describes the functions any living organization must perform to survive and adapt. The eVSM survey operationalizes this as eleven interconnected spheres (organizational capacities) and the directed relationships between them. Respondents assess each sphere and relationship, providing both a status judgment and qualitative evidence.

The result is a rich, multi-perspective picture of organizational health — what is working, what needs attention, and where different groups in the organization see things differently.

The Four Tools

evsm-svg-v3.html

Survey Tool

Design the survey and collect individual respondent assessments. Facilitators configure the survey in Setup mode; respondents fill it out in Survey mode and export a JSON response file.

evsm-aggregator.html

Aggregator

Load multiple response files and view aggregated assessments across all respondents. Filter by "world" categories to compare subgroups. Uses Claude AI to synthesize narrative insights.

evsm-report.html

Respondent Report

Generate a print-ready report for a single respondent. Shows their complete assessment across all spheres and relationships. Can be saved as a PDF.

evsm_excel_tool.html

Excel Tool

Convert between eVSM JSON and Excel workbooks. Use Excel to prepare survey content (sphere labels, descriptions, world categories), then import back to JSON for use in the survey tool.

The Eleven Spheres

Every eVSM survey assesses eleven spheres. Their labels are fully customizable — the facilitator replaces the defaults with language that fits the organization being assessed. The geometric relationships between them (which sphere connects to which) are fixed by the VSM topology.

The spheres are grouped into three layers:

Sensory / Interface Layer

These spheres face outward — they sense the environment and manage external relationships.

  • S-4 — Awareness within the neighborhood (local environment sensing)
  • S-7 — Resources (finance and material flow)
  • S-9 — Awareness beyond the neighborhood (broader environment sensing)

Internal Core Layer

These spheres manage day-to-day operations and execution.

  • S-3 — Tools and workspaces (infrastructure)
  • S-6 — Ability to get things done (execution)
  • S-8 — Quality of planning (forward thinking)

Alignment / Homeostatic Layer

These spheres coordinate across the system and maintain coherence over time.

  • S-1 — Coordination (integration across parts)
  • S-2 — Involvement (breadth and depth of participation)
  • S-5 — Quality of learning and change (adaptation)
  • S-10 — Leadership (self-organizing capacity)
  • S-11 — Culture (values and shared norms)

Assessment Status Terms

Spheres are assessed on a four-point scale:

  • Go — Functioning well; no significant concerns
  • Caution — Needs attention; some issues present
  • Stop — Urgent concern; significant dysfunction
  • Unsure — Don't have enough information to judge

Relationships (edges) are assessed on a three-point scale:

  • Agree — The relationship is working as described
  • Disagree — The relationship is not working or is blocked
  • Unknown — Not enough information to assess

World Categories

"Worlds" are demographic or role descriptors that respondents self-select at the time of the survey. Examples: Longtime resident, Under 30, Business owner, Financially precarious.

Worlds serve two purposes in analysis:

  1. Subgroup filtering — in the Aggregator, you can view assessments from only those respondents who belong to a particular world, revealing how different groups experience the organization differently.
  2. Comparative synthesis — the Aggregator can display two world sets side by side and use Claude to articulate where they agree and diverge.
No central database. All data is exchanged as JSON files. Respondents download their response file and send it to the facilitator (email, shared drive, etc.). The facilitator loads all files into the Aggregator. Nothing is stored on a server.
