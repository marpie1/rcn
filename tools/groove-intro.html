Groove Workspace

Introduction — what it is, what it does, and why it exists

Version 0.2  ·  July 2026
Introduction User Manual Presentation Open Groove ↗
Groove is a private, person-controlled collaboration workspace for small care teams. It combines a shared health record viewer, threaded discussion, and FedWiki page integration into one tool designed around a simple rule: the person receiving care controls everything.

What is Groove?

Groove takes its name and inspiration from Groove Networks (Ray Ozzie, 2000–2005) — the original peer-to-peer collaboration platform that treated private workspaces and person-controlled data as first principles, not afterthoughts.

The RCN Groove Workspace applies the same philosophy to community health. A Community Health Worker (CHW) and the person they support create a shared workspace. They use it to view the person's Shared Care Plan, hold threaded discussions about care decisions, and coordinate with 1–2 family members or other team members — all without passing health data through a third-party platform.

Who it is for

Groove is built for care teams of two to four people. One person is always the person of record — the individual receiving care. The others are people they have invited.

Person of Record

The person receiving care

Controls all access. Sees exactly who has viewed their plan and when. All entries written by others are clearly marked. Nothing leaves the workspace without their knowledge.

Community Health Worker

CHW / Care navigator

Works alongside the person during home visits and check-ins. Enters observations into the record — always marked "Entered by other." Uses Groove on a phone during visits.

Family Member

Trusted family or friend

1–2 people the person has invited. Can read and contribute to discussions. Cannot modify the health record directly — only the person and their CHW do that.

Care Team

The whole unit

Together, the team has a shared view of the care plan, a way to discuss it, and a record of who said what. No emails, no screenshots, no lost context.

The Shared Care Plan

The Shared Care Plan (SCP) is the health record at the center of Groove. It was originally developed through the Whatcom County community health project and validated with patients including an Atlanta homeless mental health cohort. The section structure has been in use for more than a decade.

In the RCN implementation, each SCP section is a FedWiki page — a structured document that lives on the web but is controlled by the person it belongs to. Groove imports these pages and displays them alongside discussions, so the care team can look at a medication entry and talk about it in the same screen.

About Me

Personal background, living situation, preferences, and goals. Written first, updated rarely.

Care Team

Everyone involved in this person's care — providers, CHW, family contacts, pharmacist.

Diagnoses

Active conditions with date of diagnosis, managing provider, and status.

Medications

Current medications, doses, prescribers, and start dates. Editable field by field.

Reactions

Allergies and adverse reactions. Substance, type of reaction, severity, and date noted.

Health Log

Timestamped entries for vitals, symptoms, and visit notes. Reverse-chronological; committed entries stay read-only.

Medical History

Past events, hospitalizations, surgeries, and significant health history.

Next Steps

Agreed actions — what needs to happen, who is responsible, by when.

Advanced Directives

The person's wishes for care decisions they cannot make in the moment.

Who's Accessed My Plan

An audit log the person controls. Every access event is recorded and readable only by them.

How Groove and the SCP fit together

The two tools are independent but designed to work as one. The SCP is where the health record lives. Groove is where the care team coordinates around it. The integration point is FedWiki's page format: an SCP page can be imported into a Groove workspace with one click, displayed alongside a discussion, and exported back without data loss.

SCP pages work without Groove. Groove works without SCP. When the care team needs to discuss a specific health record entry, they import that page — it appears like any other page in the workspace, but carries the SCP structure.

What is built today

Identity

Each person creates a local identity — a display name paired with cryptographic keypairs (Ed25519 for signing, X25519 for key wrapping). No account, no password, no external service.

SCP page templates

All 10 SCP sections are available as templates in the sidebar. Click any section to open your personal copy — a new page is created if you do not have one yet.

FedWiki page import

Paste any FedWiki page URL and click Import. The page renders in the workspace with per-item editing, pinning, and paragraph-to-discussion linking.

Threaded discussion

Create named discussions. Post messages, reply to specific messages, and reference specific paragraphs from an open page. Messages poll every 5 seconds.

Written-by-other marking

Every message has an "Entry by someone else" checkbox. Messages marked this way are visually distinguished — a core SCP governance rule.

Pin and lift

Pin a specific paragraph from a page into a discussion message (📌 pin). Lift a discussion message back into the page as a new paragraph (↑ to page).

Inline page editing

Edit any paragraph directly in Groove — split-pane markdown editor with live preview. Add new paragraphs after any existing item. Changes are journaled.

Privacy summary

View a summary of your identity and access log. The access log records who viewed what and when — readable only by the workspace owner.

FedWiki export

Export any imported page back to FedWiki JSON with a full journal. Pages round-trip without data loss.

Pilot deployment — Superior, AZ

The first real-world deployment is with Chris Casillas and the NDC group in Superior, Arizona. Care team unit: person + CHW + 1–2 family members. CHWs use phones during home visits — so the tool is designed to work on a mobile browser with no installation required. Server-mode (no peer-to-peer sync needed) for the first deployment.

Honest gaps

GapNotes
Access grant UI The API for granting, revoking, and time-limiting workspace access is built. The frontend to use it is not yet built. Access is currently managed at the server level.
Workspace membership UI The API exists. No frontend yet — adding or removing members from a workspace requires direct API calls for now.
Client-side key generation Identity keypairs are currently generated server-side for prototype simplicity. Before real deployment, generation and storage move to the browser — the server never sees secret keys.
P2P sync Currently server-mode only. Peer-to-peer sync (Automerge) for offline-first use is planned but not started. Server-mode is sufficient for the Superior AZ pilot.
Physician summary PDF A clean, printable physician summary from the full SCP section set is planned but not yet built.
File sharing and calendar Not yet built. Groove currently handles text and FedWiki pages only.
Automatic access log The "Who's Accessed My Plan" SCP page currently requires manual entry. Backend-driven automatic access logging must be completed before deployment with real patient data.

Why this exists

Community health workers and the people they support need a place to coordinate care that is private by design, not private by policy. Every platform the RCN evaluated for this work makes the operator the de facto owner of the data — the person can see it, but they cannot take it with them, revoke access, or audit who has looked at it.

Groove is built on the premise that a person's health record should travel with them, not with the organization that helped them create it. The FedWiki page model — where every page carries a journal of who wrote what and can be forked to a new home — is the right foundation for that. Groove adds the coordination layer: the discussion, the access log, and the care team workspace that makes the health record useful without surrendering control of it.
