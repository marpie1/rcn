Positive Affect Score

User manual — every control, what it does, and what the numbers mean

Document updated: August 2026
Introduction User Manual Open the Tool ↗ Conversation Navigator ↗
Open the tool, pick a timeframe, rate ten words, save. Everything else in this manual is detail on top of those four steps.

Taking a check-in

  1. Pick the timeframe first. Right now is the default. Switching it after you have rated the words keeps your ratings and re-reads them under the new framing.
  2. Rate each of the ten words 1 to 5 — 1 is very slightly or not at all, 5 is extremely. There is no neutral or skip; if a word does not describe you, that is a 1.
  3. Watch the running count. The score panel shows how many words are rated and stays blank until all ten are in. Save is disabled until then.
  4. Save the check-in. It is added to your history and, if you are in a shared session, sent to the group.

Answer quickly

The instrument is designed for a first reaction. Deliberating over each word tends to produce a description of how you think you ought to feel rather than how you do. Under a minute for all ten is normal.

Reading the score

Ten items at 1–5 gives a raw score from 10 (every word rated 1) to 50 (every word rated 5). There is no percentage and no pass mark. The number is only meaningful against another number taken the same way: your own earlier score, or the same group at a different point in the session.

Crosswalk version 0.1-provisional-watson-clark-past-week. The Shared Care Plan and My Health Picture use the same four bands and the same cutoffs, so a past-week score taken here is directly comparable with one taken there.

No band is shown for a right-now score

This is deliberate, not an omission. The crosswalk is calibrated against a past-week reading. Applying it to a momentary score would report a stable characteristic of a person on the strength of how their last twenty minutes went.

The optional NA-10

Clicking Optional: also rate negative feelings reveals a second set of ten words, scored the same way and reported separately as its own 10–50 total. It is never subtracted from the positive score and never affects the band. Leave it closed unless you specifically want the second reading — it doubles the time the check-in takes.

Where history lives

In this browser's local storage, under rcn.positive-affect-score.history. It is not sent anywhere and is not backed up. A different browser, a different device, or a cleared cache means a different history — download the JSON if it matters to you.

Shared sessions

  1. Click Start a shared session. The page reloads with a six-character session code in the URL.
  2. Send that link to everyone. Anyone who opens it lands in the same session.
  3. Each person enters a name and clicks Join, then takes the check-in as normal.
  4. The Group panel shows every score as a dot on a 10–50 axis, with a vertical mark at the mean. Your own dot is dark.

Names and anonymity

A session is open by default and names appear beside scores. If any participant ticks Make this session anonymous, the whole session goes anonymous immediately for everyone — the distribution and the mean remain, individual names disappear. Unticking it restores open mode only if nobody else has it ticked. Your own score is always visible to you.

Sessions are ephemeral

Session data lives at panas/<sessionId> in the shared RCN Realtime Database and your entry is removed when you close the tab. Nothing is archived. Anyone with the six-character link can read the session while it is live, so treat the link as the access control it is. Your own local history is separate and survives the session.

Solo mode works offline

Nothing is loaded from the network unless a session code is present in the URL — not even the database library. The tool runs from a local file with no connection at all, which is the mode to use if the check-in is going somewhere private.

In the Conversation Navigator

The same check-in appears in the Conversation Navigator as a block in the left panel, under your intent and bias self-reports. Two differences there: it is always the right-now framing, and no band is ever shown. The optional NA-10 is offered exactly as it is here. Your score appears on your participant card, the group distribution and NA-10 summary appear in the Stats view, and both go into the session's exports. Take it once at the start and again at the end — the shift is what the tool is there to show.
