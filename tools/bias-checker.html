How a session works
StartOpen the tool. A session ID is auto-generated and added to the URL.
InviteShare the URL. Anyone who opens it joins the same live session.
JoinEnter your name and click Join. Your card appears for everyone.
LeaveClose your tab. Your card is marked left and greys out, but everything you contributed stays in the session and in its export.
WithdrawRemove my input in the left panel deletes your card and your contributions for good. That is the only thing that erases them.
EndSession data persists until someone ends it. End session and delete all data on the Export tab clears every record, topic and model interaction. Export first.
Intents
📖 LearningYou are here to understand something you do not already know.
☕ SocializingThe relationship is the point — building trust, connection.
🏆 ConvincingYou have a position and want others to adopt it.
🔍 ExploringThe question is open — you do not know what you will find.
🎯 Producing somethingThe conversation has a deliverable: a decision, plan, document.
💨 VentingYou need to discharge energy. The content is secondary.
❤️ Being heardYou need to feel understood before you can engage with anything else.
📢 Sharing / TeachingYou have something to transfer and that transfer is the goal.
Select multiple intents if they are both active. Add custom intents in the field below the grid. Drag to reorder.
Biases to notice in yourself
Divided attentionPart of your attention is elsewhere.
Assuming success without checkingYou believe you have been understood — or understand — without verifying.
Doubling down when challengedPushback hardens your position rather than opening it.
Statements : Questions > 1You are asserting more than asking — driving rather than exploring.
Tone → Limiting ParticipationSomething about your manner makes it harder for others to contribute.
We need a modelReaching for a framework when specifics would serve better, or vice versa.
This is a self-report — biases you notice in yourself, not others. Update it during the conversation, not just at the start.

Positive Affect Score
What it isThe ten positive-affect words of the PANAS (Watson, Clark & Tellegen, 1988), each rated 1–5. Adding them gives a score from 10 to 50.
TimeframeAlways right now here — how you feel at this moment, not how your week has gone.
Take it againCheck in at the start and again at the end. The change is the number worth looking at: your card shows your own shift, and Stats shows how the whole room moved.
NA-10Optional second set of ten negative-affect words, opened from inside the check-in. Scored separately and never subtracted from the positive score — the two are close to independent, and a person can be high on both.
No bandsDeliberately no 1–4 activation band. That crosswalk is calibrated for a past-week reading and belongs to the health tools, not to a label on someone sitting in a meeting.
StandaloneThe same check-in with history, past-week framing, bands and export lives in Positive Affect Score ↗.
Follows the session's identity setting: names show in open mode, distribution and mean only when the session is anonymous. Your own score is always visible to you.
Built-in models
ModelHow to use it
Six QuestionsEdit the question labels and type answers. Click an answer box to mark it; counts aggregate across participants. Download SVG to archive.
CynefinClick domains (Complex, Complicated, Chaotic, Simple, Disorder) or boundary transitions to mark them as relevant to the conversation.
eVSM v2Click nodes (VSM functions) or edges (flows) to mark the current focus of discussion.
15 PsClick nodes and connections to mark relevance to the conversation topic.
Six Thinking HatsClick a hat to mark the dominant mode currently active in the conversation.
All click counts aggregate across participants in real time. Stats view shows the distribution. Upload graph JSON to add a custom diagram from the RCN Graph Tool.
Identity & consent
DefaultOpen. Names are visible to everyone — hover tooltips on stats bars and model nodes/edges show who clicked or holds that intent/bias.
Go anonymousCheck Make this session anonymous in the Identity section. As soon as one person checks the box, the whole session becomes anonymous.
Anonymous modeNo names appear in tooltips or model click data. The session stays anonymous as long as anyone has the box checked.
VotingThe count (X/Y) is visible; which specific participants voted which way is not shown.
If any participant checks the anonymous box, the session goes anonymous immediately for everyone. Uncheck to restore open mode.
