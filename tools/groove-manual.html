Groove Workspace — User Manual

Step-by-step guide to all features

v0.2 July 2026  ·  Introduction →
Getting Started

1. Interface overview

Open Groove at http://localhost:3001 (or the deployed URL your team uses). No installation, no account needed. The interface has two areas:

  • Left sidebar — identity, SCP page templates, imported pages, discussions, and privacy. Everything you open or create starts here.
  • Main panel — displays the currently selected page or discussion. Pages appear on the left half; discussions appear on the right half when both are open simultaneously.

The sidebar is divided into five sections: Identity, SCP Pages, FedWiki Pages, Discussions, and Privacy.

On a phone, the sidebar scrolls vertically. On a desktop, the page and discussion panels display side by side.

2. Create your identity

Your identity is how Groove knows who you are. It is stored in your browser — no account required, no password.

  1. Find the Identity section at the top of the sidebar.
  2. Type your name in the Your name field.
  3. Click Create (or press Enter).
  4. Your name and a short identity ID appear below the field. Your identity is now active.
Your identity persists in the browser across sessions. If you clear your browser storage, you will need to create a new identity.
About the cryptography: Behind the scenes, Groove generates two keypairs — one for signing messages (Ed25519) and one for encrypting workspace access grants (X25519). You do not need to manage these. They are what make it possible for you to grant others access to your workspace without giving them your keys.
In this version (v0.2), keypairs are generated on the server for prototype simplicity. Before real deployment with patient data, key generation will move to the browser. Do not store sensitive real patient data in this version.
Shared Care Plan

3. Opening SCP sections

The SCP Pages section of the sidebar lists all ten sections of the Shared Care Plan. Each is available as a template. Click any section name to open it:

  • If you already have a personal copy of that section, it opens immediately.
  • If you do not have one yet, a new page is created from the template and then opened.
SectionWhat it contains
About MeBackground, living situation, preferences, goals
Care TeamEveryone involved in your care
DiagnosesActive conditions with dates and managing providers
MedicationsCurrent medications, doses, prescribers, start dates
ReactionsAllergies and adverse reactions
Health LogTimestamped vitals, symptoms, and visit notes
Medical HistoryPast events, hospitalizations, surgeries
Next StepsAgreed actions and who is responsible for them
Advanced DirectivesWishes for care decisions you cannot make in the moment
Who's Accessed My PlanAccess log — who viewed what and when
Start a new Shared Care Plan by opening About Me first. Fill it in, then work through the other sections as you go.

4. Reading a page

Pages are displayed in the main panel. Each page is made up of items — paragraphs, structured SCP fields, images, or links to other pages.

Each item has a small toolbar that appears when you hover over it:

ButtonWhat it does
📌 pinPins this paragraph to your next discussion message as a reference. See Section 12.
✏️ editOpens the inline editor for this item. See Section 5.
+ add paragraphInserts a new text paragraph after this item.

At the top right of each page is an Export JSON link — click it to download the page as FedWiki JSON with its full journal. See Section 8.

5. Editing a page

Any paragraph in a page can be edited directly in Groove. Click ✏️ edit on any item to open the inline editor.

  1. Click ✏️ edit on the item you want to change.
  2. A split pane opens: the left side is a Markdown text editor, the right side shows a live preview.
  3. Make your changes in the left pane. The preview updates as you type.
  4. Click Save to commit the change. Click Cancel to discard.

Adding a new paragraph

  1. Hover over any item to reveal its toolbar.
  2. Click + add paragraph to insert a new empty paragraph after it.
  3. Type your text. Click Add to save, or Cancel to discard.
  4. To add a paragraph at the very end of the page, use the + add paragraph at end button at the bottom.
All edits create a new journal entry on the page. The page retains a complete history of who wrote what and when — this is how FedWiki tracks authorship.
All entries written by someone other than the person of record must be marked "Entry by someone else" in the discussion. Direct edits to SCP pages should only be made by the person of record or their CHW with explicit permission.
FedWiki Pages

6. Importing any FedWiki page

Groove can import any public FedWiki page, not just SCP pages. This is useful for bringing in reference materials, care resources, or pages from the community wiki.

  1. In the sidebar, find the FedWiki Pages section.
  2. Paste a FedWiki page URL into the FedWiki page URL field.
    Example: http://ward.dojo.fed.wiki/welcome-visitors
  3. Click Import (or press Enter).
  4. The page appears in the FedWiki Pages list and opens in the main panel.
FedWiki URLs follow the pattern http://[site]/[page-slug]. The slug is the page title with spaces replaced by hyphens and all lowercase.
Imported pages are stored in the Groove workspace database. They do not change when the original wiki page changes — re-import to get a fresh copy.

7. Multiple page tabs

You can have more than one page open at a time. Each page gets a tab at the top of the page panel.

  • Click a page in the sidebar to open it (or switch to it if already open).
  • Click a tab to switch between open pages.
  • Click the × on a tab to close that page. The next open page becomes active.
Open the Medications page and a Health Log discussion at the same time to review current medications while documenting today's visit.

8. Exporting a page

Any page in Groove can be exported back to FedWiki JSON format — with its full journal intact.

  1. Open the page you want to export.
  2. Click Export JSON at the top right of the page panel.
  3. Your browser downloads a .json file.
  4. To import into a FedWiki site, use the FedWiki factory or drop the JSON into the FedWiki import tool.
The exported file is standard FedWiki JSON. It round-trips without data loss — all items, item IDs, and journal entries are preserved.
Discussions

9. Creating a discussion

Discussions are named conversation threads that belong to the workspace. You can create as many as you need — one per care topic, per visit, or per question.

  1. In the sidebar, find the Discussions section.
  2. Type a name for the discussion in the Discussion name field.
    Examples: "2026-07-01 Home visit", "Medication question — Metformin", "Family check-in"
  3. Click Create (or press Enter).
  4. The discussion opens in the main panel with an empty message area and a compose box.
Name discussions with a date and topic. This makes it easy to find past conversations — especially for visit notes.

10. Posting messages

  1. Open a discussion from the sidebar.
  2. Click in the Write a message… text area at the bottom of the panel.
  3. Type your message.
  4. Click Send (or press Ctrl/Cmd+Enter).

Your message appears in the thread with your name and a timestamp. The discussion updates automatically every 5 seconds for other team members who have it open.

Messages support plain text. Keep them concise — use the page editing tools when you want to add something permanent to the health record itself.

11. Replying to a message

Groove supports threaded replies — you can reply directly to any message to keep related conversations together.

  1. Find the message you want to reply to.
  2. Click the Reply button below it.
  3. A banner appears above the compose area confirming who you are replying to.
  4. Type your reply and click Send.
  5. Your reply appears indented beneath the original message.

To cancel a reply before sending, click the ✕ on the reply banner.

12. Pinning a paragraph to a message

When you want to discuss a specific part of a page, you can pin that paragraph. A reference to it is automatically attached to your next message — so other team members can click the reference and jump directly to that paragraph.

  1. Open both a page and a discussion at the same time.
  2. In the page, hover over the paragraph you want to reference.
  3. Click the 📌 pin button on that item.
  4. The paragraph text appears in a yellow banner above the message compose area.
  5. Type your message and click Send.
  6. The sent message includes a linked reference to the pinned paragraph.

In the thread, other team members can click the reference to jump to that exact paragraph in the page — even if they have a different page open.

To unpin before sending, click the ✕ on the pin banner.

Use pin when you want to say "about this specific item" — for example, pinning a medication entry before asking a question about dosage changes.

13. Lifting a message into a page

Sometimes a message in a discussion belongs in the health record itself. The ↑ to page button copies a message's text into the currently open page as a new paragraph at the end.

  1. Make sure a page is open (it will receive the content).
  2. Find the message you want to lift.
  3. Click ↑ to page below it.
  4. The message text is added as a new paragraph at the end of the open page.
  5. The author name from the message is recorded as the paragraph author in the page journal.
After lifting, you may want to edit the new paragraph to clean up any conversational phrasing before it becomes part of the formal health record.
If the content was entered by a CHW or family member (not the person of record), mark it "Entry by someone else" in the original message first. The authorship travels with the content.

14. The "Entry by someone else" rule

This is one of the SCP's non-negotiable governance rules: every entry written by someone other than the person of record must be clearly marked.

In Groove, every message has an "Entry by someone else" checkbox in the compose area. When a CHW, family member, or anyone other than the person enters something on the person's behalf, they check this box before sending.

Messages marked this way are visually distinguished in the thread — a colored border and an "Entered by other" label appear on the message card.

Always check "Entry by someone else" when you are entering information on behalf of the person of record — even if they are present and verbally agreeing. The mark is about accurate authorship, not about consent.

When to use it

  • A CHW records vitals during a home visit
  • A family member describes a symptom they observed
  • A CHW transcribes what a person told them in conversation
  • Anyone enters information while the person is not using Groove themselves

When not to use it

  • You are the person of record entering your own information
  • You are the person of record describing your own experience
Privacy

15. Privacy summary

The Privacy section at the bottom of the sidebar gives the person of record a view of their identity and access log.

  1. Click View Privacy Summary in the sidebar.
  2. Your identity details (display name, identity ID) appear in the main panel.
  3. The access log shows who accessed your workspace and when.
The automatic access log (backend-driven, recording every page view) is not yet built. In this version, access log entries in the "Who's Accessed My Plan" SCP section are entered manually. Automatic logging will be completed before the Superior AZ pilot goes live with real patient data.
You must create an identity first to view the privacy summary. If you click the button without an identity, Groove will prompt you to create one.
Workflows

16. Home visit workflow

This is the primary CHW workflow — what to do during a home visit with the person on their care plan.

  1. Before the visit: Open Groove. Confirm your identity is set. Open the person's Medications and Health Log pages to review what's current.
  2. During the visit: Create a new discussion named with today's date and "Home visit" (e.g. 2026-07-01 Home visit).
  3. Taking vitals: Open the Health Log page. If the CHW is entering vitals on behalf of the person, check "Entry by someone else" on every message. Pin the relevant Health Log entry to each message if referencing a specific past reading.
  4. Medication check: Open the Medications page. Pin any medication entry that needs discussion and post a message about it in the visit discussion. If a medication has changed, use ✏️ edit to update the entry — check "Entry by someone else" if the person is not entering it themselves.
  5. Agreed actions: At the end of the visit, open the Next Steps page. Add new action items. If lifted from the discussion, use ↑ to page on the relevant message, then clean up the text.
  6. After the visit: The visit discussion serves as the visit record. The updated pages serve as the current health record. Nothing gets lost.
Keep the discussion open in one tab and the relevant SCP page open in another. The split-panel view lets you see both at once on a larger screen.

17. Care team coordination

When a family member or second team member needs to be involved, the discussion thread is the coordination point.

Asking the care team a question

  1. Create a discussion with a clear topic name (e.g. Question — transportation to appointment).
  2. Pin the relevant page entry if your question is about something specific.
  3. Post the question. Other team members see it the next time they open the discussion.
  4. They reply in the thread. The 5-second poll picks up new replies automatically.

Referencing the care plan in discussion

When a discussion message refers to something in the health record:

  1. Open the relevant SCP page.
  2. Pin the specific paragraph you are referring to (📌 pin).
  3. Type your message with the paragraph reference attached.
  4. Other team members can click the reference in the sent message to jump directly to that paragraph.

Keeping the record current

Discussion is where things are figured out. Pages are where the results go. After any significant discussion, review the relevant SCP pages and update them to reflect what was decided — using ↑ to page or editing directly. The discussion stays as the record of how you got there; the page stays as the current truth.

Access grants (formally adding team members to a workspace) are managed via the backend API in this version. The frontend for granting and revoking access is coming in a future release.
