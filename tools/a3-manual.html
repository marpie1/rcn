1 · Core ideas in one minute

This is Jimmerson's A3 (A3 Problem Solving for Healthcare), not the generic Toyota one. It is a one-page conversation, written by the person who does the work, about one problem seen once at the gemba. It is not a report and not a project plan.

The page reads left to right. The left side is what IS — the investigation. The right side is what SHOULD BE — the response. Three of her rules are built into the sheet rather than left to your good intentions:

1 · A problem is a gap. Her definition: the difference between what is supposed to happen and what actually happens. So Box 3 is three fields, not one. If you cannot fill the first two you do not have a problem yet — you have a complaint.

2 · Root cause is a broken process, never a person. The 5 Whys chain warns you when a why lands on someone forgetting, failing or not caring. That is the point at which most healthcare A3s quietly stop being useful.

3 · Countermeasures, not solutions. A countermeasure is a specific change to the work, tried and measured, kept or dropped. "Solution" implies you are done. Each one points back at the why it answers, so the right side cannot drift free of the left.

Current Condition and Target Condition are drawings in her method — small value stream sketches with storm bursts on them. A page whose Current Condition is a paragraph is not an A3.

2 · The interface

Two regions:

  • Toolbar (top) — documentation links, zoom, save, load, Markdown, print, Demo, and at the far right New — clear page.
  • The sheet — a fixed 11×17 landscape page. Everything is edited in place: click any field and type.

Zoom

  • − / + step the zoom; Fit returns to automatic fit-to-window, which is the default.
  • The sheet is a fixed 1632 × 1056 px — 17 in × 11 in at 96 dpi — so what you see at 100% is exactly what prints.
New — clear page sits alone at the far right, away from Save and Load, and asks before wiping the page. Clearing an hour of observation is not an undo away. It only asks if you have actually typed something — empty starter rows do not count as work.

3 · The header block

FieldWhat goes in it
ThemeThe problem in one line. Not the solution, not the department — the problem.
Written byThe person who does the work. Her method is emphatic that this is not written by a consultant or a manager.
ObservedWhat you watched — "one patient, one referral, followed end to end."
WhereAt the gemba. The actual place.
WhenA date and a time. One observation, not a month of them.
StatusDraft → Countermeasure in test → Effect confirmed, or Dropped. Dropped is a real outcome and is meant to be used.
Just do itHer escape hatch. A problem you can fix this morning does not get an A3. Ticking the box marks a page that should have been a five-minute fix.

4 · Box 1 — Background

One or two lines: who is affected, and why this stream was worth an hour of watching. Not a literature review.

5 · Box 2 — Current Condition

The largest box on the left, and it is mostly a drawing. Three ways to get a picture in:

  • Click the slot (or Add drawing…) and choose a file.
  • Drag and drop an image onto the slot.
  • Paste — click the slot once so it is the active one, then Cmd+V. Whichever slot you last clicked receives the paste.

It takes a photo of a pencil sketch, a screenshot, or an SVG/PNG exported from the Graph Tool. The image is stored inside the .a3.rcn.json file, so the A3 travels as one self-contained document.

Underneath is a one-line caption — what the drawing shows and the times you recorded — and then the storm burst list.

Hovering a filled slot reveals a Remove button in its corner, so you can swap a sketch without clearing the page.

6 · Storm bursts

A storm burst is a problem you saw while observing. In her method they are drawn as jagged stars on the current-state sketch; here they are also a list, so they are searchable and exportable.

  • + storm burst adds a row. Type what went wrong.
  • Click the burst glyph to mark the one this A3 is about. It fills amber and the text goes bold. Exactly one can be marked — choosing another releases the first.
  • The rest stay named as candidate A3s. Naming them is how they survive the walk back to the desk.
Every problem seen gets a burst, but only one gets this page. Trying to fix four things on one A3 is the most common way an A3 dies.

7 · Box 3 — Problem Statement

Three stacked fields, and the shape of the box is the teaching:

FieldWhat goes in it
What is supposed to happenThe standard, the policy, the promise made to the patient.
What actually happenedWhat you watched happen, once, with times.
The gap — this is the problemThe difference between the two, in a number if you have one. Highlighted amber, because this is the sentence the rest of the page answers.
If the first field is empty, stop. Not knowing what was supposed to happen is itself a finding, and usually a bigger one than the gap you came for.

8 · Box 4 — Root Cause & the 5 Whys

Five chained fields. Ask why until you reach something you can actually change, then write it in the green Root cause strip at the foot of the box — the conclusion of the left side, and the thing the right side must answer.

The blame check

As you type, each why is checked against a list of phrasings that name a person rather than a process — forgot, failed to, didn't check, careless, human error, needs more training, should have known, and others. A why that matches turns pink and a warning appears:

"Why 5 names a person, not a process. Ask once more: what about the work made that the easy thing to do?"

It is a prompt, not a lock — nothing stops you keeping it. But it is the single most useful thing on the sheet, because "the nurse forgot" is where an A3 stops being an improvement and starts being a disciplinary note.

9 · Box 5 — Target Condition

The mirror of Box 2: a drawing of the same stream redrawn — same notation, fewer steps, with the burst gone. Same three ways to add an image. Below it, say plainly what is different: who does what, when, instead of what.

10 · Box 6 — Countermeasures

Numbered rows. Each has the change itself and an Answers column — write why 3, why 5, the why this countermeasure addresses.

That small column is doing real work. It is what stops the right side of the page from becoming a wish list that has nothing to do with the analysis on the left.

+ row adds; the × at the end of a row removes it.

11 · Box 7 — Implementation Plan

What · Who · By when · Status.

  • Who is a person's name. Not a department, not "the team" — an owner who can say yes or no.
  • By when is a date, not "ongoing".
  • Status cycles through planned / doing / done / dropped.

12 · Box 8 — Test & Follow-up

How you will know it worked, and when you will look.

FieldWhat goes in it
MeasureOne measure, stated so anyone could collect it — "days from referral to first CHW contact."
BaselineWhat it is now. If you came from a value stream map this arrives pre-filled with the observed lead time.
TargetWhat good looks like.
Check onA date. An A3 with no check date is an opinion.
ActualFilled in after.

Below is Effect confirmation: what actually changed, whether it was kept or dropped, and what you learned.

Counts and days belong on a run chart. Hand the measure to the SPC tool rather than judging a change from two numbers — the tool says so on the sheet, and it means it.

13 · Making it fit on one page

The constraint is hers, not the tool's: an A3 is one page. The sheet is a fixed size and does not grow.

Paper clips silently, so every field measures itself after each keystroke. When your text runs past its box the field outlines in red and a badge appears reading "too long for the page". Nothing is lost while you are on screen — the field scrolls — but it would be lost on paper, so shorten it.

This is the sheet asking you to say it shorter, which is most of what the A3 format is for.

14 · Saving, loading, autosave

  • Save .a3.rcn.json downloads the whole A3 as one file, drawings included, named from the theme.
  • Load… opens a dialog — drop a file, choose one, or paste the JSON. It replaces the page you are looking at.
  • Autosave runs continuously into browser local storage, so a refresh or a closed tab does not lose work. It is per-browser and per-origin — it is not a backup. Save the file.

The file format is the RCN family idiom, matching the other tools/*.rcn.json files: {version, kind:"rcn.a3", …}.

15 · Printing to 11×17

Print / PDF prints the sheet at 1:1 on a single 17 in × 11 in landscape page. The toolbar, the italic hint text, the overflow badges and the remove buttons all drop away; only the A3 prints.

Do one test print on your own printer before a meeting. The page requests size: 17in 11in; a printer that does not honour it will scale the sheet, and a printer with no tabloid tray will fit it to letter, which is legible but small.

16 · Markdown export

Markdown writes the whole A3 as a .md file — every box in order, the whys as a list, the plan as a real table. Written unwrapped, one line per paragraph, so it ports straight into FedWiki with no reflow step.

The drawings are noted as present but not embedded; the markdown is for the text of the A3, and the sheet or its PDF is for the picture.

17 · Coming from a value stream map

In her method the A3 does not start on a blank page. You map the stream, find the storm bursts, and A3 the worst one.

  1. Draw the current state in VSM mode in the Graph Tool.
  2. Place a storm burst at every problem you saw, and mark one as the A3's problem.
  3. Press VSM→A3. It writes a .a3.rcn.json.
  4. Open this tool, press Load…, and drop that file in.

The page arrives with the theme taken from the burst, the full burst list with the chosen one marked, the observed times in the caption and in "what actually happened", and the lead time as your follow-up baseline. The halves only a person can fill — what was supposed to happen, the gap, the five whys — are deliberately left empty.

Export the current-state map as SVG or PNG from the Graph Tool and drop it into the Current Condition slot. Then the A3 carries the picture the numbers came from.

18 · Working patterns

Write it at the gemba, not at the desk

Fill the left side where the work happens, while you can still check what you saw. The right side can wait; the observation cannot be reconstructed.

Print it and mark it up

It is a conversation tool. Her A3s are pencil, and they get argued over. Print it, take it to the people who do the work, let them correct it, and bring the corrections back to the file.

Keep the candidates

The bursts you did not choose are the backlog. When this A3 closes, the next one is already named on the page.

One page, one problem, one owner

If the plan needs more than a handful of rows, you have more than one A3.
