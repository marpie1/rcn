# Kitchen Table Notes Sheet

Print one of these if you would rather keep your notes on paper. One line per step is enough, and more is better. Written at the time, not reconstructed afterwards.

For each step, two things: how it felt, and what you understood or accomplished there.

- Step 1 — thinking of an area of present concern and care. Felt: … Understood: …
- Step 2 — listing at least fifteen things. Felt: … Understood: …
- Step 3 — choosing about eight and enclosing them. Felt: … Understood: …
- Step 4 — connecting the ones that matter to you. Felt: … Understood: …
- Step 5 — labelling the lines. Felt: … Understood: …
- Step 6 — adding arrowheads. Felt: … Understood: …
- Step 7 — splitting the double arrows. Felt: … Understood: …
- Step 8 — making every label an action verb. Felt: … Understood: …
- Step 9 — reading the graph out loud. Felt: … Understood: …
- Step 10 — going back through and recalling each step. Felt: … Understood: …
- Step 11 — what have you learned, what could you do with it, was it interesting. Felt: … Understood: …

Two more, at the end, worth recording honestly.

- Which step was hardest, and what exactly was hard about it?
- At any point, did you want someone to tell you whether you were doing it right? Where?

Keep this. If you do the exercise again in a year on a different topic, the two sheets side by side will tell you something neither one can.
